maternal Rh-negative status + negative antibody screen (unsensitized) + partner Rh-positive.

An unsensitized Rh-negative patient carrying a potentially Rh-positive fetus is at risk for alloimmunization \[ACOG PB 181\].

Scene 3 — Prioritize Hypotheses (cloze/dropdown)

\[Rh(D) immune globulin\]

\[maternal anti-D antibody formation\]

that could harm a future Rh-positive fetus.

Anti-D prophylaxis prevents sensitization; it is prevention, not treatment \[ACOG PB 181\].

Scene 4 — Generate Solutions (extended multiple response)

Select the 3 correct elements of the anti-D plan across this pregnancy.

✅ Administer Rh(D) immune globulin routinely at about 28 weeks

✅ Administer anti-D after any sensitizing event (bleeding, amniocentesis)

✅ Administer anti-D within 72 hours postpartum if the newborn is Rh-positive

❌ Give anti-D only if she develops symptoms

❌ Withhold anti-D because the antibody screen is negative

❌ Give the rubella vaccine in place of anti-D

A negative screen is exactly why she still needs prophylaxis; it means she is not yet sensitized \[ACOG PB 181\].

Scene 5 — Take Actions (drag-and-drop ordering)

At 28 weeks the antibody screen is repeated and remains negative; anti-D is ordered.

Place the steps in order.

Verify Rh-negative status and negative antibody screen

Confirm the order and educate the patient on purpose and timing

Administer Rh(D) immune globulin as ordered

Document administration and schedule postpartum reassessment

Verification precedes administration; documentation closes the loop for the postpartum dose \[ACOG PB 181\].

Prophylaxis was completed correctly and on time — antenatal dose at 28 weeks plus postpartum dose within 72 hours for an Rh-positive infant.

Timely completion of both doses is the marker of successful prevention; the same negative screen with correct timing indicates no sensitization occurred \[ACOG PB 181\].
