## 02  NUTRITIONAL NEEDS & WEIGHT GAIN

Adequate nutrition is essential for fetal growth, maternal health, and positive birth outcomes. Inadequate or excessive intake may contribute to preterm birth, congenital anomalies, miscarriage, GDM, SGA newborns, hypertensive disorders, and neurocognitive issues. **Prenatal vitamins** are prescribed routinely; iron (27 mg/day) and folic acid (400–800 mcg/day) must be supplemented because diet alone is insufficient to meet increased pregnancy demands.

### Dietary Reference Intakes (DRIs) for Pregnancy

| Nutrient | Nonpregnant | Pregnant | Lactating |
|---|---|---|---|
| Calories | 1,800–2,200 | 2,200–2,400 (2nd tri) / 2,400–2,600 (3rd tri) | 2,200–2,400 |
| Protein | 56 g | 71 g | 71 g |
| Folate | 400 mcg | 600 mcg | 500 mcg |
| Iron | 8 mg | 27 mg | 9–10 mg |
| Calcium | 1,000 mg | 1,000–1,300 mg | 1,000–1,300 mg |
| Vitamin D | 600 IU | 600 IU | 600 IU |
| Vitamin C | 90 mg | 80–85 mg | 115–120 mg |
| Iodine | 220 mcg | 290 mcg | 290 mcg |

### Recommended Weight Gain by Prepregnancy BMI

| Prepregnancy BMI | Total Weight Gain | 1st Tri Gain | 2nd/3rd Tri Rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Underweight (<18.5) | 28–40 lb | ≥5 lb | Slightly >1 lb/week |
| Normal (18.5–24.9) | 25–35 lb | 3.5–5 lb | ~1 lb/week |
| Overweight (25–29.9) | 15–25 lb | ~2 lb | 0.5–0.6 lb/week |
| Obese (≥30) | 11–20 lb | ~2 lb | 0.5–0.6 lb/week |

### Where Does the Weight Go?

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Infant birth weight | 7–8 lb |
| Blood volume increase | 3–4 lb |
| Uterus | 2 lb |
| Breast tissue increase | 1–3 lb |
| Placenta | 1.5 lb |
| Maternal fluid volume | 2–3 lb |
| Maternal fat stores | 6–8 lb |
| Amniotic fluid | 2 lb |
| Total | ~24.5–31.5 lb |

### Food Safety During Pregnancy

- Avoid shark, swordfish, king mackerel, marlin, orange roughy, bigeye tuna, and tilefish (high mercury).
- Eat 8–12 oz/week of low-mercury fish: shrimp, salmon, tilapia, cod, haddock, pollock, catfish (limit albacore tuna to 6 oz/week).
- Avoid raw/unpasteurized milk, soft cheeses (feta, Brie, Camembert, blue-veined), refrigerated smoked seafood, raw sprouts.
- Avoid deli meats and hot dogs unless reheated until steaming hot. Do not eat refrigerated pâté or meat spreads.
- Limit caffeine to <200–300 mg/day. No alcohol — no safe amount or trimester. Limit artificial sweeteners.
- Wash all fruits and vegetables. Avoid store-made salads (ham, chicken, egg, tuna, seafood). Refrigerate perishables within 2 h.

### Special Nutritional Considerations

- **Pica:** compulsive ingestion of nonfood substances (dirt/clay, ice/pagophagia, laundry starch/amylophagia). Associated with iron-deficiency anemia, parasitic infections, constipation, poor glucose control. Assess non-judgmentally; stress harmful effects of nonfood items.
- **Lactose intolerance:** substitute lactose-free dairy, calcium-enriched OJ or soy milk. More common in African, Asian, South American descent.
- **Vegetarian/vegan:** ensure adequate protein (soy, beans, lentils, nuts), iron (meat alternatives + vitamin C), calcium (fortified OJ, kale, broccoli, tofu), vitamin B12 (fortified soy/cereals + supplement), vitamin D.

> **■ TAKE NOTE! Dieting during pregnancy is NEVER recommended, even for patients with overweight/obesity.**
>  
> Severe caloric restriction is associated with decreased birth weight. Focus on nutrient density. Additional 340 cal/day in 2nd tri; 450 cal/day in 3rd tri.

## 03  COMMON DISCOMFORTS OF PREGNANCY BY TRIMESTER

Most pregnant patients experience discomforts. Always rule out pathologic causes before attributing to normal pregnancy.

| Discomfort | Timing | Cause | Nursing Interventions / Teaching |
|---|---|---|---|
| Urinary frequency | 1st & 3rd tri | Uterine compression of bladder (1st); fetal head engagement (3rd) | Rule out UTI/GDM. Decrease fluids 2–3 h before bedtime. Limit caffeine. Pelvic floor exercises. Report dysuria. |
| Fatigue | 1st & 3rd tri | ↑ metabolic demands, ↑ progesterone, sleep disturbances | Rule out anemia. Schedule daily nap/rest periods. Use pillows for left-lateral sleep. Regular moderate exercise. |
| Nausea & vomiting (≥90%) | 1st tri (peaks 8–12 wk; resolves by 16–18 wk) | ↑ hCG, estrogen, progesterone; altered GI motility; possible B6/zinc deficiency | Eat crackers/toast before arising. Small frequent meals (5–6/day). Avoid empty stomach. Separate fluids from solids. Avoid triggers (strong odors, greasy/spicy foods). Ginger (1 g/day divided). Vitamin B6 10–25 mg q6–8h. Doxylamine/pyridoxine (Diclegis) FDA-approved. Severe = hyperemesis gravidarum. |
| Breast tenderness | 1st tri | ↑ estrogen & progesterone → fat layer thickening, ↑ milk ducts/glands | Well-fitting supportive bra with wide straps (wear even while sleeping). Reassure — normal adaptation. |
| Constipation (≤40%) | 1st & 3rd tri | Progesterone → ↓ peristalsis; ↑ water absorption; iron/calcium supplements; uterine compression | ↑ fiber (fruits/vegetables/whole grains), ↑ fluids (6–8 glasses water/day), daily exercise. Warm fluids upon rising. Bulk-forming laxative (Metamucil) if needed. Reduce cheese, refined carbs. |
| Nasal stuffiness, bleeding gums, epistaxis | Throughout | Estrogen → edema of nasal/oral mucous membranes | Hydrate. Cool-mist humidifier. Blow nose gently, one nostril at a time. Avoid decongestant sprays. Soft toothbrush; warm saline mouthwashes. For epistaxis: lean forward, pinch nostrils 10–15 min, ice to bridge. |
| Backache (≤60%) | 2nd & 3rd tri | Shifted center of gravity; ↑ relaxin → joint laxity; ligament stretching | Pelvic tilt/rock exercises. Proper posture. Low-heeled supportive shoes. Avoid heavy lifting/bending from waist. Heat or ice. Acetaminophen (avoid NSAIDs in 3rd tri). Sleep left lateral with pillow between knees. |
| Leg cramps (≤50%) | 2nd & 3rd tri | Uterine pressure on pelvic nerves/vessels; possible Ca/Mg deficiency | Dorsiflex foot during cramp. Warm moist towel. Avoid pointing toes/stretching legs in bed. Calf-stretching exercises before bed. Elevate legs. Calcium/magnesium-rich foods. Avoid standing in one position or crossing legs. |
| Heartburn / pyrosis | 2nd & 3rd tri | Progesterone → ↓ LES tone + delayed gastric emptying; uterine pressure on stomach | Small frequent meals. Avoid spicy/greasy foods, citrus, chocolate, caffeine. Do NOT lie down for 3 h after eating. Elevate HOB 30°. Antacids sparingly. Stop smoking. |
| Varicosities & hemorrhoids | 2nd & 3rd tri | ↑ Venous pressure from gravid uterus; progesterone → vessel wall relaxation; constipation | Walk daily. Elevate legs. Avoid constrictive clothing, prolonged standing, crossing legs. Support stockings. Prevent constipation (fiber, fluids, exercise). Sitz baths and witch hazel compresses for hemorrhoids. |
| Leukorrhea | Throughout (↑ progressively) | Estrogen → ↑ vascularity & hypertrophy of cervical glands/vaginal cells | Perineal hygiene with mild soap/water. Cotton underwear; avoid pantyhose/tight nylon. Avoid douching and tampons. Report foul odor, itching, or change in color → rule out infection. |
| Braxton Hicks contractions | 2nd & 3rd tri (↑ noticeable last month) | Normal intermittent uterine contractions; irregular, painless; do NOT cause cervical change | Reassure — normal. Change position or mild exercise to reduce sensation. Increase fluids. Differentiate from preterm labor: regular, painful contractions + cervical change = call provider. |

> **■ TAKE NOTE! Nausea/vomiting affects ~90% — but hyperemesis gravidarum is NOT normal morning sickness.** >5% weight loss + dehydration + ketonuria = hyperemesis. Requires NPO → IV fluids → antiemetics.

## 04  NORMAL DISCOMFORT vs WARNING SIGN

Critical nursing skill: differentiating expected findings from danger signs requiring immediate evaluation.

| Symptom | Normal Discomfort | Warning Sign — Report Immediately |
|---|---|---|
| Nausea & vomiting | Morning sickness; improves by 16–18 wk; can eat/drink between episodes | Persistent, severe vomiting; >5% weight loss; ketonuria; unable to keep fluids down → **hyperemesis gravidarum** |
| Fatigue | Improves with rest; normal Hgb/Hct | Extreme fatigue with pallor, tachycardia, ↓ Hgb/Hct → **anemia** |
| Urinary frequency / leakage | No dysuria; no fever; urine dip negative | Dysuria, urgency, frequency, flank pain, fever → **UTI/pyelonephritis** |
| Vaginal discharge (leukorrhea) | Whitish-yellow, non-irritating, no odor | Foul odor, itching, burning, change in color/consistency → **infection** (candidiasis, BV, STI) |
| Backache | Improves with rest, position change, heat; no uterine activity | Rhythmic low back pain with regular uterine contractions → **preterm labor** |
| Abdominal / round ligament pain | Sharp, brief groin pain with position change; resolves quickly | Constant severe abdominal pain; vaginal bleeding; shoulder pain + dizziness → **ectopic pregnancy rupture** |
| Contractions (Braxton Hicks) | Irregular, painless; relieved by walking/position change/hydration; NO cervical change | Regular contractions q10 min or less; painful; low dull backache; pelvic pressure; cervical dilation → **preterm labor** |
| Edema | Dependent edema (lower extremities); improves with elevation; no ↑ BP | Sudden weight gain; periorbital/facial/hand edema; ↑ BP; proteinuria; headache → **preeclampsia** |
| Fetal movement | Consistent pattern; 10 movements in ≤2 h | Absent or significantly decreased movement >12 h → **fetal hypoxia/distress** |
| Headache | Mild, occasional; relieved by rest, hydration, acetaminophen | Severe, persistent; visual changes (blurred vision, scotomata); epigastric pain; ↑ BP → **preeclampsia** |
| Calf discomfort | Bilateral, symmetrical; improves with elevation | Unilateral calf pain, warmth, redness, swelling; pain with dorsiflexion (Homan sign) → **DVT** |

🔴 **Teach every pregnant patient:** If warning signs occur, contact provider immediately. Do NOT wait for the next scheduled appointment.

## 05  MEDICATIONS, SUBSTANCES & IMMUNIZATIONS IN PREGNANCY

In 2015, the FDA replaced the old A-B-C-D-X letter categories with the **Pregnancy and Lactation Labeling Rule (PLLR)**. The new system provides narrative subsections: Pregnancy (including labor/delivery), Lactation, and Females and Males of Reproductive Potential. This replaces the simplistic letter categories with detailed risk summaries to assist clinical decision-making. For RNSG 1523, the old PLLA categories are still commonly tested: **P**regnancy risk, **L**actation safety, **L**abor/delivery effects, and reproductive potential **A**dverse effects.

### Common Medications — Safe Use & Nursing Implications

| Medication | Use in Pregnancy | Nursing Implications / Key Points |
|---|---|---|
| **Prenatal vitamins** (iron 27 mg + folic acid 400–800 mcg) | Routinely prescribed for all pregnant patients | Iron: take on empty stomach with vitamin C for absorption; may cause constipation/black stools. Folic acid: prevents neural tube defects; start preconception. Higher dose (4 mg) if prior NTD or on antiepileptics. |
| **Acetaminophen (Tylenol)** | Preferred analgesic/antipyretic during pregnancy | Use lowest effective dose for shortest duration. Avoid exceeding 3,000–4,000 mg/day. Recent studies suggest possible association with ADHD/autism with prolonged use — use judiciously. |
| **Ibuprofen / NSAIDs** (Advil, Motrin, naproxen) | Generally avoided, especially in 3rd trimester | 3rd trimester: may cause premature closure of ductus arteriosus, oligohydramnios, prolonged labor. 1st/2nd trimester: use only if clearly needed and prescribed. Acetaminophen is safer alternative. |
| **Calcium carbonate** (Tums) | Safe for heartburn/indigestion during pregnancy | Also provides calcium supplementation. Avoid excessive use (> recommended dose). May interfere with iron absorption — space doses. |
| **Famotidine (Pepcid)** / H2 blockers | Considered safe; preferred over PPIs when antacids insufficient | Used for refractory heartburn/GERD unrelieved by lifestyle modifications and antacids. Category B equivalent. |
| **Doxylamine + pyridoxine** (Diclegis) | FDA-approved specifically for nausea/vomiting of pregnancy | First-line pharmacotherapy for morning sickness. 2 tablets at bedtime; may add 1 AM + 1 mid-afternoon. Contains 10 mg doxylamine + 10 mg pyridoxine per tablet. Safe profile. |
| **Vitamin B6 (pyridoxine)** | First-line OTC for morning sickness | 10–25 mg PO q6–8h. May be sufficient alone for mild N/V. Often combined with doxylamine for moderate symptoms. |
| **Ginger** (up to 1 g/day divided doses) | Complementary therapy for N/V of pregnancy | 250 mg capsules QID. Increases GI tone and peristalsis. Generally recognized as safe. Counsel on appropriate dosing. |
| **Diphenhydramine (Benadryl)** | Second-line antiemetic; also for allergic reactions, sleep | Generally considered safe. May cause drowsiness — counsel on safety. Use lowest effective dose. |
| **Ondansetron (Zofran)** | Second/third-line antiemetic for refractory N/V | Used when first-line therapies fail. Some studies suggest small increased risk of cleft palate and cardiac defects in 1st trimester — balance risks vs benefits of dehydration/malnutrition from severe N/V. |
| **Iron supplements** (ferrous sulfate 325 mg) | Treatment of iron-deficiency anemia in pregnancy | Absorption enhanced by vitamin C; inhibited by calcium, antacids, tea/coffee. Take between meals. Side effects: constipation, dark stools, GI upset. ↑ fiber/fluids to manage constipation. |
| **Influenza vaccine** (inactivated) | Recommended for all pregnant patients during flu season (any trimester) | Inactivated vaccine only — NOT live attenuated (FluMist). Pregnant patients at higher risk for severe influenza complications. Protects newborn via passive antibody transfer. |
| **Tdap vaccine** | Recommended 27–36 weeks EACH pregnancy | Regardless of prior Tdap history. Provides passive pertussis immunity to newborn before infant vaccination begins at 2 months. Optimal timing: 27–36 weeks to maximize maternal antibody response and placental transfer. |
| **COVID-19 vaccine** | Recommended; may be given at any trimester | Pregnancy increases risk of severe COVID-19. Vaccination reduces maternal ICU admission, preterm birth, and stillbirth. Passive antibody transfer to newborn via placenta and breast milk. |

### Substances to Avoid — Effects & Nursing Implications

| Substance | Effect on Pregnancy / Fetus | Nursing Implications |
|---|---|---|
| **Alcohol** | Fetal alcohol spectrum disorder (FASD); neurodevelopmental deficits; growth restriction; characteristic facies. Leading preventable cause of intellectual disability. | **NO safe amount or trimester.** Screen all patients. Use AUDIT/T-ACE screening tools. Refer to treatment programs. |
| **Tobacco / Nicotine** | Vasoconstriction → ↓ placental perfusion → IUGR, low birth weight, preterm birth, stillbirth, placental abruption, placenta previa. Newborn: nicotine withdrawal, ↑ SIDS risk. | **Strongly encourage cessation.** Offer nicotine replacement therapy (NRT) if needed — safer than continued smoking. Counseling + pharmacotherapy more effective than either alone. Avoid e-cigarettes/vaping — not proven safe. |
| **Cannabis** | ↑ Risk of low birth weight, preterm birth, NICU admission. Neurodevelopmental effects on offspring. THC crosses placenta; detectable in breast milk. | **Discourage use.** No established safe dose. Screen non-judgmentally. Refer to substance use treatment if needed. |
| **Opioids** (prescription & illicit) | Neonatal abstinence syndrome (NAS/NOWS); low birth weight; preterm birth; placental abruption; fetal demise. Maternal: overdose, infection, withdrawal. | Medication-assisted treatment (MAT) with methadone or buprenorphine is standard of care — safer than continued opioid misuse. DO NOT recommend abrupt withdrawal. Coordinate with addiction medicine. |
| **Cocaine / Stimulants** | Placental abruption, preterm birth, IUGR, fetal demise, congenital anomalies, neonatal neurobehavioral abnormalities. | Screen and refer. Abruption may present with vaginal bleeding, uterine tenderness, fetal distress. No approved pharmacotherapy for stimulant use disorder. |
| **Caffeine** | ↑ Risk of miscarriage and low birth weight at high doses (>300 mg/day). Crosses placenta — fetus cannot metabolize efficiently. | Limit to <200–300 mg/day (~1–2 cups of coffee). Counsel on hidden sources: tea, soda, energy drinks, chocolate. |

> **■ TAKE NOTE! Tdap is recommended during EVERY pregnancy at 27–36 weeks.** Goal: passive pertussis antibody transfer to newborn. Flu vaccine (inactivated) in any trimester. AVOID live vaccines (MMR, varicella). RhoGAM at 28 wk if Rh−.

## 06  BREAST CARE & SELF-CARE DURING PREGNANCY

### Breast Care

- **Wear a supportive, well-fitting bra** throughout pregnancy and postpartum. Wide straps; cotton preferred. May wear while sleeping for comfort.
- **Wash breasts with water only** — avoid soap on nipples (dries and removes natural lubricants). Pat dry gently.
- **Colostrum** (yellowish, creamy pre-milk fluid) may be expressible by the 3rd trimester — normal. Do not aggressively express; may stimulate oxytocin release.
- **Flat or inverted nipples:** assess at 36 weeks. If present, teach nipple rolling or breast shell use to prepare for latch. Consultation with lactation specialist recommended.
- **Montgomery tubercles** (sebaceous glands on areola) become prominent — provide natural lubrication. Do not squeeze or apply creams/alcohol.
- **Breast self-awareness:** continue monthly breast self-exam during pregnancy. Report any new lumps, dimpling, or nipple discharge (especially bloody).

### Self-Care & Health Promotion

- **Exercise:** 150 min/week of moderate-intensity activity (brisk walking, swimming, stationary cycling). Avoid contact sports, scuba diving, activities with fall risk. Pelvic floor exercises daily.
- **Sleep & rest:** Left lateral position with pillows between knees and under abdomen. Avoid supine position after 20 weeks. Nap daily if fatigued.
- **Hygiene:** Daily showering with mild soap. Avoid douching and tampons. Cotton underwear. Perineal care front-to-back.
- **Clothing:** Loose-fitting, non-constrictive. Low-heeled supportive shoes. Avoid tight belts/waistbands, constrictive stockings.
- **Sexual activity:** Generally safe in uncomplicated pregnancy unless contraindicated (bleeding, preterm cervical dilation, placenta previa). Libido may fluctuate by trimester. Alternative non-coital intimacy encouraged if intercourse uncomfortable.
- **Employment:** Most occupations safe during pregnancy. Avoid heavy lifting, prolonged standing, exposure to toxic chemicals/radiation. Discuss workplace accommodations if needed.
- **Travel:** Safest time = 2nd trimester (14–28 wk). Wear seatbelt (lap belt below abdomen, shoulder belt between breasts). Frequent position changes/stretching on long trips. Avoid travel after 36 weeks.
- **Immunizations:** Tdap (27–36 wk each pregnancy), inactivated influenza (any trimester), COVID-19 (any trimester). AVOID live vaccines: MMR, varicella, intranasal influenza (FluMist). RhoGAM if Rh-negative at 28 weeks.

> **■ TAKE NOTE! 150 min/week of moderate exercise** reduces GDM, preeclampsia, excessive weight gain. Pelvic floor exercises strengthen perineal muscles. Avoid supine position after 20 wk. Stop if dizziness, chest pain, contractions, bleeding.

## 07  PSYCHOSOCIAL ADAPTATIONS DURING PREGNANCY

~15–20% of pregnant/postpartum people experience perinatal mood and anxiety disorders. **Screen every patient at every prenatal visit.**

### Common Emotional Responses

| Emotional Response | Timing | Nursing Consideration |
|---|---|---|
| **Ambivalence** | 1st trimester; evolves to acceptance by 2nd tri when fetal movement felt | Universal and normal. Conflicting feelings. Assure patient this is common when preparing for a major life change. Influenced by relationship quality, career goals, family situation. |
| **Introversion (self-focus)** | 1st & 3rd trimesters | Normal psychological adaptation. Patient becomes preoccupied with self and fetus. May appear passive/withdrawn. Educate partner/family that this behavior ensures a safe pregnancy outcome. |
| **Acceptance** | 2nd trimester (↑ with fetal movement, ultrasound, hearing FHT) | Tangible signs of separate being. Patient verbalizes positive feelings about pregnancy. Support and validate feelings at each prenatal visit. |
| **Mood swings (emotional lability)** | Throughout pregnancy | Extreme emotional shifts common. Educate patient and partner that this is normal. Screen for depression/anxiety if symptoms are persistent, severe, or interfere with functioning. |
| **Body image changes** | Throughout; varies greatly between patients | Some feel beautiful; others distressed. Discuss expected bodily changes openly. Provide thorough explanations; normalize concerns about weight gain and appearance changes. |
| **Perinatal mood & anxiety disorders** | Any trimester; ~15–20% of pregnant/postpartum people | **RED FLAGS:** persistent sadness, anhedonia, excessive anxiety, sleep/appetite disturbance beyond pregnancy norms, thoughts of self-harm or harming infant. SCREEN at every prenatal visit (Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale). Refer to mental health services immediately if positive. DO NOT dismiss as "hormones." |

### Rubin's Maternal Tasks

| Task | 1st Trimester | 2nd Trimester | 3rd Trimester |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ensuring safe passage** | Focus on self, not on fetus | Develops attachment of great value to fetus | Concern for self AND fetus as a unit |
| **Seeking acceptance of infant by others** | Acceptance of pregnancy by self and others | Family relates to fetus as member | Unconditional acceptance without rejection |
| **Seeking acceptance of self in maternal role ("binding in")** | Accepts idea of pregnancy, not of infant | Quickening → acknowledges fetus as separate entity | Longs to hold infant; tired of being pregnant |
| **Learning to give of oneself** | Identifies what must be given up for new role | Identifies with infant; learns to delay own desires | Questions ability to become a good parent |

> **■ TAKE NOTE! Perinatal depression is the most common complication of pregnancy.** Screen with Edinburgh Scale. Untreated depression associated with poor prenatal care, substance use, low birth weight, preterm birth. Sertraline is preferred SSRI in pregnancy/breastfeeding. Do NOT abruptly discontinue antidepressants.

Choose the **single best** answer.

**Q1.** A patient at 28 weeks’ gestation with a prepregnancy BMI of 22 reports consuming a balanced diet. She asks the nurse how much total weight she should gain. Which is the correct recommendation?

- A. 11–20 lb
- B. 15–25 lb
- C. 25–35 lb
- D. 28–40 lb

**Q2.** A patient at 10 weeks’ gestation reports severe nausea and vomiting. Which statement by the patient indicates a need for further evaluation for hyperemesis gravidarum?

- A. "I feel nauseous mostly in the morning, but it improves by lunchtime."
- B. "I have lost 8 lb since my last visit 3 weeks ago and cannot keep any fluids down."
- C. "Eating dry crackers before getting out of bed helps a little."
- D. "Ginger tea seems to reduce my nausea somewhat."

**Q3.** The nurse is teaching a pregnant patient about danger signs. Which patient statement indicates correct understanding?

- A. "If I have a headache, I will take ibuprofen and rest at home."
- B. "A sudden gush of fluid from my vagina could mean my water broke — I should call the provider."
- C. "It is normal if I stop feeling the baby move for a whole day in the third trimester."
- D. "Swelling in my feet means I am developing preeclampsia and I should go to the ER immediately."

**Q4.** A patient at 36 weeks’ gestation asks the nurse about taking ibuprofen for back pain. What is the best response?

- A. "Ibuprofen is safe at any time during pregnancy; you may take it as needed."
- B. "Ibuprofen should be avoided in the third trimester because it may cause premature closure of the ductus arteriosus. Acetaminophen is a safer option."
- C. "You should not take any pain medication during pregnancy."
- D. "Only prescription-strength ibuprofen is safe — do not use OTC products."

**Q5.** A patient at 32 weeks’ gestation reports eating laundry starch several times per week. The nurse suspects pica. What is the priority nursing action?

- A. Dismiss it as a cultural practice and document in the chart.
- B. Assess for iron-deficiency anemia, discuss harms of consuming nonfood items, and refer to nutritionist.
- C. Report the patient to child protective services for endangering the fetus.
- D. Tell the patient to switch to eating ice instead of laundry starch.

**Q6.** Which patient should receive the Tdap vaccine during pregnancy?

- A. Only patients who have never received Tdap before
- B. All pregnant patients, during 27–36 weeks of EACH pregnancy
- C. Only patients who plan to breastfeed
- D. Patients during the first trimester only

## 09  ANSWER KEY & RATIONALE

| Q | Ans | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 | **C** | Normal prepregnancy BMI (18.5–24.9) → 25–35 lb total. A = obese. B = overweight. D = underweight. |
| Q2 | **B** | Hyperemesis gravidarum criteria: >5% weight loss, dehydration, ketonuria, inability to tolerate oral intake. B describes significant weight loss (8 lb ≈ >5% of body weight) and inability to keep fluids down. A, C, D describe typical self-limiting morning sickness. |
| Q3 | **B** | Sudden gush/leakage of fluid = possible PROM — requires immediate evaluation. A: ibuprofen avoided in 3rd tri; severe headache + visual changes = preeclampsia warning. C: decreased/absent fetal movement >12 h is a danger sign — NEVER normal. D: dependent pedal edema is normal; periorbital/facial/hand edema + ↑ BP = preeclampsia. |
| Q4 | **B** | NSAIDs (ibuprofen) in 3rd trimester → premature closure of ductus arteriosus, oligohydramnios, prolonged labor. Acetaminophen is the preferred analgesic. A is false — ibuprofen is NOT safe in 3rd tri. C is overly restrictive. D is false — OTC and Rx carry same risks. |
| Q5 | **B** | Priority: assess for iron-deficiency anemia (common with pica), educate non-judgmentally on harmful effects of nonfood substances (laundry starch replaces iron-rich foods, causes constipation, poor glucose control, excessive weight gain), and refer to nutritionist. A dismisses a real health risk. C is punitive and inappropriate — pica is a medical condition, not child abuse. D simply substitutes one form of pica for another without addressing the underlying cause. |
| Q6 | **C** | ACIP/ACOG recommend Tdap during 27–36 weeks of EACH pregnancy regardless of prior vaccination history, to provide passive pertussis immunity to the newborn before infant vaccination begins at 2 months. A, B, D are all incorrect. |
