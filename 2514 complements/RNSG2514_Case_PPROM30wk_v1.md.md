Gush of clear fluid; nitrazine turns blue; ferning seen on microscopy; temperature 37.0 °C; no uterine tenderness; no contractions; FHR 145 with moderate variability.

Highlight the cues confirming prelabor rupture of membranes before term.

blue nitrazine + ferning + gush of fluid at 30 weeks (preterm) = PPROM.

Positive nitrazine and ferning confirm ROM; before 37 weeks it is PPROM \[ACOG PB 217\].

Scene 4 — Generate Solutions (extended multiple response)

Select the 3 correct management elements.

✅ Administer antenatal corticosteroids for fetal lung maturity

✅ Give latency antibiotics per protocol

Steroids, latency antibiotics, and neuroprotection are standard; digital exams are minimized to reduce infection \[ACOG PB 217; CO 713\].

Scene 5 — Take Actions (SATA)

Which nursing actions reduce infection risk and support the fetus? Select all that apply.

✅ Minimize digital vaginal examinations

✅ Monitor temperature, pulse, and FHR for infection signs

✅ Administer steroids and antibiotics as ordered

✅ Educate on reporting fever, foul discharge, or contractions

❌ Encourage tub bathing

Fewer digital exams after ROM means fewer intraamniotic infections \[ACOG PB 217\].

Scene 6 — Evaluate Outcomes (trend)

Day 1 afebrile, no contractions, reassuring FHR; Day 3 afebrile, steroid course complete; Day 5 develops temperature 38.4 °C, maternal HR 108, fetal baseline 170, uterine tenderness.

Which interpretation should drive the next decision?

Infection (chorioamnionitis) is now developing; expectant management ends and delivery with antibiotics is indicated.

The trend shift to fever, tachycardia, and tenderness overrides latency — the fetus is safer delivered \[ACOG PB 217\].

Debrief — three decision points students most often miss

Doing digital exams "to check progress."

After ROM, each exam adds infection risk; minimize them.

Treating latency as the fixed goal.

Latency ends the moment infection appears — reassess continuously.

Forgetting neuroprotection.

Magnesium sulfate before 32 weeks reduces cerebral palsy and is easy to overlook.
