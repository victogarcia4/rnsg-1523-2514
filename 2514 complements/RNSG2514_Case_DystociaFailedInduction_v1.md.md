Scene 1 — Recognize Cues (highlight)

Bishop score 3 (unfavorable cervix). After cervical ripening and oxytocin, the monitor shows 7 contractions in 10 minutes averaged over 30 minutes; the FHR shows recurrent late decelerations with minimal variability.

Highlight the cues of tachysystole with a concerning tracing.

Excess uterine activity with a Category II tracing signals over-stimulation compromising the fetus \[ACOG-SMFM OCC No. 1\].

Scene 4 — Generate Solutions (extended multiple response)

Select the 3 correct interventions.

✅ Stop or decrease the oxytocin

✅ Reposition the patient (e.g., left lateral) and give an IV fluid bolus

✅ Reassess the tracing and notify the provider

❌ Apply routine maternal oxygen despite a normal maternal SpO₂

❌ Increase the oxytocin to "push through"

❌ Take no action while variability is minimal

Reduce stimulation and resuscitate; routine oxygen is not indicated when maternal saturation is normal \[ACOG 2025 intrapartum FHR CPG\].

Scene 5 — Take Actions (drag-and-drop ordering)

Order the nurse's actions.

Decrease or stop the oxytocin

Reposition and give an IV fluid bolus

Reassess contractions and the FHR tracing

Notify the provider and prepare for possible cesarean if no improvement

Document the event and interventions

Correct the stimulation, resuscitate, reassess, then escalate if needed \[ACOG-SMFM OCC No. 1\].

Scene 6 — Evaluate Outcomes (trend)

#### Trend after intervention:

hour 0 — 7 contractions/10 min, late decels, minimal variability; hour 1 — 5 contractions/10 min, occasional late decel, variability returning; hour 2 — 4 contractions/10 min, no decels, moderate variability. Cervix remains 4 cm with no further change over 4 hours despite adequate contractions.

Which interpretation should drive the next decision?

Intrauterine resuscitation resolved the tracing, but the labor now meets criteria for arrest (no cervical change with adequate contractions), so cesarean birth is anticipated.

Evaluate on two fronts: the tracing improved (resuscitation worked), while arrest of dilation — not "failure to progress" — now guides delivery planning \[ACOG-SMFM OCC No. 1\].
