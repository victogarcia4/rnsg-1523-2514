6 scenes, one per NCJMM operation.

Tanya, 26, G1 P0, at 30 weeks, calls the prenatal triage line.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Scene 1 — Recognize Cues (highlight)

Reports a severe headache "that won't go away," some blurred vision, mild nasal congestion, mild ankle swelling by evening, and new puffiness of her face and hands since this morning.

Highlight the reports that are warning signs.

severe persistent headache, blurred vision, and sudden facial/hand swelling.

These cluster toward preeclampsia and require urgent evaluation; nasal congestion and evening ankle swelling are benign \[ACOG PB 222; Lowdermilk\].

The neurologic-visual-edema cluster points to a hypertensive emergency risk and drives immediate triage \[ACOG PB 222\].

Scene 4 — Generate Solutions (extended multiple response)

Select the 3 correct triage actions.

✅ Advise her to come in now (or go to OB triage) for BP and evaluation

✅ Ask about additional symptoms (RUQ pain, decreased fetal movement)

✅ Stay on the line and arrange safe transport

❌ Tell her to take acetaminophen and call back tomorrow

❌ Reassure her that headaches are always normal

❌ Advise bed rest at home without evaluation

Warning-sign clusters require prompt in-person assessment, not home management \[ACOG PB 222\].

Scene 5 — Take Actions (drag-and-drop ordering)

Order the nurse's triage actions.

Recognize the warning-sign cluster and its urgency

Direct the patient to immediate evaluation and arrange transport

Communicate the report to the provider/OB triage

Document the call, advice given, and disposition

Recognition drives disposition; hand-off and documentation follow \[ACOG PB 222\].

Scene 6 — Evaluate Outcomes (trend)

#### On arrival and over 2 hours:

BP 168/110 → after evaluation and treatment 150/96 → 138/88; labs show platelets 140,000, mild proteinuria; magnesium started for seizure prophylaxis; symptoms improving.

Which interpretation best evaluates the triage decision?

Urgent triage led to timely diagnosis and treatment; the BP and symptom trend improved — escalating the call was the correct action.

Evaluate the triage by whether early recognition changed the trajectory; here it did \[ACOG PB 222\].

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Debrief — three decision points students most often miss

Averaging danger with benign symptoms.

One or two warning signs in a list still trigger urgent evaluation, regardless of the benign items.

Home-managing a warning-sign cluster.

Acetaminophen-and-call-back is unsafe for possible preeclampsia.

Missing the sudden-vs-gradual edema distinction.

Evening ankle swelling is normal; sudden facial/hand swelling is not.
