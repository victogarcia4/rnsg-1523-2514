OB history & dating

Gravida, Term, Preterm, Abortions, Living — five-element pregnancy-history shorthand.

Gravida (G)

Total number of pregnancies including the current one, regardless of outcome.

Number of births after 20 weeks, whether live or stillborn.

Term (GTPAL T)

Births at or beyond 37 0/7 weeks of gestation.

Preterm (GTPAL P)

Births from 20 0/7 through 36 6/7 weeks of gestation.

Abortions (GTPAL A)

Pregnancy losses before 20 weeks, spontaneous or induced.

Living (GTPAL L)

Number of children currently alive, regardless of current age.

Last Menstrual Period; the first day of it anchors Naegele's rule.

Estimated Date of Birth; modern preferred term for the due date.

Estimated Date of Confinement; traditional term for the due date.

Gestational Age, expressed in completed weeks plus days (e.g., 36 0/7).

Advanced Maternal Age; 35 years or older at delivery.

Naegele's rule

LMP minus 3 months, plus 7 days, plus 1 year; assumes a 28-day cycle.

Estimated Date of Delivery; synonym for the due date, used interchangeably with EDB.

0/7 notation

Weeks plus completed days; '36 0/7' means exactly 36 weeks and 0 days.

Signs of pregnancy

hCG / β-hCG

Human Chorionic Gonadotropin; basis of pregnancy tests, doubles early in viable pregnancy.

Fetal Heart Rate; a positive (diagnostic) sign of pregnancy.

Transvaginal Ultrasound; visualizes early gestation and confirms a positive sign.

Fetal Heart Tones; auscultated heartbeat, a positive sign of pregnancy.

First maternal perception of fetal movement, around 16–20 weeks; a presumptive sign.

Chadwick sign

Bluish discoloration of cervix and vagina from increased blood flow; a probable sign.

Goodell sign

Softening of the cervix in early pregnancy; a probable sign.

Screening & diagnostics

Nuchal Translucency; aneuploidy screen with serum markers at 10 3/7–13 6/7 weeks.

Cell-free DNA; screens maternal plasma for trisomy 21/18/13. Screening, not diagnostic.

NIPT / NIPS

Non-Invasive Prenatal Testing; trade term for cfDNA screening.

Maternal Serum Alpha-Fetoprotein; elevated suggests neural tube defect, low suggests aneuploidy.

Quad screen

AFP, hCG, unconjugated estriol, inhibin A; drawn at 15–22 weeks.

Neural Tube Defect such as spina bifida or anencephaly; folic acid reduces risk.

Chorionic Villus Sampling; diagnostic karyotype at 10–13 weeks, earlier than amniocentesis.

Amniocentesis

Diagnostic karyotype from amniotic fluid at 15 weeks or later.

Glucose Challenge Test; 50 g non-fasting 1-hour screen for gestational diabetes.

Oral Glucose Tolerance Test; 100 g fasting 3-hour diagnostic for gestational diabetes.

Group B Streptococcus; vaginal-rectal culture at 36 0/7–37 6/7 weeks.

Intrapartum Antibiotic Prophylaxis; penicillin G first-line for GBS-positive labor.

Rhesus D antigen; Rh-negative means the D antigen is absent.

RhIG / RhoGAM

Rh(D) immune globulin; give at 28 weeks and within 72 h postpartum if infant Rh-positive.

Non-Stress Test; reactive means adequate accelerations, reassuring fetal status.

Biophysical Profile; five components scored 0–10 for fetal well-being.

MCA Doppler

Middle Cerebral Artery Doppler; surveils for fetal anemia.

Pregnancy-Associated Plasma Protein A; first-trimester serum aneuploidy marker.

Alpha-Fetoprotein; elevated maternal serum suggests neural tube or abdominal wall defect.

Chromosomal Microarray Analysis; detects submicroscopic deletions and duplications.

Gestational Diabetes Mellitus; glucose intolerance first recognized in pregnancy.

Early-Onset Disease; neonatal group B strep sepsis within the first 7 days.

Hemoglobin / Hematocrit; screen for anemia, expect dilutional fall in pregnancy.

Hepatitis B surface antigen; positive triggers neonatal vaccine plus immune globulin.

Contraction Stress Test; assesses fetal tolerance of contraction-induced hypoxic stress.

CF carrier screen

Cystic Fibrosis carrier screening; offered to all pregnant patients preconception or prenatally.

Contraception

U.S. Medical Eligibility Criteria for contraceptive use; CDC categories 1–4 by condition.

Combined Oral Contraceptive with estrogen and progestin; estrogen raises VTE risk.

Progestin-Only Pill; requires strict same-time daily dosing.

Depot medroxyprogesterone acetate; injection every 13 weeks, may reduce bone density.

Long-Acting Reversible Contraception; IUDs and implant, most effective reversible methods.

Levonorgestrel-releasing intrauterine device; thins endometrium, lightens menses.

Copper IUD; non-hormonal, also effective as emergency contraception up to 5 days.

Ulipristal acetate; oral emergency contraceptive effective up to 120 hours.

Lactational Amenorrhea Method; relies on exclusive breastfeeding, under 6 months, no menses.

Venous Thromboembolism; key risk of estrogen-containing contraceptives.

U.S. Selected Practice Recommendations; how to use a chosen contraceptive method correctly.

Emergency Contraception; copper IUD most effective, then ulipristal, then levonorgestrel.

Fertility Awareness-Based Methods; track cycle to avoid intercourse in the fertile window.

Deep Vein Thrombosis / Pulmonary Embolism; the feared VTE outcomes of estrogen use.

Typical-use effectiveness

Failure rate reflecting real-world inconsistent use; the number to counsel patients on.

Menopause/Gyn

Follicle-Stimulating and Luteinizing Hormones; FSH rises in menopause.

Vasomotor Symptoms; hot flashes and night sweats of menopause.

Genitourinary Syndrome of Menopause; vaginal dryness, dyspareunia, urinary symptoms.

Menopausal Hormone Therapy; most effective treatment for vasomotor symptoms.

Primary Ovarian Insufficiency; ovarian failure before age 40.

Abnormal Uterine Bleeding; always evaluate postmenopausal bleeding for cancer.

Primary dysmenorrhea

Painful menses without pelvic pathology; prostaglandin-mediated, responds to NSAIDs.

Secondary dysmenorrhea

Painful menses caused by pelvic pathology such as endometriosis or fibroids.

Gonadotropin-Releasing Hormone; hypothalamic driver of the FSH/LH cycle.

HT risk window

Menopausal hormone therapy safest when started under age 60 or within 10 years of menopause.

Bone Mineral Density measured by dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry; screens for osteoporosis.

Postmenopausal bleeding

Any bleeding after menopause; requires endometrial evaluation to exclude cancer.

Nutrition/Meds/Physiology

Body Mass Index (kg/m²); sets recommended gestational weight-gain range.

Gestational Weight Gain; target set by prepregnancy BMI per IOM 2009.

0.4 mg daily preconception reduces neural tube defects; higher with prior NTD.

Pregnancy and Lactation Labeling Rule; narrative labeling that replaced letter categories.

Legacy A/B/C/D/X

Old FDA pregnancy letter categories, removed by the PLLR; still seen in textbooks.

Angiotensin-converting-enzyme inhibitors / receptor blockers; fetotoxic, contraindicated in pregnancy.

USDA supplemental nutrition program for Women, Infants, and Children.

Rupture of Membranes; document time, color, odor, and fetal heart rate.

Intimate Partner Violence; screen privately, pregnancy can escalate risk.

Physiologic anemia

Plasma volume rises faster than red cells, lowering hematocrit; a dilutional normal.

IOM / NASEM

Institute of Medicine, now National Academy of Medicine; source of weight-gain guidance.

Dietary Folate Equivalents; unit accounting for better absorption of synthetic folic acid.

Iron in pregnancy

Increased demand for fetal stores and red-cell mass; supplement per anemia screening.

Listeria precautions

Avoid unpasteurized dairy, deli meats, and cold smoked fish to prevent fetal infection.

Fish/mercury guidance

Limit high-mercury fish; mercury is a neurodevelopmental teratogen.

PROM (1523)

Prelabor rupture of membranes at or after 37 weeks; formerly called 'premature'.

Supine hypotensive syndrome

Uterine compression of the vena cava when supine; relieve with left-lateral tilt.

Any agent causing structural or functional fetal harm; risk greatest during organogenesis.

EHR trending

Electronic Health Record graphing of BP, weight, and fundal height across visits.

Discrimination

Distinguish presumptive from probable signs

Presumptive = subjective (amenorrhea, nausea); probable = examiner-observed (Goodell, positive test).

Distinguish probable from positive signs

Probable can have other causes; positive proves a fetus: FHR, fetal movement felt by examiner, ultrasound.

Distinguish screening from diagnostic tests

Screening estimates risk (cfDNA, quad); diagnostic confirms with karyotype (CVS, amniocentesis).

Distinguish GCT from OGTT

GCT is the 1-hour non-fasting screen; OGTT is the 3-hour fasting diagnostic.

Distinguish EDC/EDB from GA

EDB is the due date; GA is how far along now, in weeks and days.

Distinguish COC from POP eligibility

Estrogen (COC) is contraindicated with high VTE or migraine-with-aura risk; POP is safer there.

Distinguish primary from secondary dysmenorrhea

Primary has no pelvic pathology; secondary is caused by identifiable disease.

Distinguish PLLR from letter categories

PLLR is narrative risk labeling; A/B/C/D/X are the removed legacy letters.

Distinguish CVS from amniocentesis timing

CVS at 10–13 weeks gives an earlier karyotype; amniocentesis is done at 15+ weeks.

Distinguish presumptive discomfort from warning sign

Nausea and urinary frequency are normal; vaginal bleeding and severe headache are warning signs.

Distinguish LARC from short-acting methods

LARC (IUD, implant) needs no daily adherence; pills and rings depend on consistent use.

Distinguish reactive from non-reactive NST

Reactive shows adequate accelerations and is reassuring; non-reactive needs further testing.

Distinguish VMS from GSM

Vasomotor symptoms are hot flashes/night sweats; GSM is vaginal and urinary tissue change.

Estrogen contraceptive danger signs: Abdominal pain, Chest pain, Headache, Eye problems, Severe leg pain.

IUD danger signs: Period late, Abdominal pain, Chills/fever, Trouble/foul discharge, Heavy bleeding.
