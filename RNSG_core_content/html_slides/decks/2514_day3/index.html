Open speaker notes

"Dystocia is abnormal progression of labor, accounting for about fifty percent of unplanned cesareans in nulliparous patients. The five Ps are Power, the expulsive forces; Passenger, the fetus; Passage, the pelvis and birth canal; Position, the maternal position; and Psyche, maternal stress. Fear and anxiety release catecholamines that reduce uterine contractility."

HYPERTONIC vs HYPOTONIC

HYPERTONIC vs HYPOTONIC

HYPERTONIC vs HYPOTONIC quick recall table
FeatureHypertonicHypotonic
PhaseLatentActive
ContractionsUncoordinatedWeak
PainIncreasedLess
ManagementRest + sedationAmniotomy + oxytocin
Open speaker notes

"Hypertonic dysfunction occurs in the latent phase with uncoordinated, painful contractions and high resting tone. Management is therapeutic rest and sedation. Hypotonic dysfunction occurs in the active phase with coordinated but weak contractions. Management is amniotomy plus oxytocin augmentation, after ruling out cephalopelvic disproportion. The memory hook is: hypertonic in latent phase means rest, hypotonic in active phase means augment."

SHOULDER DYSTOCIA

SHOULDER DYSTOCIA

Head delivers, shoulders wedged behind pubic bone. Obstetric emergency.

Response: McRoberts maneuver + suprapubic pressure (NOT fundal).

Avoid: fundal pressure, strong traction. Assess newborn for Erb palsy, fractures.

Open speaker notes

"Shoulder dystocia occurs when the fetal head delivers but the shoulders wedge behind the pubic bone. It is an obstetric emergency because the chest cannot expand and the cord is compressed. The response is the McRoberts maneuver, hyperflexing the thighs to the abdomen, plus suprapubic pressure. Avoid fundal pressure and strong traction, which increase brachial plexus injury. After birth, assess the newborn for Erb palsy and fractures."

TAKE NOTE: NO FUNDAL PRESSURE

TAKE NOTE! SHOULDER DYSTOCIA

FUNDAL pressure is contraindicated. It does not free the shoulder — it increases injury risk.

Use SUPRAPUBIC pressure with McRoberts maneuver.

Open speaker notes

"Take note. Fundal pressure is contraindicated in shoulder dystocia. It does not free the shoulder and increases the risk of injury. Use suprapubic pressure with the McRoberts maneuver. This is a frequently tested point."

BISHOP SCORE

BISHOP SCORE — CERVICAL READINESS

Range 0–13. >8 = favorable (likely succeed). <5 = unfavorable → ripen first.

Evaluates: dilation, effacement, station, consistency, position.

Open speaker notes

"The Bishop score evaluates cervical readiness for induction, ranging from zero to thirteen. A score above eight is favorable and likely to succeed. A score below five is unfavorable, so the cervix must be ripened before induction. The score evaluates dilation, effacement, station, consistency, and position."

OXYTOCIN NURSING

OXYTOCIN (PITOCIN) NURSING

10 units in 1,000 mL isotonic IV pump. Titrate to contractions every 2–3 min.

Monitor: contractions, FHR, VS, I&O, cervical progress.

DISCONTINUE: tetany >90 sec, tachysystole, Category II/III.

Open speaker notes

"Oxytocin is given as ten units in 1,000 milliliters of isotonic solution via IV pump. Titrate to contractions every two to three minutes lasting forty to sixty seconds. Monitor contractions, fetal heart rate continuously, vital signs, intake and output, and cervical progress. Discontinue for tetanic contractions over ninety seconds, tachysystole, or a Category II or III tracing."

TAKE NOTE: RESTING TONE

TAKE NOTE! UTERINE RESTING TONE

Resting tone above 20 mm Hg compromises uteroplacental perfusion → fetal hypoxia.

Continuous FHR monitoring mandatory.

Tachysystole: >5 ctx/10 min over 30 min. → Stop/decrease oxytocin.

Open speaker notes

"Take note. A resting uterine tone above 20 millimeters of mercury compromises uteroplacental perfusion and causes fetal hypoxia. Continuous fetal monitoring is mandatory. Tachysystole is more than five contractions in ten minutes over thirty minutes. The action is to stop or decrease the oxytocin — never increase it."

CORD PROLAPSE

UMBILICAL CORD PROLAPSE

Cord slips ahead of presenting part. Obstetric emergency — occlusion = death.

Risk: malpresentation, prematurity, hydramnios, PROM, unengaged part.

First sign: sudden bradycardia or variable decels after ROM.

Open speaker notes

"Umbilical cord prolapse occurs when the cord slips ahead of the presenting part through the cervix. Complete occlusion means fetal death if not relieved. Risk factors include malpresentation, prematurity, hydramnios, PROM, and an unengaged presenting part. The first sign is sudden fetal bradycardia or variable decelerations after membranes rupture."

CORD PROLAPSE RESPONSE

CORD PROLAPSE — EMERGENCY RESPONSE

  1. Call for help — stay with patient.
  2. Push presenting part off cord.
  3. Position: knee-chest / Trendelenburg.
  4. NEVER replace cord into uterus.
  5. Oxygen, continuous FHR.
  6. Prepare emergency cesarean.

Hand stays in place until delivery.

Open speaker notes

"The emergency response. Call for help immediately but do not leave the patient. Insert a sterile gloved hand and push the presenting part off the cord, holding it there. Position the patient in knee-chest or steep Trendelenburg. Never attempt to replace the cord into the uterus. Administer oxygen and continuous fetal monitoring. Prepare for emergency cesarean if the cervix is not fully dilated. The hand that finds the prolapse stays in place until delivery."

UTERINE RUPTURE (TOLAC)

UTERINE RUPTURE — TOLAC

Triad during TOLAC: → Sudden tearing pain between contractions → Loss of fetal station (part rises) → Prolonged bradycardia (FHR 60s–70s)

Rupture until proven otherwise.

Open speaker notes

"Uterine rupture is a tearing of the uterine wall during a trial of labor after cesarean. The triad is sudden severe tearing abdominal pain between contractions, loss of fetal station as the presenting part rises, and prolonged fetal bradycardia in the sixties or seventies. This triad means uterine rupture until proven otherwise."

RUPTURE EMERGENCY ACTIONS

RUPTURE — EMERGENCY ACTIONS

  1. STOP oxytocin immediately.
  2. Call for help — notify surgical team.
  3. Prepare emergency cesarean (no imaging).
  4. Large-bore IV, prepare blood.
  5. Position for perfusion, O2 if hypoxic.

Dual emergency: fetal hypoxia + maternal bleed.

Open speaker notes

"The emergency actions. Stop the oxytocin infusion immediately — stimulation worsens rupture. Call for help and notify the surgical and neonatal teams. Prepare for emergency cesarean without delaying for imaging. Establish large-bore IV access and prepare blood products. Position for optimal perfusion and give oxygen if maternal hypoxia is present. Rupture is a dual emergency — fetal hypoxia and maternal bleeding."

KEY TERMS: LABOR

KEY TERMINOLOGY — LABOR

Dystocia = abnormal labor progression CPD = Cephalopelvic disproportion Tachysystole = >5 ctx/10 min MVU = Montevideo units (≥200 adequate) Bishop score = cervical favorability Labor arrest = replaces "failure to progress" TOLAC/VBAC = trial of labor after cesarean

Open speaker notes

"Key terminology. Dystocia is abnormal labor progression. CPD is cephalopelvic disproportion. Tachysystole is more than five contractions in ten minutes. Montevideo units measure contraction strength, with about 200 considered adequate. The Bishop score assesses cervical favorability. Labor arrest replaces the outdated term failure to progress. TOLAC is trial of labor after cesarean, and VBAC is vaginal birth after cesarean."

NGN CASE: FAILED INDUCTION

NGN CASE — DYSTOCIA & FAILED INDUCTION

Bianca, 28, G1 P0, 41 weeks, induction. Bishop score 3 (unfavorable).

After oxytocin: 7 ctx/10 min, late decels, minimal variability.

→ Tachysystole + Category II.

Open speaker notes

"An NGN case. Bianca is 28, G1 P0, at 41 weeks, admitted for induction with a Bishop score of three, meaning an unfavorable cervix. After cervical ripening and oxytocin, the monitor shows seven contractions in ten minutes with recurrent late decelerations and minimal variability. This is oxytocin-induced tachysystole with a Category II tracing."

CASE: INDUCTION ACTIONS

CASE — PRIORITY ACTIONS

  1. Stop/decrease oxytocin.
  2. Reposition (left lateral) + fluids.
  3. Reassess tracing.
  4. Notify provider.

NO routine oxygen if SpO2 normal (ACOG 2025 reversal).

Open speaker notes

"The priority actions. Stop or decrease the oxytocin. Reposition the patient to the left lateral and give an IV fluid bolus. Reassess the tracing and notify the provider. Do not apply routine oxygen if maternal saturation is normal — this is the 2025 ACOG reversal. Do not increase oxytocin to push through."

EXIT TICKET — DAY 3

QUICK RECALL — PART 1

Dystocia 5 Ps: Powers, Passenger, Passage, Position, Psyche.

Hypertonic: latent, uncoordinated, REST. Hypotonic: active, weak, oxytocin.

Shoulder dystocia: McRoberts + suprapubic (NOT fundal).

Bishop <5 = ripen first; >8 = favorable.

Open speaker notes

"Quick recall. The five Ps of dystocia are Powers, Passenger, Passage, Position, and Psyche. Hypertonic dysfunction is in the latent phase, uncoordinated, and treated with rest. Hypotonic is in the active phase, weak, and treated with oxytocin. Shoulder dystocia is McRoberts plus suprapubic pressure, never fundal. A Bishop score below five requires ripening first, and above eight is favorable."

QUICK RECALL (2/2)

QUICK RECALL — PART 2

Oxytocin: continuous FHR, d/c if tachysystole or Cat II/III.

Tachysystole: >5 ctx/10 min → reduce oxytocin.

Cord prolapse: push part off cord, knee-chest/Trendelenburg, hand stays.

Uterine rupture: tearing pain + loss of station + bradycardia → cesarean.

Open speaker notes

"Oxytocin requires continuous fetal monitoring, and is discontinued for tachysystole or Category II or III. Tachysystole means more than five contractions in ten minutes, so reduce oxytocin. Cord prolapse is managed by pushing the part off the cord, positioning knee-chest or Trendelenburg, and keeping the hand in place. Uterine rupture presents with tearing pain, loss of station, and bradycardia, requiring emergency cesarean."
