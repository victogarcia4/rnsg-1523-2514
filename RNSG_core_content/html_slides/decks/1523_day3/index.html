NUTRITIONAL NEEDS OVERVIEW

NUTRITIONAL NEEDS

Essential for fetal growth and outcomes. Inadequate intake → preterm, anomalies, miscarriage, GDM, SGA.

Iron 27 mg + folic acid 400–800 mcg MUST be supplemented — diet insufficient.

Open speaker notes

"Adequate nutrition is essential for fetal growth, maternal health, and positive birth outcomes. Inadequate or excessive intake contributes to preterm birth, congenital anomalies, miscarriage, gestational diabetes, and small-for-gestational-age newborns. Critically, iron at 27 milligrams per day and folic acid at 400 to 800 micrograms per day must be supplemented because diet alone cannot meet the increased demands of pregnancy."

FOOD SAFETY

FOOD SAFETY DURING PREGNANCY

Avoid high-mercury fish: shark, swordfish, king mackerel, tilefish.

Listeria: no unpasteurized dairy, deli meats (unless steaming), soft cheeses.

No alcohol — no safe amount or trimester. Limit caffeine <200–300 mg/day.

Open speaker notes

"Food safety during pregnancy is as important as adequate intake. Avoid high-mercury fish including shark, swordfish, king mackerel, and tilefish. To prevent listeria, avoid unpasteurized dairy, deli meats unless heated until steaming, and soft cheeses. There is no safe amount of alcohol and no safe trimester. Limit caffeine to less than 200 to 300 milligrams per day."

SPECIAL CONSIDERATIONS

SPECIAL NUTRITIONAL CONSIDERATIONS

Pica: ingestion of nonfood items (dirt, ice, starch). Assess non-judgmentally. Screen for iron-deficiency anemia.

Lactose intolerance: lactose-free dairy, calcium-enriched substitutes.

Vegetarian/vegan: ensure protein, iron, calcium, B12, vitamin D.

Open speaker notes

"Special nutritional considerations. Pica is the compulsive ingestion of nonfood substances such as dirt, ice, or laundry starch. Assess the patient non-judgmentally and screen for iron-deficiency anemia, which is commonly associated. For lactose intolerance, substitute lactose-free dairy and calcium-enriched alternatives. For vegetarian and vegan patients, ensure adequate protein, iron, calcium, vitamin B12, and vitamin D through fortified foods and supplements."

TAKE NOTE: DIETING

TAKE NOTE! NO DIETING

Dieting is NEVER recommended in pregnancy, even with overweight or obesity.

Severe caloric restriction → decreased birth weight.

Focus on nutrient density. +340 cal/day in 2nd tri. +450 cal/day in 3rd tri.

Open speaker notes

"Take note of this fundamental rule. Dieting is never recommended during pregnancy, even for patients with overweight or obesity. Severe caloric restriction is associated with decreased birth weight. Focus on nutrient density instead. The additional requirement is 340 calories per day in the second trimester and 450 calories per day in the third trimester."

NAUSEA MANAGEMENT

NAUSEA & VOMITING MANAGEMENT

Crackers/toast before arising. Small frequent meals (5–6/day). Separate fluids from solids. Avoid triggers (odors, greasy).

Ginger 1 g/day; B6 10–25 mg q6–8h. Diclégis (doxylamine + pyridoxine). Severe = hyperemesis gravidarum.

Open speaker notes

"Nausea and vomiting affects over ninety percent of pregnant patients. Start with crackers or toast before arising. Eat small frequent meals, five to six per day. Separate fluids from solids. Avoid triggers like strong odors and greasy foods. Ginger at one gram per day and vitamin B6 at 10 to 25 milligrams every six to eight hours are first-line. Diclégis, combining doxylamine and pyridoxine, is FDA-approved. Severe vomiting that does not respond is hyperemesis gravidarum."

TAKE NOTE: HYPEREMESIS

TAKE NOTE! HYPEREMESIS

N/V affects ~90% — but hyperemesis is NOT normal morning sickness.

>5% weight loss + dehydration + ketonuria = hyperemesis.

Treatment: NPO → IV fluids → antiemetics.

Open speaker notes

"Take note. Nausea and vomiting affects ninety percent of patients, but hyperemesis gravidarum is not normal morning sickness. The diagnostic criteria are greater than five percent weight loss, dehydration, and ketonuria. Treatment is NPO to rest the gut, then IV fluids, then antiemetics. Do not force oral intake — it prolongs vomiting."

NORMAL vs WARNING SIGNS

NORMAL DISCOMFORT vs WARNING SIGN

Critical nursing skill: differentiating expected from danger.

Severity, suddenness, and location separate danger from normal.

Teach every patient to report warning signs immediately.

Open speaker notes

"Now the critical distinction between normal discomfort and warning signs. Severity, suddenness, and location separate danger from normal. For example, mild dependent edema is normal, but sudden facial and hand edema with hypertension is preeclampsia. Teach every pregnant patient to report warning signs immediately and not to wait for the next scheduled appointment."

EDEMA: NORMAL vs DANGER

EDEMA — KEY DISTINCTION

NORMAL: dependent lower-extremity edema, improves with elevation, no ↑ BP.

DANGER: sudden facial/hand edema, sudden weight gain, ↑ BP, proteinuria → PREECLAMPSIA.

Sudden facial/hand swelling = warning. Evening ankle swelling = normal.

Open speaker notes

"The edema distinction deserves special attention because it is frequently tested. Normal edema is dependent, in the lower extremities, improves with elevation, and has no blood pressure elevation. Danger signs are sudden facial and hand edema, sudden weight gain, elevated blood pressure, and proteinuria — this cluster points to preeclampsia. Remember: sudden facial and hand swelling is a warning sign. Evening ankle swelling is normal."

MEDICATIONS: PLLR

MEDICATIONS — PLLR SYSTEM

2015: FDA replaced A-B-C-D-X letters with Pregnancy & Lactation Labeling Rule.

PLLR = narrative risk labeling. Old letters still in older texts — teach both.

ACE inhibitors/ARBs = fetotoxic. Isotretinoin = contraindicated.

Open speaker notes

"Now medications in pregnancy. In 2015, the FDA replaced the old A-B-C-D-X letter categories with the Pregnancy and Lactation Labeling Rule, or PLLR. This new system provides narrative risk information in three subsections: pregnancy, lactation, and reproductive potential. The old letters still appear in older textbooks, so teach both. Under PLLR, ACE inhibitors and ARBs are fetotoxic. Isotretinoin is contraindicated."

NSAIDs IN PREGNANCY

NSAIDs (IBUPROFEN) — CONTRAINDICATED 3RD TRI

3rd trimester: → Premature ductus arteriosus closure → Oligohydramnios → Prolonged labor

Use acetaminophen instead. 1st/2nd tri: only if clearly needed.

Open speaker notes

"NSAIDs such as ibuprofen are contraindicated in the third trimester. They cause premature closure of the ductus arteriosus, oligohydramnios, and prolonged labor. In the first and second trimesters, use only if clearly needed. Acetaminophen is the safer alternative for pain. This is a frequently tested point."

IMMUNIZATIONS IN PREGNANCY

SAFE (recommended): → Tdap 27–36 weeks EVERY pregnancy → Inactivated influenza (any trimester) → COVID-19 (any trimester)

TAKE NOTE: Tdap

TAKE NOTE! Tdap EVERY PREGNANCY

Tdap at 27–36 weeks EACH pregnancy, regardless of prior history.

Purpose: passive pertussis immunity to newborn before infant vaccines.

Open speaker notes

BREAST CARE

BREAST CARE DURING PREGNANCY

Supportive, well-fitting bra. Wash with water only — no soap on nipples. Colostrum by 3rd tri — normal. Assess flat/inverted nipples at 36 weeks. Montgomery tubercles = normal. Monthly breast self-exam continues.

Open speaker notes

"Breast care during pregnancy. Wear a supportive, well-fitting bra with wide straps. Wash breasts with water only — avoid soap on the nipples, which dries and removes natural lubricants. Colostrum in the third trimester is normal. Assess for flat or inverted nipples at 36 weeks. Montgomery tubercles are normal and provide lubrication. Continue monthly breast self-exam."

PSYCHOSOCIAL ADAPTATIONS

PSYCHOSOCIAL ADAPTATIONS

~15–20% experience perinatal mood disorders. Screen at EVERY prenatal visit.

Normal responses: → Ambivalence (1st tri) → Introversion (1st/3rd tri) → Acceptance (2nd tri) → Mood swings (throughout)

Open speaker notes

"Psychosocial adaptations. Fifteen to twenty percent of pregnant and postpartum patients experience perinatal mood disorders, so screen at every prenatal visit. Normal emotional responses include ambivalence in the first trimester, introversion in the first and third trimesters, acceptance in the second trimester as fetal movement is felt, and mood swings throughout. These are normal adaptive tasks, not pathology."

TAKE NOTE: PERINATAL DEPRESSION

TAKE NOTE! PERINATAL DEPRESSION

Most common complication of pregnancy.

Screen with Edinburgh Scale at every prenatal visit.

Red flags: anhedonia, persistent sadness, thoughts of self-harm.

Sertraline = preferred SSRI. Do NOT abruptly stop antidepressants.

Open speaker notes

"Take note. Perinatal depression is the most common complication of pregnancy. Screen with the Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale at every prenatal visit. Red flags include persistent sadness, anhedonia, and thoughts of self-harm. Sertraline is the preferred SSRI in pregnancy and breastfeeding. Never abruptly discontinue antidepressants. Refer to mental health services if screening is positive."
