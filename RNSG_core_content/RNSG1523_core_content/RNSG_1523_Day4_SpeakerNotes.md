---

## SLIDE 04 — SCREENING vs DIAGNOSTIC
"This is the most important distinction in the entire session. Screening tests identify risk. Diagnostic tests confirm a diagnosis. Screening tests are noninvasive with minimal risk. Diagnostic tests may be invasive with procedure-related risks. An abnormal screening result needs further diagnostic follow-up. A diagnostic result is definitive. Confusing these two is a common source of error and patient anxiety."

---

## SLIDE 05 — TAKE NOTE: SCREENING NOT DIAGNOSIS
"Take note of this critical principle. A positive screen does not diagnose. It estimates increased risk. Never treat a screening result as a diagnosis. When a screen is positive, offer diagnostic testing such as CVS or amniocentesis. A positive cfDNA or quad screen means the risk is higher — it does not mean the fetus is definitely affected."

---

## SLIDE 06 — PRENATAL SCREENING TESTS PART 1
"Prenatal screening tests are offered to all pregnant patients. The complete blood count at the first visit screens for anemia and infection. Blood type and Rh factor identify Rh-negative patients. Rubella titer screens for immunity. Hepatitis B surface antigen, HIV with opt-out testing, and syphilis are all screened at the first visit. Each positive result triggers specific management."

---

## SLIDE 07 — PRENATAL SCREENING TESTS PART 2
"Additional screening tests are timed to specific gestational ages. MSAFP is drawn at 15 to 20 weeks. The quad screen at 15 to 22 weeks. Cell-free DNA, also called NIPT, is available from nine to ten weeks. Nuchal translucency ultrasound at 11 to 14 weeks. GDM screening at 24 to 28 weeks. And Group B Streptococcus culture at 36 to 37 weeks."

---

## SLIDE 08 — MSAFP & QUAD SCREEN
"MSAFP, or maternal serum alpha-fetoprotein, is drawn at 15 to 20 weeks, optimally 16 to 18 weeks. An elevated level suggests neural tube defects, omphalocele, or multiple gestation. A low level suggests trisomy 21 or 18. The quad screen adds hCG, unconjugated estriol, and inhibin A to AFP, improving Down syndrome detection. Abnormal results warrant detailed ultrasound and genetic counseling."

---

## SLIDE 09 — cfDNA & NUCHAL TRANSLUCENCY
"Cell-free fetal DNA is a noninvasive screen using maternal blood, available from nine to ten weeks. It has high sensitivity for trisomy 21, 18, and 13, but it is screening only — a positive result still requires diagnostic confirmation. Nuchal translucency ultrasound measures the fluid-filled space at the fetal neck between 11 and 14 weeks. Increased thickness suggests chromosomal or cardiac anomalies. Both estimate risk and need diagnostic confirmation."

---

## SLIDE 10 — DIAGNOSTIC TESTS OVERVIEW
"Diagnostic tests are definitive but carry procedure-related risks, so they require informed consent. The five we cover are ultrasound, which is noninvasive; amniocentesis at 15 to 20 weeks; chorionic villus sampling at 10 to 13 weeks; the nonstress test after 32 weeks; and the biophysical profile in the third trimester."

---

## SLIDE 11 — ULTRASOUND
"Ultrasound is noninvasive with no known risks. The first-trimester transvaginal ultrasound is the most accurate method of dating a pregnancy. The 18 to 20 week scan is the detailed anatomy scan. The 32 to 36 week scan assesses growth and position. Ultrasound visualizes gestational age, fetal number, viability, anatomy, placental location, and amniotic fluid."

---

## SLIDE 12 — AMNIOCENTESIS
"Amniocentesis is performed at 15 to 20 weeks. A transabdominal needle aspirates amniotic fluid under ultrasound guidance. It detects chromosomal abnormalities, neural tube defects, metabolic errors, and fetal lung maturity. Risks include miscarriage at about one in 300 to 500, infection, and Rh sensitization. Nursing care includes informed consent, administering RhoGAM if the patient is Rh-negative, monitoring vital signs and fetal heart rate after the procedure, and teaching danger signs to report."

---

## SLIDE 13 — CVS
"Chorionic villus sampling is performed at 10 to 13 weeks, earlier than amniocentesis. It aspirates chorionic villi, which are placental tissue of embryonic origin. It detects chromosomal disorders, cystic fibrosis, enzyme deficiencies, and sex-linked disorders. Importantly, it does not detect neural tube defects because there is no amniotic fluid AFP. Risks include miscarriage at about one in 200 to 400 and bleeding."

---

## SLIDE 14 — NST INTERPRETATION
"The nonstress test is noninvasive and assessed after 32 weeks. A reactive result requires two or more accelerations of at least 15 beats per minute lasting 15 seconds within 20 minutes. This indicates adequate fetal oxygenation and is reassuring. A nonreactive result fails to meet criteria within 40 minutes. Over fifty percent are false positives due to fetal sleep or prematurity. The next step is to repeat the test, use vibroacoustic stimulation, or proceed to BPP."

---

## SLIDE 15 — BPP SCORING
"The biophysical profile combines ultrasound with the nonstress test. Five components are scored, two points each: fetal breathing, fetal movement, fetal tone, amniotic fluid volume, and NST reactivity. A score of eight to ten is normal. Six is equivocal, so repeat in 24 hours or deliver if near term. A score of four or less is abnormal and associated with fetal compromise — consider expedited delivery."

---

## SLIDE 16 — TAKE NOTE: HYPOXIA ORDER
"Take note of the fetal hypoxia progression. The fetus loses functions in reverse developmental order. Tone is lost first, because it was acquired at eight weeks. Then movement, acquired at nine weeks. Then breathing, acquired at 20 weeks. Then FHR reactivity, acquired at 24 weeks. So in hypoxia, tone is the first thing to go. This is a high-yield testing point."

---

## SLIDE 17 — ETHICS IN TESTING
"The ethical principles of prenatal testing. Autonomy means the patient decides about her own body. Informed consent is required for invasive procedures. Beneficence weighs the benefit of early diagnosis against the risk of loss. Confidentiality protects sensitive genetic information. Nondirective counseling presents all options without bias. Justice ensures equitable access regardless of socioeconomic status."

---

## SLIDE 18 — TAKE NOTE: PATIENT DECIDES
"Take note. The decision to test, and what to do with the results, rests solely with the patient. Nurses provide nondirective support. Some patients decline testing entirely, especially if they would not terminate regardless of results. Respect the decision. GINA 2008 protects against genetic discrimination by health insurers and employers only — it does not cover life, disability, or long-term care insurance."

---

## SLIDE 19 — INTEGRATED CASE
"Let us work through an integrated case. A 36-year-old G2 P1 at 32 weeks presents with decreased fetal movement over 24 hours and a frontal headache. Blood pressure is 148 over 92, urine protein is 2 plus, and fundal height is 30 centimeters. Recognize the cues: decreased movement, elevated blood pressure, and proteinuria. Analyze: elevated blood pressure plus proteinuria plus headache suggests preeclampsia, and decreased movement suggests fetal hypoxia. Prioritize: preeclampsia is the primary maternal concern."

---

## SLIDE 20 — CASE ACTIONS
"The priority actions. Notify the provider stat. Obtain a nonstress test immediately. Position the patient left lateral to optimize placental perfusion. Initiate continuous fetal monitoring. Draw labs including CBC, platelets, and liver function tests. And anticipate magnesium sulfate for seizure prophylaxis if severe features develop."

---

## SLIDE 21 — KEY TERMS SCREENING
"Key terminology for screening. NT is nuchal translucency. cfDNA and NIPT refer to cell-free DNA screening. MSAFP is maternal serum alpha-fetoprotein. The quad screen combines AFP, hCG, unconjugated estriol, and inhibin A. GCT is the one-hour glucose challenge, a screening test. OGTT is the three-hour glucose tolerance test, the diagnostic test. GBS is Group B Streptococcus."

---

## SLIDE 22 — KEY TERMS DIAGNOSTIC
"Key terminology for diagnostics. CVS is chorionic villus sampling at 10 to 13 weeks. Amniocentesis provides a karyotype at 15 weeks or later. NST is the nonstress test. BPP is the biophysical profile with five components. CST is the contraction stress test. RhIG or RhoGAM is Rh immune globulin. MCA Doppler surveils for fetal anemia."

---

## SLIDE 23 — NGN CASE QUAD SCREEN
"An NGN case. Priya is 33, G2 P1, at 17 weeks. Her quad screen is positive for trisomy 21. She calls anxious, asking, does my baby have Down syndrome? The key teaching point is that a positive screen is a risk estimate, not a diagnosis. Your job is to correct that misunderstanding before any decision."

---

## SLIDE 24 — CASE COUNSELING
"The correct counseling actions. Explain that the screen estimates risk and does not diagnose. Describe diagnostic options such as amniocentesis and emphasize it is her choice. Offer genetic counseling referral. Do not tell her the baby has Down syndrome. Do not recommend a specific decision. Do not state that no further information exists. Nondirective, accurate information supports informed autonomy."

---

## SLIDE 25 — NGN CASE Rh-NEGATIVE
"Another case. Aisha is 24, G1 P0, at 10 weeks. Her blood type is O negative, her antibody screen is negative, and her partner is Rh-positive. The key point is that a negative antibody screen means she is unsensitized — which is exactly the population that benefits from prophylaxis."

---

## SLIDE 26 — CASE ANTI-D PLAN
"The anti-D plan. Administer RhoGAM routinely at about 28 weeks. Administer after any sensitizing event such as bleeding or amniocentesis. Administer within 72 hours postpartum if the newborn is Rh-positive. Do not give it only if symptoms develop. Do not withhold it because the antibody screen is negative. And do not substitute the rubella vaccine — anti-D is passive antibody prophylaxis, not immunization."

## SLIDE 35 — EXIT TICKET
"Exit ticket questions. First, is cfDNA a screening or diagnostic test? Screening. Second, what is the GBS culture window? Thirty-six weeks and zero days to 37 weeks and six days. Third, what is the routine antenatal anti-D timing? About 28 weeks, plus within 72 hours postpartum if the infant is Rh-positive. Fourth, an abnormal one-hour glucose challenge — what is the next step? The three-hour OGTT, the diagnostic test."

## SLIDE 36 — ANSWER KEY Q1 TO Q4
"Answers. Question one: C — screening identifies risk, diagnostic confirms diagnosis. Question two: B — the one-hour test uses a 50-gram glucose load without fasting, drawing blood one hour later. Question three: B — reactive, a reassuring result meeting acceleration criteria. Question four: A — RhoGAM after an invasive procedure prevents Rh sensitization."

"Question five: C — a BPP of four or less is abnormal and associated with fetal compromise, so consider delivery. Question six: B — autonomy means respecting her choice to decline, documenting, and continuing support. Question seven: B — CVS is earlier than amniocentesis and both are diagnostic. Question eight: B — cfDNA is a screen, so a high-risk result needs diagnostic confirmation."

## SLIDE 38 — QUICK RECALL PART 1
"Quick recall. Screening identifies risk; diagnostic confirms. Rh-negative management: RhoGAM at 28 weeks, postpartum, and after any procedure or bleeding. Rubella titer of 1 to 8 or less means not immune, so immunize postpartum. Hepatitis B positive means the newborn gets HBIG and vaccine within 12 hours of birth."

---

## SLIDE 39 — QUICK RECALL PART 2
"MSAFP at 15 to 20 weeks: elevated suggests neural tube defect, low suggests trisomy. Quad screen at 15 to 22 weeks. cfDNA from nine to ten weeks, screening only. Nuchal translucency at 11 to 14 weeks. GDM screen at 24 to 28 weeks. GBS culture at 36 to 37 weeks."

---

## SLIDE 40 — QUICK RECALL PART 3
"Amniocentesis at 15 to 20 weeks, with RhoGAM if Rh-negative. CVS at 10 to 13 weeks, which does not detect neural tube defects. NST reactive means two accelerations in 20 minutes. BPP: eight to ten normal, four or less means deliver. Tone is lost first in hypoxia. Informed consent is required for invasive tests. GINA 2008 covers insurers and employers only."
