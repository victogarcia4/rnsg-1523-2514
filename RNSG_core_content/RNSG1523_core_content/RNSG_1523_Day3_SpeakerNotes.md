## SLIDE 04 — NUTRITIONAL NEEDS OVERVIEW
"Adequate nutrition is essential for fetal growth, maternal health, and positive birth outcomes. Inadequate or excessive intake contributes to preterm birth, congenital anomalies, miscarriage, gestational diabetes, and small-for-gestational-age newborns. Critically, iron at 27 milligrams per day and folic acid at 400 to 800 micrograms per day must be supplemented because diet alone cannot meet the increased demands of pregnancy."

---

## SLIDE 05 — DRI TABLE
"Here are the dietary reference intakes during pregnancy. Calories increase to 2,200 to 2,600 per day. Protein rises to 71 grams. Folate rises to 600 micrograms. Iron rises to 27 milligrams. Calcium is 1,000 to 1,300 milligrams. Vitamin D remains 600 international units. Notice how iron and folate requirements jump dramatically compared to the non-pregnant state — this is why supplementation is essential."

---

## SLIDE 06 — WEIGHT GAIN BY BMI
"Recommended weight gain depends on prepregnancy BMI. Underweight patients should gain 28 to 40 pounds. Normal BMI patients gain 25 to 35 pounds. Overweight patients gain 15 to 25 pounds. Obese patients gain only 11 to 20 pounds. For a normal BMI patient, the rate is about one pound per week in the second and third trimesters. Individualize the plan based on BMI."

---

## SLIDE 07 — FOOD SAFETY
"Food safety during pregnancy is as important as adequate intake. Avoid high-mercury fish including shark, swordfish, king mackerel, and tilefish. To prevent listeria, avoid unpasteurized dairy, deli meats unless heated until steaming, and soft cheeses. There is no safe amount of alcohol and no safe trimester. Limit caffeine to less than 200 to 300 milligrams per day."

---

## SLIDE 08 — SPECIAL CONSIDERATIONS
"Special nutritional considerations. Pica is the compulsive ingestion of nonfood substances such as dirt, ice, or laundry starch. Assess the patient non-judgmentally and screen for iron-deficiency anemia, which is commonly associated. For lactose intolerance, substitute lactose-free dairy and calcium-enriched alternatives. For vegetarian and vegan patients, ensure adequate protein, iron, calcium, vitamin B12, and vitamin D through fortified foods and supplements."

---

## SLIDE 09 — TAKE NOTE: DIETING
"Take note of this fundamental rule. Dieting is never recommended during pregnancy, even for patients with overweight or obesity. Severe caloric restriction is associated with decreased birth weight. Focus on nutrient density instead. The additional requirement is 340 calories per day in the second trimester and 450 calories per day in the third trimester."

---

## SLIDE 10 — DISCOMFORTS OVERVIEW
"Most pregnant patients experience discomforts. The critical nursing principle is to always rule out pathologic causes before attributing symptoms to normal pregnancy. We organize the common discomforts by trimester, and each has a specific, evidence-based nursing intervention."

---

## SLIDE 11 — FIRST-TRIMESTER DISCOMFORTS
"First-trimester discomforts. Urinary frequency is managed by limiting evening fluids and ruling out infection. Fatigue is managed with daily naps and regular moderate exercise. Nausea and vomiting respond to crackers before rising and small frequent meals. Breast tenderness responds to a supportive bra. Constipation responds to fiber, fluids, and activity."

---

## SLIDE 12 — SECOND/THIRD-TRIMESTER DISCOMFORTS
"Second and third trimester discomforts. Backache responds to pelvic tilt exercises and acetaminophen. Leg cramps are managed by dorsiflexing the foot — never point the toes. Heartburn responds to small meals and elevating the head of the bed. Varicosities respond to walking and leg elevation. Leukorrhea requires perineal hygiene. Braxton Hicks contractions are normal and respond to position change."

---

## SLIDE 13 — NAUSEA MANAGEMENT
"Nausea and vomiting affects over ninety percent of pregnant patients. Start with crackers or toast before arising. Eat small frequent meals, five to six per day. Separate fluids from solids. Avoid triggers like strong odors and greasy foods. Ginger at one gram per day and vitamin B6 at 10 to 25 milligrams every six to eight hours are first-line. Diclégis, combining doxylamine and pyridoxine, is FDA-approved. Severe vomiting that does not respond is hyperemesis gravidarum."

---

## SLIDE 14 — TAKE NOTE: HYPEREMESIS
"Take note. Nausea and vomiting affects ninety percent of patients, but hyperemesis gravidarum is not normal morning sickness. The diagnostic criteria are greater than five percent weight loss, dehydration, and ketonuria. Treatment is NPO to rest the gut, then IV fluids, then antiemetics. Do not force oral intake — it prolongs vomiting."

---

## SLIDE 15 — NORMAL vs WARNING SIGNS
"Now the critical distinction between normal discomfort and warning signs. Severity, suddenness, and location separate danger from normal. For example, mild dependent edema is normal, but sudden facial and hand edema with hypertension is preeclampsia. Teach every pregnant patient to report warning signs immediately and not to wait for the next scheduled appointment."

---

## SLIDE 16 — WARNING SIGNS TABLE
"Here is the warning signs table. Severe vomiting suggests hyperemesis. Dysuria with fever suggests UTI or pyelonephritis. Rhythmic back pain with contractions suggests preterm labor. Severe headache with visual changes suggests preeclampsia. Unilateral calf pain suggests DVT. And a sudden gush of fluid suggests rupture of membranes. Each of these requires immediate evaluation."

---

## SLIDE 17 — EDEMA: NORMAL vs DANGER
"The edema distinction deserves special attention because it is frequently tested. Normal edema is dependent, in the lower extremities, improves with elevation, and has no blood pressure elevation. Danger signs are sudden facial and hand edema, sudden weight gain, elevated blood pressure, and proteinuria — this cluster points to preeclampsia. Remember: sudden facial and hand swelling is a warning sign. Evening ankle swelling is normal."

---

## SLIDE 18 — MEDICATIONS: PLLR
"Now medications in pregnancy. In 2015, the FDA replaced the old A-B-C-D-X letter categories with the Pregnancy and Lactation Labeling Rule, or PLLR. This new system provides narrative risk information in three subsections: pregnancy, lactation, and reproductive potential. The old letters still appear in older textbooks, so teach both. Under PLLR, ACE inhibitors and ARBs are fetotoxic. Isotretinoin is contraindicated."

---

## SLIDE 19 — SAFE MEDICATIONS
"Medications generally considered safe in pregnancy. Acetaminophen is the preferred analgesic and antipyretic. Prenatal vitamins provide iron and folic acid. Calcium carbonate is safe for heartburn. Famotidine is used for refractory GERD. Diclégis is first-line for nausea. Vitamin B6 and ginger are safe for morning sickness."

---

## SLIDE 20 — NSAIDs IN PREGNANCY
"NSAIDs such as ibuprofen are contraindicated in the third trimester. They cause premature closure of the ductus arteriosus, oligohydramnios, and prolonged labor. In the first and second trimesters, use only if clearly needed. Acetaminophen is the safer alternative for pain. This is a frequently tested point."

---

## SLIDE 21 — IMMUNIZATIONS
"Immunizations in pregnancy. Safe and recommended: Tdap at 27 to 36 weeks in every pregnancy, inactivated influenza in any trimester, and COVID-19 vaccine in any trimester. Avoid live vaccines: MMR, varicella, and the intranasal influenza vaccine, FluMist. RhoGAM is given at 28 weeks if the patient is Rh-negative."

---

## SLIDE 22 — TAKE NOTE: Tdap
"Take note. Tdap is recommended at 27 to 36 weeks during every pregnancy, regardless of prior vaccination history. The purpose is passive transfer of pertussis antibodies to the newborn before infant vaccination begins at two months. Influenza vaccine must be the inactivated form only. Live vaccines are contraindicated during pregnancy."

---

## SLIDE 23 — SUBSTANCES TO AVOID
"Substances to avoid. Alcohol causes fetal alcohol spectrum disorder — there is no safe amount. Tobacco causes IUGR, placental abruption, and increased SIDS risk. Cannabis increases low birth weight risk. Opioids cause neonatal abstinence syndrome. Cocaine causes placental abruption and fetal demise. Caffeine above 300 milligrams daily increases miscarriage risk. Screen non-judgmentally and refer for treatment."

---

## SLIDE 24 — BREAST CARE
"Breast care during pregnancy. Wear a supportive, well-fitting bra with wide straps. Wash breasts with water only — avoid soap on the nipples, which dries and removes natural lubricants. Colostrum in the third trimester is normal. Assess for flat or inverted nipples at 36 weeks. Montgomery tubercles are normal and provide lubrication. Continue monthly breast self-exam."

---

## SLIDE 25 — SELF-CARE & EXERCISE
"Self-care and health promotion. Exercise is 150 minutes per week of moderate activity, stopping if bleeding or fluid leakage occurs. Sleep in the left lateral position and avoid supine after 20 weeks. Avoid douching and tampons. Travel is safest in the second trimester, 14 to 28 weeks."

---

## SLIDE 26 — PSYCHOSOCIAL ADAPTATIONS
"Psychosocial adaptations. Fifteen to twenty percent of pregnant and postpartum patients experience perinatal mood disorders, so screen at every prenatal visit. Normal emotional responses include ambivalence in the first trimester, introversion in the first and third trimesters, acceptance in the second trimester as fetal movement is felt, and mood swings throughout. These are normal adaptive tasks, not pathology."

---

## SLIDE 27 — RUBIN'S MATERNAL TASKS
"Rubin's maternal tasks describe the psychological work of pregnancy. Task one is ensuring safe passage through pregnancy and birth. Task two is seeking acceptance of the infant by others. Task three is seeking acceptance of self in the maternal role, also called binding in. Task four is learning to give of oneself. These tasks progress across the trimesters."

---

## SLIDE 28 — TAKE NOTE: PERINATAL DEPRESSION
"Take note. Perinatal depression is the most common complication of pregnancy. Screen with the Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale at every prenatal visit. Red flags include persistent sadness, anhedonia, and thoughts of self-harm. Sertraline is the preferred SSRI in pregnancy and breastfeeding. Never abruptly discontinue antidepressants. Refer to mental health services if screening is positive."

---

## SLIDE 29 — KEY TERMS NUTRITION & MEDS
"Key terminology. BMI is body mass index, which sets the weight gain target. GWG is gestational weight gain. PLLR is the Pregnancy and Lactation Labeling Rule. ACE inhibitors and ARBs are fetotoxic antihypertensives. WIC is the supplemental nutrition program. PROM is prelabor rupture of membranes. IPV is intimate partner violence, which we screen for privately."

---

## SLIDE 30 — NGN CASE WARNING-SIGN TRIAGE
"Let us apply this with an NGN case. Tanya is 26 years old, G1 P0, at 30 weeks. She calls the triage line reporting a severe headache, blurred vision, mild nasal congestion, evening ankle swelling, and new puffiness of her face and hands since this morning. Your job is to identify which reports are warning signs and which are normal discomforts."

---

## SLIDE 31 — CASE ANALYZE CUES
"Analyzing the cues. Severe headache is a warning sign. Blurred vision is a warning sign. Sudden facial and hand swelling is a warning sign. Mild nasal congestion is normal. Evening ankle swelling is normal. The cluster of headache, visual change, and sudden facial swelling points to preeclampsia. Remember, one or two warning signs in a list still trigger urgent evaluation regardless of the benign items present."

---

## SLIDE 32 — CASE PRIORITY ACTIONS
"The priority actions. First, recognize the warning-sign cluster and its urgency. Second, direct the patient to immediate evaluation and arrange safe transport. Third, communicate the report to the provider or OB triage. Fourth, document the call, the advice given, and the disposition. Do not advise acetaminophen and call back tomorrow. Do not reassure her that headaches are always normal. Warning-sign clusters require prompt in-person assessment."
