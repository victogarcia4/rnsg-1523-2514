## SLIDE 04 — HYPERTENSIVE SPECTRUM
"Hypertensive disorders complicate up to sixteen percent of pregnancies and are the leading cause of maternal death. The spectrum moves from chronic hypertension, present before 20 weeks, to gestational hypertension, new after 20 weeks without proteinuria, to preeclampsia, hypertension with proteinuria or end-organ dysfunction, to eclampsia, which adds tonic-clonic seizures."

---

## SLIDE 05 — PREECLAMPSIA SEVERITY
"Preeclampsia without severe features has blood pressure of at least 140 over 90 and mild symptoms. With severe features, blood pressure reaches 160 over 110 or higher, with severe headache, blurred vision, platelets below 100,000, and possible HELLP. Eclampsia adds seizures. Remember, preeclampsia can be diagnosed on severe features even without proteinuria."

---

## SLIDE 06 — MAGNESIUM SULFATE
"Magnesium sulfate is seizure prophylaxis. It is not an antihypertensive. Severe-range blood pressure needs a separate antihypertensive such as labetalol, hydralazine, or nifedipine. The loading dose is four to six grams, then maintenance at one to two grams per hour. Monitor deep tendon reflexes, respiratory rate, and urine output."

---

## SLIDE 07 — TAKE NOTE: MAG TOXICITY
"Take note of magnesium toxicity. The sequence is loss of deep tendon reflexes, then respiratory depression, then cardiac arrest. Deep tendon reflexes disappear first. The antidote is calcium gluconate, which must be kept at the bedside. The first action in toxicity is to stop the infusion and give calcium gluconate."

---

## SLIDE 08 — HELLP SYNDROME
"HELLP stands for Hemolysis, Elevated Liver enzymes, and Low Platelets. It is a severe variant of preeclampsia occurring in four to twelve percent of cases. Findings include epigastric or right-upper-quadrant pain, nausea, and headache. Labs show decreased hemoglobin, elevated LDH, elevated AST and ALT, and platelets below 100,000."

---

## SLIDE 09 — GDM OVERVIEW
"Gestational diabetes is glucose intolerance with onset during pregnancy. Placental hormones cause insulin resistance. Risk factors include obesity, prior GDM, PCOS, family history, and a prior large-for-gestational-age infant. Class A1 is diet-controlled, and A2 requires medication. Insulin is first-line because it does not cross the placenta."

---

## SLIDE 10 — GDM SCREENING & TARGETS
"GDM is screened with a 75-gram OGTT at 24 to 28 weeks. Diagnostic thresholds are fasting at least 92, one-hour at least 180, and two-hour at least 153. Treatment targets are fasting below 95, one-hour below 140, and two-hour below 120. Any single value meeting the threshold diagnoses GDM."

---

## SLIDE 11 — GDM EFFECTS
"GDM affects both parent and fetus. The parent risks preeclampsia, polyhydramnios, UTI, cesarean, and future type 2 diabetes. The fetus risks macrosomia, shoulder dystocia, neonatal hypoglycemia, RDS, and hyperbilirubinemia."

---

## SLIDE 12 — PRETERM LABOR
"Preterm labor is regular contractions with cervical change before 37 weeks. Warning signs include a change in vaginal discharge, pelvic pressure, low backache, menstrual-like cramps, and more than six contractions per hour. Testing includes cervical length less than 30 millimeters and fetal fibronectin above 50 nanograms per milliliter."

---

## SLIDE 13 — PRETERM MEDICATIONS
"Medications for preterm labor. Betamethasone matures fetal lungs, given as two IM doses 24 hours apart. Nifedipine is a first-line tocolytic. Indomethacin is used before 32 weeks for a short course. Magnesium provides neuroprotection before 32 weeks, reducing cerebral palsy. Remember, tocolytics delay birth two to seven days for steroids and transport — they do not prevent preterm birth."

---

## SLIDE 14 — PROM & PPROM
"PROM is prelabor rupture of membranes at 37 weeks or later. PPROM is rupture before 37 weeks with higher infection risk. Diagnosis is by pooling of fluid in the vaginal vault, the fern test showing salt crystallization, and nitrazine paper turning blue because amniotic fluid is alkaline."

---

## SLIDE 15 — TAKE NOTE: NO DIGITAL EXAM
"Take note. With PROM or PPROM, never perform a digital cervical exam — use a sterile speculum only. Digital exams raise infection risk. Each exam after rupture adds risk. Watch for chorioamnionitis: fever, maternal or fetal tachycardia, uterine tenderness, and foul-smelling fluid. Infection ends expectant management."

---

## SLIDE 16 — KEY TERMS HYPERTENSION
"Key terminology. gHTN is gestational hypertension. HELLP is hemolysis, elevated liver enzymes, and low platelets. LDH is lactate dehydrogenase, elevated in hemolysis. DTRs are deep tendon reflexes, monitored on magnesium. Magnesium sulfate is MgSO4. RUQ is right-upper-quadrant pain, a liver capsule signal. The P-to-C ratio is the urine protein-to-creatinine ratio."

---

## SLIDE 17 — KEY TERMS DIABETES & PRETERM
"More terminology. GDM A1 is diet-controlled, A2 requires medication. HbA1c reflects three-month glucose. PTL is preterm labor. PROM is rupture at term, PPROM is preterm rupture. fFN is fetal fibronectin, where a negative result strongly predicts no delivery within two weeks. ACS is antenatal corticosteroids."

---

## SLIDE 18 — NGN CASE PREECLAMPSIA
"An NGN case. Grace is 34, G1 P0, at 35 weeks, admitted with blood pressure 158 over 104, headache, right-upper-quadrant pain, platelets 130,000, AST 40, and P-to-C ratio 0.5. Six hours later, platelets dropped to 88,000, AST rose to 160, LDH to 700, and blood pressure to 166 over 112. This is progression to preeclampsia with severe features and HELLP. The falling platelet and rising enzyme trajectory is the alarm."

---

## SLIDE 19 — CASE PRIORITY ACTIONS
"The priority actions. Begin magnesium sulfate for seizure prophylaxis. Give a separate antihypertensive such as labetalol or nifedipine for the severe-range blood pressure. Plan delivery and give corticosteroids if indicated. Keep calcium gluconate at the bedside and monitor deep tendon reflexes, respirations, and urine output. Magnesium does not lower blood pressure."

---

## SLIDE 20 — NGN CASE PPROM
"Another case. Leah is 27, G2 P1, at 30 weeks, with a sudden gush of clear fluid. Nitrazine turns blue and ferning is seen, confirming PPROM. Management is expectant: antenatal corticosteroids for lung maturity, latency antibiotics, and magnesium for neuroprotection before 32 weeks if birth is imminent. Minimize digital vaginal exams to reduce infection."

## SLIDE 27 — EXIT TICKET
"Exit ticket questions. The purpose of magnesium in preeclampsia is seizure prophylaxis, not antihypertensive. The antidote for magnesium toxicity is calcium gluconate. HELLP stands for hemolysis, elevated liver enzymes, and low platelets. And blood pressure of 162 over 110 with platelets of 84,000 and no proteinuria is preeclampsia with severe features."

---

## SLIDE 28 — ANSWER KEY Q1 TO Q3
"Answers. Question one: A — magnesium sulfate, because severe features signal impending eclampsia and magnesium prevents seizures. Question two: B — decreased platelets, elevated AST and ALT, and elevated LDH confirm HELLP. Question three: B — calcium gluconate, the antidote for magnesium toxicity."

---

## SLIDE 29 — ANSWER KEY Q4 TO Q6
"Question four: B — fasting of 96 meets the threshold of 92. Question five: A — betamethasone matures fetal lungs and reduces RDS and intraventricular hemorrhage. Question six: C — digital exam is contraindicated with PROM; use a sterile speculum."

---

## SLIDE 30 — QUICK RECALL PART 1
"Quick recall. Chronic hypertension is at least 140 over 90 before 20 weeks. Gestational hypertension is after 20 weeks without proteinuria. Preeclampsia adds proteinuria or end-organ dysfunction. Eclampsia adds seizures. HELLP is hemolysis, elevated liver enzymes, and low platelets. DIC shows decreased platelets, decreased fibrinogen, and positive D-dimer. Magnesium is seizure prophylaxis, with calcium gluconate as the antidote."

---

## SLIDE 31 — QUICK RECALL PART 2
"GDM screening is the OGTT at 24 to 28 weeks, fasting target below 95, with insulin first-line. Preterm labor is contractions with cervical change, treated with betamethasone and tocolytics. PROM and PPROM require no digital exam, with nitrazine and ferning for diagnosis, and watch for infection. Chorioamnionitis presents with fever, tachycardia, and uterine tenderness, requiring delivery."
