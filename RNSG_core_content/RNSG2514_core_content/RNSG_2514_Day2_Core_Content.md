## 02 1. HYPERTENSIVE DISORDERS OF PREGNANCY

Hypertensive disorders complicate up to 16% of pregnancies and are the leading cause of maternal death in the United States. They form a spectrum.

### The Spectrum

| CONDITION | BP | PROTEINURIA | TIMING / NOTES |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Chronic hypertension** | ≥140/90 | No | Before pregnancy or before 20 weeks; may persist after birth |
| **Gestational hypertension (gHTN)** | ≥140/90 | No | After 20 weeks; resolves by 12 weeks postpartum |
| **Preeclampsia** | ≥140/90 | Yes (≥300 mg/24 h) **OR** organ dysfunction | New onset after 20 weeks; targets cardiovascular, hepatic, renal, CNS |
| **Eclampsia** | Usually severe | Variable | Preeclampsia + tonic-clonic seizures = emergency |

> **Terminology note:** Say "gestational hypertension" — NOT "PIH." Say "preeclampsia without or with severe features" — NOT "mild vs severe."

### Preeclampsia: Without vs With Severe Features vs Eclampsia

| FEATURE | WITHOUT SEVERE | WITH SEVERE | ECLAMPSIA |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Blood pressure | ≥140/90 | ≥160/110 | >160/110 |
| Seizures | No | No | **Yes** |
| Key findings | Headache, oliguria | Severe headache, blurred vision, epigastric/RUQ pain, thrombocytopenia <100,000, pulmonary edema, HELLP | Seizure, cerebral hemorrhage, renal failure, HELLP |

**Risk factors:** primigravida • age <20 or >35 • obesity • prior preeclampsia • diabetes/chronic HTN/kidney disease • multiple gestation • family history.

**Prevention:** low-dose aspirin **81 mg daily from 12–16 weeks** for high-risk patients.

### Management

| SEVERITY | CARE |
| --- | --- |
| Without severe features | Home: rest, BP checks, fetal kick counts, labs (CBC, liver enzymes, platelets); admit if BP rises or symptoms appear; expectant management to 37 weeks |
| With severe features | Hospitalize: antihypertensives (hydralazine, labetalol, nifedipine), **magnesium sulfate**, quiet/dim room, seizure precautions, continuous fetal monitoring, deliver |
| Eclampsia | Seizure management; turn patient on side; pad rails; suction + O2; continue magnesium sulfate 24 h after last seizure; then deliver |

## 03 MAGNESIUM SULFATE (SEIZURE PROPHYLAXIS)

**Magnesium sulfate is seizure prophylaxis — NOT an antihypertensive.** Severe-range BP needs a separate antihypertensive (labetalol, hydralazine, or nifedipine).

| Parameter | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Dose | Loading 4–6 g IV over 15–30 min, then maintenance 1–2 g/h |
| Monitor | DTRs, respiratory rate, hourly urine output, serum magnesium |
| Toxicity signs | **RR <12/min**, **absent DTRs**, **urine output <30 mL/h**, lethargy, hypotension |
| Toxicity sequence | Loss of DTRs → respiratory depression → cardiac arrest |
| Antidote | **Calcium gluconate** 10% 10 mL IV over 3 min — **keep at bedside** |
| First toxicity action | **Stop infusion**, give calcium gluconate |
| Newborn effect | May show respiratory/neurologic depression, hypotonia, hypocalcemia |

> **■ TAKE NOTE!** In the patient receiving magnesium sulfate, toxicity = respiratory rate <12/min, absent DTRs, and urine output <30 mL/h — give calcium gluconate. **Deep tendon reflexes disappear first in toxicity.**

> **The three things most likely to be tested:** (1) magnesium ≠ antihypertensive; (2) **reflexes disappear first** in toxicity; (3) antidote = **calcium gluconate**.

## 04 2. HELLP SYNDROME & DIC

**HELLP = Hemolysis, Elevated Liver enzymes, Low Platelets.** A severe variant of preeclampsia (4–12% of cases), onset 27 weeks to delivery (may be postpartum).

**Findings:** **epigastric / right-upper-quadrant pain** • nausea, malaise • headache • visual changes.

**Labs:** ↓ hemoglobin, **↑ LDH** (hemolysis) • **↑ AST/ALT** (liver) • **↓ platelets <100,000**.

**Complications:** DIC • liver rupture • placental abruption • eclampsia • acute renal failure • retinal detachment.

**Care:** stabilize BP • **magnesium sulfate** • steroids for fetal lungs • transfer to tertiary center • deliver.

**DIC Lab Picture:** ↓ platelets • ↓ fibrinogen • ↑ PT/aPTT • positive D-dimer.

**Preeclampsia can be diagnosed on severe features WITHOUT proteinuria** — do not gate action on a protein result. Severe features (thrombocytopenia, RUQ pain, transaminases) justify acting now.

**Discrimination:** gHTN is hypertension alone; preeclampsia adds proteinuria or end-organ dysfunction. HELLP adds hemolysis, elevated liver enzymes, and low platelets — may occur with mild BP.

## 05 3. GESTATIONAL DIABETES MELLITUS (GDM)

**Definition:** glucose intolerance with onset during pregnancy (usually 2nd/3rd trimester). Distinct from pregestational diabetes (type 1 / type 2 present before conception).

**Why it happens:** placental hormones (human placental lactogen, cortisol, prolactin, progesterone) cause insulin resistance; beta-cell dysfunction → hyperglycemia.

**Risk factors:** obesity • prior GDM • first-degree relative with DM • PCOS • prior infant >9 lb • hypertension • certain ancestries.

### Screening & Glucose Targets (75 g OGTT at 24–28 weeks)

| MEASURE | DIAGNOSTIC THRESHOLD (GDM) | TREATMENT TARGET |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fasting | ≥92 mg/dL | <95 mg/dL |
| 1-hour postprandial | ≥180 mg/dL | <140 mg/dL |
| 2-hour postprandial | ≥153 mg/dL | <120 mg/dL |

### Effects on Parent vs Fetus/Newborn

| EFFECTS ON PARENT | EFFECTS ON FETUS / NEWBORN |
| --- | --- |
| Hypertension, preeclampsia • polyhydramnios • UTI/monilial vaginitis • ketoacidosis • difficult labor, cesarean • future type 2 DM | Macrosomia • shoulder dystocia • **neonatal hypoglycemia** • respiratory distress syndrome • hyperbilirubinemia • polycythemia • congenital anomalies (pregestational) |

### Management

- **Nutrition (Diabetes Plate Method) + exercise**; monitor blood glucose 4x/day (before meals + bedtime).
- **Insulin is first-line when medication is needed** (does not cross the placenta).
- Metformin crosses the placenta — avoid in hypertension/preeclampsia.
- Frequent prenatal visits; serial ultrasounds, NST, biophysical profile; daily kick counts.
- **Breastfeeding encouraged** (lowers glucose, reduces future type 2 DM risk).
- **GDM A1** = diet-controlled; **GDM A2** = requires medication.

> **■ TAKE NOTE!** Nutrient needs and weight-gain goals are the same for pregnant people with and without diabetes; the difference is tight glucose control.

## 06 4. PRETERM LABOR

**Definition:** regular uterine contractions + cervical change (effacement & dilation) before 37 weeks.

**Warning signs:** change/increase in vaginal discharge • pelvic pressure • low dull backache • menstrual-like cramps • >6 contractions/hour.

**Testing:** cervical length <30 mm (increased risk); fetal fibronectin (fFN) >50 ng/mL predicts preterm birth within 14 days. **Negative fFN strongly predicts NOT delivering within two weeks.**

### Medications

| MEDICATION | ACTION | NURSING IMPLICATIONS |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Magnesium sulfate** | Tocolysis (off-label); **neuroprotection** for fetus <32 wk | Loading 4–6 g, then 1–4 g/h; assess DTRs hourly; watch toxicity (RR <12, absent DTRs, UO <30 mL/h); calcium gluconate at bedside |
| **Indomethacin (Indocin)** | Prostaglandin inhibitor (tocolytic) | Oral with food; monitor bleeding; **contraindicated >32 weeks**, peptic ulcer; use ≤48 h |
| **Nifedipine (Procardia)** | Calcium channel blocker (tocolytic) — common first-line | Caution with magnesium sulfate (hypotension); monitor BP/pulse; watch flushing, headache, edema |
| **Betamethasone (Celestone)** | Matures fetal lungs (surfactant); reduces RDS & intraventricular hemorrhage | Two IM doses 24 hours apart; monitor for maternal infection/pulmonary edema; given <34 weeks |

> **■ Tocolytics delay birth 2–7 days** (time for steroids + transport) — they do NOT prevent preterm birth.

**Contraindications to tocolysis:** abruption • fetal distress/death • severe preeclampsia/eclampsia • chorioamnionitis • active bleeding • dilation >6 cm.

**Nursing:** left side-lying • continuous fetal monitoring • limit vaginal exams • I&O • hydrate.

## 07 5. PROM & PPROM

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| **PROM** | Prelabor rupture of membranes at ≥37 weeks |
| **PPROM** | Preterm prelabor rupture of membranes — before 37 weeks, higher infection risk |

**Presentation:** leakage/gush of fluid, vaginal discharge, pelvic pressure — without contractions.

**Complications:** infection (chorioamnionitis) • cord compression/prolapse • placental abruption • preterm labor.

### Diagnosis

| TEST | FINDING |
| --- | --- |
| Pooling | Clear fluid pooled in the vaginal vault |
| Fern test | Dried fluid shows ferning (salt crystallization) under microscope |
| Nitrazine paper | Turns blue (amniotic fluid alkaline, pH 7.0 vs vaginal 4.5) |

### Management & Infection Watch

- **NO digital cervical exam** — sterile speculum only (digital exam raises infection risk). Minimize digital vaginal examinations — each exam adds infection risk after ROM.
- **Monitor for chorioamnionitis:** fever • maternal/fetal tachycardia >160 • uterine tenderness • cloudy, foul-smelling fluid • ↑ WBC.
- If lungs mature: induce labor. If immature (<34 wk): expectant management — hydration, pelvic rest, antibiotics, **corticosteroids**, close observation. MgSO4 for neuroprotection <32 wk if birth is imminent.
- **Home teaching:** daily temperature + kick counts • no tampons, douching, intercourse, tub bathing • report fever, contractions, foul-smelling discharge.

> **Latency ends the moment infection appears** — reassess continuously.

## 08 KEY TERMINOLOGY — DAY 2 MASTER DECK

**Hypertension**

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| HDP | Hypertensive Disorders of Pregnancy; spectrum from gHTN to preeclampsia and HELLP |
| Gestational hypertension (gHTN) | New hypertension after 20 weeks without proteinuria or severe features |
| Chronic hypertension | Hypertension before pregnancy or before 20 weeks |
| Preeclampsia | New hypertension after 20 weeks with proteinuria or end-organ signs |
| Preeclampsia w/ severe features | Severe BP (≥160/110), thrombocytopenia (<100k), renal/liver involvement, pulmonary edema, or CNS symptoms |
| HELLP | Hemolysis, Elevated Liver enzymes, Low Platelets; a severe preeclampsia variant |
| LDH | Lactate dehydrogenase; elevated with hemolysis of HELLP |
| AST/ALT | Liver transaminases; elevation signals hepatic involvement in HELLP or severe preeclampsia |
| P:C ratio | Urine protein-to-creatinine ratio; quantifies proteinuria without a 24-hour collection |
| DTRs | Deep Tendon Reflexes; monitored on magnesium — loss signals rising toxicity |
| Magnesium sulfate | Seizure prophylaxis in preeclampsia; **NOT an antihypertensive** |
| Magnesium antidote | Calcium gluconate reverses magnesium toxicity; keep at the bedside |
| MAP | Mean Arterial Pressure; average perfusion pressure across the cardiac cycle |
| Low-dose aspirin | 81 mg started 12–28 weeks for high-risk patients to reduce preeclampsia |
| RUQ pain | Right upper quadrant pain; liver capsule signal in HELLP or severe preeclampsia |

**Diabetes / Preterm / Membranes**

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| GDM A1 vs A2 | A1 gestational diabetes is diet-controlled; A2 requires medication |
| PGDM | Pregestational Diabetes Mellitus; diabetes predating pregnancy, higher anomaly risk |
| HbA1c | Glycosylated hemoglobin; reflects average glucose over roughly 3 months |
| Neonatal hypoglycemia | Low newborn glucose after maternal hyperglycemia caused fetal hyperinsulinism |
| PTL | Preterm Labor; regular contractions with cervical change before 37 weeks |
| PROM | Prelabor Rupture of Membranes at or after 37 weeks |
| PPROM | Preterm Prelabor Rupture of Membranes; rupture before 37 weeks, high infection risk |
| fFN | Fetal Fibronectin; negative result strongly predicts NOT delivering within two weeks |
| Cervical length | Transvaginal ultrasound measure; short cervix raises spontaneous preterm-birth risk |
| ACS / ANCS | Antenatal Corticosteroids (betamethasone/dexamethasone); accelerate fetal lung maturity |
| RDS | Respiratory Distress Syndrome; surfactant-deficient preterm lung disease ACS helps prevent |
| Nifedipine (tocolytic) | Calcium channel blocker used to delay preterm birth long enough for steroids |
| Indomethacin (tocolytic) | NSAID tocolytic limited to before 32 weeks and short duration |
| Chorioamnionitis | Intraamniotic infection; maternal fever, tachycardia, uterine tenderness → needs delivery |
| Mg neuroprotection | Magnesium sulfate before ~32 weeks reduces cerebral palsy in the preterm neonate |

## 09 NGN UNFOLDING CASE — Preeclampsia Progressing to HELLP

**Grace, 34, G1 P0, at 35 weeks**, admitted with elevated blood pressure.

**Scene 1 — Recognize Cues:** BP 158/104, reports headache and RUQ discomfort, platelets 130,000, AST 40, P:C ratio 0.5. *BP ≥140/90 after 20 weeks + proteinuria + headache/RUQ = preeclampsia.*

**Scene 2 — Analyze Cues (6 hours later):** Platelets 88,000; AST 160; LDH 700; haptoglobin low; BP 166/112. *Falling platelets, rising transaminases, and hemolysis markers = progression to HELLP. A single platelet value can mislead; the falling/rishing trajectory is the alarm.*

**Scene 3 — Prioritize:** Evolving diagnosis = **preeclampsia with severe features / HELLP**. Priority interventions = **seizure prophylaxis (magnesium) and blood pressure control with delivery planning**.

**Scene 4 — Generate Solutions (3 correct):** ✅ Begin magnesium sulfate for seizure prophylaxis. ✅ Give antihypertensive (labetalol, hydralazine, or nifedipine) for severe-range BP. ✅ Prepare for delivery planning and give corticosteroids if indicated. ❌ Use magnesium to lower BP (it's not an antihypertensive!). ❌ Discharge home. ❌ Withhold treatment until 24-hour urine is complete.

**Scene 5 — Take Actions (SATA):** ✅ Keep calcium gluconate at the bedside. ✅ Monitor DTRs, respirations, urine output. ✅ Assess for pulmonary edema and continuous fetal monitoring. ✅ Reassess BP and treat severe-range values promptly.

**Scene 6 — Evaluate:** Day 1 post-delivery: platelets 110,000, AST 90 → Day 2: platelets 150,000, AST 45; BP improving; reflexes 2+, respirations 16. **Evidence of HELLP resolving after delivery.**

**Debrief — three decision points:**
- **Expecting magnesium to lower BP** — use separate antihypertensives.
- **Waiting for proteinuria quantification** — severe features justify acting NOW.
- **Missing the HELLP trend** — falling platelets + rising enzymes = alarm.

## 10 NGN UNFOLDING CASE — PPROM at 30 Weeks

**Leah, 27, G2 P1, at 30 weeks**, reports a sudden gush of clear fluid.

**Recognize Cues:** Gush of clear fluid; nitrazine turns blue; ferning seen; temp 37.0°C; no contractions; FHR 145 moderate variability. *Positive nitrazine + ferning + gush at 30 weeks = PPROM.*

**Management (expectant):** ✅ Administer antenatal corticosteroids. ✅ Give latency antibiotics. ✅ Provide MgSO4 for neuroprotection if birth imminent (<32 weeks). ✅ Minimize digital vaginal exams. ❌ Frequent digital cervical exams. ❌ Induce labor immediately (before 34 weeks, prematurity risk usually outweighs waiting — unless infection appears).

**Infection watch:** Day 5 develops temp 38.4°C, maternal HR 108, fetal baseline 170, uterine tenderness → chorioamnionitis → expectant management ends, deliver.

**Debrief:** Doing digital exams "to check" adds infection risk. Latency ends the moment infection appears. Remember Mg neuroprotection <32 weeks.

## 12 ANSWER KEY & RATIONALE

| Q | Ans | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | A | Severe features (BP ≥160/110, headache, visual changes, epigastric pain, hyperreflexia) signal impending eclampsia; magnesium sulfate prevents seizures. |
| 2 | B | HELLP = ↓ platelets, ↑ AST/ALT, ↑ LDH (hemolysis, liver injury, thrombocytopenia). |
| 3 | B | RR <12 and absent DTRs = magnesium toxicity; give calcium gluconate (the antidote). |
| 4 | B | GDM is diagnosed when ANY OGTT value meets threshold: fasting ≥92 mg/dL (96 meets it). |
| 5 | A | Betamethasone matures fetal lungs (surfactant) and reduces RDS and IVH in a preterm newborn. |
| 6 | C | With PROM/PPROM, a digital vaginal exam is contraindicated (infection risk); use a sterile speculum examination. |
