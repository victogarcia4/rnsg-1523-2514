## SLIDE 04 — BLEEDING EARLY vs LATE
"Bleeding at any point in pregnancy is potentially life-threatening. The biggest single cause of maternal death worldwide is obstetric hemorrhage. Early bleeding, before 20 weeks, includes spontaneous abortion, ectopic pregnancy, gestational trophoblastic disease, and cervical insufficiency. Late bleeding, after 20 weeks, includes placenta previa, placental abruption, and placenta accreta spectrum. Management is always recognition, assessment, and resuscitation."

---

## SLIDE 05 — SPONTANEOUS ABORTION
"Spontaneous abortion, or miscarriage, is the loss of a pregnancy before 20 weeks from natural causes. It is the most common complication of early pregnancy, affecting up to thirty-one percent of first-trimester pregnancies. There are six categories: threatened, inevitable, incomplete, complete, missed, and septic. Loss after 20 weeks is a stillbirth."

---

## SLIDE 06 — ABORTION CATEGORIES
"Here are the categories. Threatened abortion has bleeding present but the cervix is closed — pregnancy may continue. Inevitable abortion has bleeding and a dilated cervix — loss cannot be stopped. Incomplete abortion has bleeding with tissue and a dilated cervix — products are retained and need suction curettage. Complete abortion has decreased bleeding and a closed cervix — all products passed. Missed abortion has absent or brown bleeding and a closed cervix — the fetus died but is retained. Septic abortion has bleeding with fever and a dilated cervix — infection of retained tissue, an emergency."

---

## SLIDE 07 — ABORTION NURSING PRIORITIES
"Nursing priorities for abortion. Assess the color, amount, and presence of clots or tissue. Have the patient save any tissue passed. Monitor vital signs and watch for shock. Prepare for suction curettage, dilation and curettage, or misoprostol. Administer RhoGAM within 72 hours if the patient is Rh-negative and not sensitized. And critically, reassure the family that their actions did not cause the loss — most spontaneous abortions result from a fetal abnormality."

---

## SLIDE 08 — ECTOPIC PREGNANCY
"Ectopic pregnancy is implantation outside the uterus, ninety-six percent in the fallopian tube. No other site can accommodate a growing embryo, so rupture and massive hemorrhage are the risks. Risk factors include previous ectopic, PID, IUD use, and smoking. Treatment for a stable non-ruptured ectopic is single-dose IM methotrexate. A ruptured ectopic requires surgery, typically salpingectomy."

---

## SLIDE 09 — TAKE NOTE: ECTOPIC RUPTURE
"Take note. The hallmark of ectopic pregnancy is abdominal pain with spotting six to eight weeks after a missed period. Shoulder pain is referred pain from intra-abdominal blood and suggests rupture — a surgical emergency. Hypotension and tachycardia indicate shock. Do not miss this presentation."

---

## SLIDE 10 — GTD MOLAR PREGNANCY
"Gestational trophoblastic disease is abnormal overgrowth of trophoblastic tissue. The pregnancy is not viable. The tissue produces hCG, which serves as the clinical marker. A hydatidiform mole shows chorionic villi degenerating into grape-like vesicles. Complete mole is an empty egg fertilized by sperm, producing 46 all-paternal chromosomes. Partial mole is triploid with 69 chromosomes. Findings include vaginal bleeding, a uterus larger than dates, hyperemesis, and preeclampsia before 20 weeks, which is a red flag."

---

## SLIDE 11 — GTD MANAGEMENT
"Management is evacuation by dilation and curettage or hysterectomy, followed by serial beta-hCG measurements until non-detectable. Reliable contraception is needed for six to twelve months, because pregnancy would hide the rising hCG used to detect malignancy. Methotrexate chemotherapy is given if indicated."

---

## SLIDE 12 — CERVICAL INSUFFICIENCY
"Cervical insufficiency is painless cervical dilation in the second trimester leading to expulsion of an immature fetus. Risk factors include prior cervical trauma or surgery, a second-trimester loss, and DES exposure. Findings are pink-tinged discharge, pelvic pressure, and painless dilation. Management is cerclage, a purse-string suture reinforcing the internal os, placed in the second trimester."

---

## SLIDE 13 — PREVIA vs ABRUPTION
"Placenta previa and placental abruption both cause late bleeding but are managed very differently. Previa has painless, bright-red bleeding with a soft uterus and usually normal fetal heart rate. Abruption has painful, dark bleeding with a rigid, tender uterus and fetal distress or absent heart rate. Previa onset is insidious; abruption is sudden."

---

## SLIDE 14 — PLACENTA PREVIA KEY POINTS
"Placenta previa means the placenta covers or is near the internal cervical os. Risk factors include prior previa, prior cesarean, and multiparity. The critical rule: never perform a vaginal exam, because it can disrupt the placenta and cause hemorrhage. Care includes IV access, type and crossmatch, fetal monitoring, bed rest, and planned cesarean birth."

---

## SLIDE 15 — PLACENTAL ABRUPTION KEY POINTS
"Placental abruption is premature separation of a normally implanted placenta after 20 weeks. Risk factors include hypertension, trauma, and cocaine use. Emergency care includes two large-bore IVs, type and crossmatch, oxygen, and continuous fetal monitoring. Cesarean is performed if fetal distress occurs. The major complication is DIC. Watch for bleeding gums, oozing at the IV site, and petechiae."

---

## SLIDE 16 — TAKE NOTE: BLOOD LOSS
"Take note of this critical point. In pregnancy, vital signs can look normal even with thirty to forty percent blood loss, because the expanded blood volume masks the deficit. Do not wait for hypotension to recognize hemorrhage. Act on the bleeding and the risk, not on the vital signs alone."

---

## SLIDE 17 — DIC
"Disseminated intravascular coagulation is widespread clotting that uses up clotting factors, after which bleeding occurs. Obstetric triggers include abruption, amniotic fluid embolism, retained dead fetus, sepsis, molar pregnancy, and HELLP. The laboratory picture is decreased platelets, decreased fibrinogen, elevated PT and aPTT, and positive D-dimer. Treatment is treating the cause and transfusing PRBCs, FFP, cryoprecipitate, and platelets."

---

## SLIDE 18 — ABORTION MEDICATIONS
"Abortion-related medications. Misoprostol stimulates uterine contractions to terminate pregnancy or evacuate retained products. Mifepristone is a progesterone antagonist given before misoprostol. Dinoprostone, prostaglandin E2, stimulates contractions for second-trimester loss. RhoGAM prevents isoimmunization in Rh-negative, non-sensitized patients."

---

## SLIDE 19 — HYPEREMESIS GRAVIDARUM
"Hyperemesis gravidarum is severe, persistent, uncontrollable nausea and vomiting beginning around five to six weeks. It causes weight loss greater than five percent of prepregnancy weight, dehydration, ketosis, electrolyte imbalance, and ketonuria. The cause is elevated hCG and estrogen with a relaxed lower esophageal sphincter. Risk factors include molar pregnancy and multiple gestation."

---

## SLIDE 20 — HYPEREMESIS MANAGEMENT
"Management is step by step. Step one, stop all oral intake to rest the gut. Step two, rehydrate with IV normal saline plus vitamin B6 and electrolytes. Step three, control nausea with antiemetics such as ondansetron or Diclégis. Step four, gradually reintroduce oral intake. Step five, escalate to TPN or PEG feeding if there is no improvement."

---

## SLIDE 21 — TAKE NOTE: NPO FIRST
"Take note. The priority in hyperemesis is to stop all oral intake until vomiting stops, then rehydrate. Do not force fluids by mouth — it prolongs vomiting. This is a commonly tested point."

---

## SLIDE 22 — PPH 4 Ts
"Postpartum hemorrhage is cumulative blood loss of 1,000 milliliters or more, or loss with hypovolemia, within 24 hours. The four Ts organize the causes. Tone is uterine atony, the most common cause, treated with massage and oxytocin. Trauma is laceration, treated by repair. Tissue is retained placenta, treated by evacuation. Thrombin is coagulopathy, treated by correcting clotting factors."

---

## SLIDE 23 — UTEROTONIC DRUG CARD
"The uterotonic drug card. Oxytocin is first-line. Methylergonovine is contraindicated in hypertension. Carboprost is contraindicated in asthma. Misoprostol is often usable when others are contraindicated. Tranexamic acid is given within three hours as an adjunct. These contraindications — methylergonovine in hypertension and carboprost in asthma — are classic exam questions."

---

## SLIDE 24 — KEY TERMS HEMORRHAGE
"Key terminology. APH is antepartum hemorrhage. PPH is postpartum hemorrhage. GTD is gestational trophoblastic disease. DIC is disseminated intravascular coagulation. TXA is tranexamic acid. QBL is quantitative blood loss, which is preferred over visual estimation. EPL is early pregnancy loss before 13 weeks."

---

## SLIDE 25 — NGN CASE PPH
"An NGN case. Denise is 29, G3 P3, twenty minutes after vaginal birth of a 4,000-gram infant. The fundus is boggy, two centimeters above the umbilicus, and deviated to the right. There is a steady trickle of bright-red blood, the pad is saturated in ten minutes, and the heart rate is 104. The likely cause is uterine atony — a boggy, displaced fundus with bleeding. A full bladder worsens it."

---

## SLIDE 26 — CASE PRIORITY ACTIONS
"The priority actions. Perform firm fundal massage. Empty the bladder, which is displacing the fundus and worsening atony. Administer oxytocin and quantify blood loss. If bleeding continues, escalate with two large-bore IVs, a second-line uterotonic such as carboprost if no asthma, and prepare for transfusion. Remember, a full bladder both displaces the fundus and worsens atony."

## SLIDE 33 — EXIT TICKET
"Exit ticket questions. Previa bleeding is described as painless and bright red. The most common cause of PPH is uterine atony, the Tone of the four Ts. The uterotonic to avoid in asthma is carboprost. With undiagnosed late-pregnancy bleeding, never perform a digital vaginal exam. And for a boggy fundus with bleeding, the first actions are massage, oxytocin, and emptying the bladder."

Question two: B — shoulder pain with hypotension and tachycardia signals rupture from intra-abdominal blood. Question three: B — serial beta-hCG detects residual tissue, and contraception prevents pregnancy from masking rising hCG."

## SLIDE 36 — QUICK RECALL PART 1
"Quick recall. Spontaneous abortion is loss before 20 weeks — save tissue and give RhoGAM if Rh-negative. Ectopic pregnancy presents with pain and spotting six to eight weeks after a missed period, with shoulder pain indicating rupture. Molar pregnancy has a uterus larger than dates, requires hCG tracking, and no pregnancy for six to twelve months. Cervical insufficiency is painless dilation treated with cerclage."

---

## SLIDE 37 — QUICK RECALL PART 2
"Placenta previa is painless bright-red bleeding with a soft uterus, and never do a vaginal exam. Placental abruption is painful dark bleeding with a rigid uterus and fetal distress — watch for DIC. DIC labs show decreased platelets, decreased fibrinogen, elevated PT and aPTT, and positive D-dimer. Hyperemesis is nausea and vomiting with greater than five percent weight loss — NPO first, then IV fluids."

---

## SLIDE 38 — QUICK RECALL PART 3
"The four Ts of postpartum hemorrhage are Tone, Trauma, Tissue, and Thrombin. Uterine atony is the most common cause. Methylergonovine is contraindicated in hypertension. Carboprost is contraindicated in asthma. Tranexamic acid is given within three hours. Quantitative blood loss beats visual estimation. And vital signs can hide thirty to forty percent loss."
