## 02 1. BIRTH INJURIES

Birth injury = impairment of newborn body function/structure from adverse events during childbirth (~2–3% of births). Most resolve spontaneously; cesarean births have decreased the incidence.

| INJURY | DESCRIPTION | MANAGEMENT |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Midclavicular fracture** | Most common fracture; associated with shoulder dystocia, macrosomia | Arm immobilized (sleeve pinned to shirt); heals in 2–4 weeks |
| **Erb palsy** | Upper brachial plexus injury; arm adducted, internally rotated, absent Moro/bicep reflexes, **grasp reflex present** | Immobilize upper arm across abdomen for 1 week; gentle passive ROM exercises |
| **Klumpke palsy** | Lower brachial plexus injury; **absent grasp reflex**, hand/wrist weakness | Hand in neutral position; passive ROM exercises |
| **Facial nerve palsy** | Forceps or in-utero pressure; face asymmetric when crying, eye persistently open on affected side | Usually resolves in 1 week to months; eye protection (patches/tears); support feeding |
| **Cephalohematoma** | Subperiosteal blood, **does NOT cross suture lines** | Resolves over 2–3 weeks without treatment |
| **Caput succedaneum** | Soft-tissue edema, **crosses suture lines**, associated with molding | Resolves in first few days without treatment |

> **■ TAKE NOTE! Cephalohematoma stays within suture lines. Caput succedaneum crosses suture lines. Both resolve on their own.**

## 03 2. HYPERBILIRUBINEMIA (JAUNDICE)

**Definition:** total serum bilirubin (TSB) >5 mg/dL. ~80% of newborns appear jaundiced. Caused by breakdown of RBCs (short-lived fetal hemoglobin).

Unconjugated (indirect) = fat-soluble, **toxic**. Conjugated (direct) = water-soluble, nontoxic. Newborn liver must conjugate bilirubin on its own after birth.

| FEATURE | PHYSIOLOGIC (BENIGN) | PATHOLOGIC (SIGNIFICANT) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Onset | After 24 hours of age | **Within first 24 hours of age** |
| Peak | Day 3–4 | Variable |
| TSB level | 8–14 mg/dL peak | >95th percentile; rising **>5 mg/dL/day** or **>0.2 mg/dL/h** |
| Treatment | Early frequent feedings, reassurance | **Phototherapy**; exchange transfusion if phototherapy fails |
| Danger | Rare | Severe TSB **≥25** → BIND/kernicterus (bilirubin crosses blood-brain barrier → irreversible brain damage) |

### Phototherapy

- **Mechanism:** blue LED light converts bilirubin into water-soluble isomers excreted via bile/urine (without conjugation).
- **Nursing:** expose maximum skin • **cover eyes and genitals** • **turn every 2 h** • remove from lights only for feedings • monitor temp every 4 h • watch for dehydration • stools become loose and green.
- **At home:** lights 12–30 in above newborn; eye patches; keep skin clean/dry.

> **■ TAKE NOTE! Jaundice before 24 hours of age = pathologic until proven otherwise** — report immediately. Do not rely on visual estimate; use TCB or TSB.

## 04 3. INFANT OF A DIABETIC MOTHER (IDM)

**Definition:** newborn of a person with pregestational (type 1/2) or gestational diabetes. Up to 90% of diabetic pregnancies are GDM.

**Cause:** maternal hyperglycemia → fetal hyperinsulinism → excessive growth (or growth restriction if vascular compromise).

### Key Problems & Critical Lab Values

| PROBLEM | MECHANISM | LAB VALUE |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Hypoglycemia** | Abrupt cessation of maternal glucose + ongoing fetal insulin | **<50 mg/dL (AAP: <40)** |
| Macrosomia | Fetal hyperinsulinism acts as growth factor | >4,000 g (>90th percentile) |
| RDS | High insulin inhibits surfactant production | Assess respiratory distress |
| Hypocalcemia | Abrupt cessation of maternal calcium transfer | <7 mg/dL |
| Hypomagnesemia | Related to maternal levels before birth | <1.5 mg/dL |
| Polycythemia | Increased erythropoiesis from intrauterine hypoxia | Venous hematocrit >65% |
| Hyperbilirubinemia | Excessive RBC hemolysis + bruising from macrosomia | >12 mg/dL (term) |

### Priority Nursing Actions

- **Blood glucose hourly for first 4 hours**, then every 3–4 h. **<40 mg/dL = hypoglycemia.**
- **Early feeding** (breast milk or formula) every 2–3 h; if unstable, IV dextrose. **Do not withhold feeding.** Feed-then-recheck with escalation is standard.
- **Neutral thermal environment** — cold stress increases glucose demand.
- Monitor for respiratory distress (RDS/TTN), calcium/magnesium, bilirubin, hematocrit.
- Look for birth trauma (shoulder dystocia, fractures, brachial plexus).

> **■ TAKE NOTE! Screen the at-risk infant early; a low value in an "asymptomatic" infant still needs action.** The mother's intrapartum glucose control directly predicts newborn hypoglycemia.

## 05 4. NEONATAL SEPSIS

**Definition:** bloodstream infection within first 28 days. Mortality rate ~3%. **Early-onset** = vertical transmission (intrapartum). **Late-onset** = horizontal (nursery).

**Most common organisms:** Group B Streptococcus (GBS) and E. coli.

**Risk factors:** maternal GBS colonization • ROM >18 h • intrapartum fever • chorioamnionitis • prematurity • low birth weight.

**Signs (nonspecific):** temperature instability • poor feeding • lethargy • irritability • respiratory distress • **"doesn't look right."**

**Diagnosis:** CBC, CRP, blood/CSF/urine cultures. **Start antibiotics before cultures result.**

> **■ TAKE NOTE! Neonatal sepsis often presents with nonspecific signs.** If the newborn "doesn't look right," investigate.

## 06 5. COMPROMISED NEWBORN RESPIRATORY CONDITIONS

| CONDITION | ONSET / KEY FEATURE | PATHOPHYSIOLOGY | MANAGEMENT |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Perinatal asphyxia** | At birth; apnea, cyanosis, limp, bradycardia | Impaired gas exchange → hypoxemia + hypercapnia + metabolic acidosis | **Resuscitate: ABC** (airway, breathing, circulation); dry/stimulate; PPV; O2; warm |
| **TTN** | Within a few hours; **tachypnea >60**; risk: cesarean, macrosomia | Delayed clearance of fetal lung fluid → pulmonary edema | Self-limiting (1–2 days); supportive: O2, IV fluids/gavage feed, neutral thermal environment |
| **RDS** | Within hours; tachypnea, **grunting, retractions**, "**ground-glass**" chest x-ray | **Surfactant deficiency** → alveolar collapse (atelectasis), stiff lungs | **Exogenous surfactant**; CPAP/mechanical ventilation; term/preterm, IDM, cesarean without labor |
| **MAS** | Term/postterm; **meconium-stained fluid**; respiratory distress after birth | Meconium aspirated into lungs → airway obstruction, chemical pneumonitis, ball-valve effect | Tracheal suctioning before first breath (if non-vigorous); O2; CPAP/ventilation; antibiotics if indicated |

> **■ TAKE NOTE! RDS = surfactant deficiency** (preterm, IDM). **TTN = retained fluid** (cesarean, fast labor). **MAS = meconium** (term/postterm, distress).

## 07 6. VEAL CHOP — FHR PATTERN INTERPRETATION

| V | Variable decelerations | C | **C**ord compression | Action: reposition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| E | Early decelerations | H | **H**ead compression | Benign (mirrors contractions) |
| A | Accelerations | O | **O**K | Reassuring |
| L | Late decelerations | P | **P**lacental insufficiency | **Concerning — act!** |

### FHR Category → Action Algorithm

| Category | Definition | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Category I** | Normal tracing — baseline 110–160, moderate variability, no late/variable decels | Routine monitoring |
| **Category II** | Indeterminate — everything not I or III | **Intrauterine resuscitation**, evaluate; NOT immediate delivery |
| **Category III** | Abnormal — absent variability + recurrent decels/bradycardia | **Expedite delivery** |

**Intrauterine Resuscitation = Reposition** (left lateral) + **IV fluids** + **reduce uterotonics** + **treat hypotension**.

> **Routine maternal oxygen is NOT recommended for Category II/III when maternal SpO₂ is adequate** (ACOG 2025 reversal — supersedes older guidance). Only give O₂ if maternal hypoxia is present.

## 08 7. NEWBORN RESUSCITATION PRIORITIES (NRP / ABC)

- **Dry, stimulate, warm.** If no response → active resuscitation.
- **Airway:** position, suction (mouth then nose).
- **Breathing:** **positive-pressure ventilation (PPV)** — the single most important NRP step for a non-breathing newborn.
- **Circulation:** chest compressions if HR <60 after adequate ventilation.
- **Goal:** HR >100 bpm, spontaneous breathing, pink tongue (good brain oxygenation).

### Special: Non-Vigorous Meconium-Stained Newborn

- **Tracheal suctioning + PPV under radiant warmer.** Do NOT suction before birth (routine intrapartum suctioning no longer recommended).
- Therapeutic hypothermia: neonatal cooling within 6 hours to limit brain injury after significant hypoxia (HIE).

## 09 8. FETAL DEMISE & PERINATAL LOSS

**Perinatal loss:** any pregnancy loss and/or neonatal death up to 1 month of age. Prevalence ~5.5 per 1,000 births.

- **Miscarriage:** fetal death before 20 weeks.
- **Stillbirth / IUFD:** fetal death after 20 weeks — confirmed by ultrasound-confirmed absent cardiac activity.
- **Neonatal death:** death within first 28 days.

**Causes:** placental abnormalities • diabetes • hypertension • infection • trauma • congenital anomalies • often unexplained.

### Grief Support & Nursing Care

- **Confirm demise:** inability to obtain fetal heart sounds; ultrasound confirms absent cardiac activity.
- Induction or expectant management (most go into spontaneous labor in 1–2 weeks).
- **Compassionate, clear communication** — use the word "died." Provide a private, unhurried environment.
- **Allow parents to view, touch, hold;** provide privacy. Offer, without pressure, the option to see, hold, name, and create keepsakes.
- **Memory-making:** photographs, hand/footprints, lock of hair, memory box.
- **Honor cultural and spiritual practices.** Include the partner; validate grief — each person grieves differently.
- **Monitor maternal safety** — prolonged fetal retention risks **coagulopathy** (DIC).
- Refer: SHARE Pregnancy & Infant Loss Support; bereavement counselor; community resources.
- Follow-up: send card from nursing staff; attend funeral if appropriate; remember at anniversaries.

> **■ TAKE NOTE! Grief has no timeline.** "Meeting the family where they are" and listening without judgment are the most important nursing interventions. **Never minimize the loss** — "You can have another" invalidates grief. Acknowledge this baby. Follow the family's lead; support meaning-making rather than steering grief.

## 10 KEY TERMINOLOGY — DAY 4 MASTER DECK

**Surveillance / Neonatal**

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| EFM | Electronic Fetal Monitoring; continuous fetal heart rate and contraction tracing |
| Category I FHR | Normal tracing; routine monitoring, no intervention needed |
| Category II FHR | Indeterminate tracing; requires evaluation and intrauterine resuscitation, NOT immediate delivery |
| Category III FHR | Abnormal tracing (absent variability with recurrent decels or bradycardia); expedite delivery |
| Intrauterine resuscitation | Reposition, IV fluids, reduce uterotonics, treat hypotension — restore fetal oxygenation |
| Routine O₂ reversal | ACOG 2025 advises against routine maternal O₂ for Category II/III without maternal hypoxia |
| FGR | Fetal Growth Restriction; estimated weight or abdominal circumference below the 10th percentile |
| Umbilical artery Doppler | Absent or reversed end-diastolic flow signals severe FGR |
| NRP | Neonatal Resuscitation Program; the AAP/AHA algorithm for newborn resuscitation |
| PPV | Positive Pressure Ventilation; the single most important NRP step for a non-breathing newborn |
| HIE | Hypoxic-Ischemic Encephalopathy; brain injury from perinatal oxygen deprivation |
| Therapeutic hypothermia | Neonatal cooling within 6 hours to limit brain injury after significant hypoxia |
| MAS | Meconium Aspiration Syndrome; meconium below the cords causing respiratory distress |
| TTN | Transient Tachypnea of the Newborn; self-limited from retained fetal lung fluid |
| RDS | Respiratory Distress Syndrome; surfactant-deficient preterm lung disease |
| NAS / NOWS | Neonatal Abstinence / Opioid Withdrawal Syndrome; irritability, poor feeding, high-pitched cry |
| IA | Intermittent auscultation; periodic fetal heart listening for low-risk labor |
| Cord blood gas | Documents fetal acid-base status at birth |
| IUFD | Intrauterine Fetal Demise; fetal death before birth, confirmed by absent cardiac activity |
| BIND / Kernicterus | TSB ≥25 → crosses blood-brain barrier → irreversible brain damage |

**Discrimination pearls:**

| Pair | Distinction |
| --- | --- |
| Category I/II/III | I = normal, no action; II = indeterminate, resuscitate and evaluate; III = abnormal, expedite delivery |
| Early vs Late decels | Early mirror contractions (head compression, benign); late lag behind (placental insufficiency, concerning) |
| Variable vs Late decels | Variable are abrupt and vary (cord compression); late are gradual and delayed (placental insufficiency) |
| PROM vs PPROM | PROM is rupture at ≥37 weeks; PPROM is rupture before 37 weeks with higher infection risk |

## 11 NGN UNFOLDING CASE — GDM in Labor and Neonatal Hypoglycemia

**Ana, 33, G2 P1, at 39 weeks** with medication-requiring gestational diabetes (class A2), now in labor. Fetus estimated large for gestational age.

**Scene 1 — Recognize Cues:** Class A2 GDM on insulin; intrapartum glucose 150 mg/dL; EFW >90th percentile. *Maternal hyperglycemia in labor + LGA fetus = maternal glucose drives fetal hyperinsulinism → sets up newborn hypoglycemia after cord clamping.*

**After birth (hour 1):** Jitteriness/tremors, poor feeding, lethargy, point-of-care glucose **32 mg/dL**. *Consistent with hypoglycemia in an IDM.*

**Scene 3 — Prioritize:** Priority for this LGA IDM = **early glucose screening**. Risk = **neonatal hypoglycemia from fetal hyperinsulinism**. Anticipate and screen; do not wait for florid symptoms.

**Scene 4 — Generate Solutions (3 correct):** ✅ Initiate early feeding (breast or formula) per protocol. ✅ Check POC blood glucose on schedule. ✅ Treat confirmed hypoglycemia per protocol and recheck. ❌ Withhold feeding for several hours. ❌ Ignore glucose of 32 in an asymptomatic infant.

**Scene 5 — Take Actions (in order):** Recognize low glucose + symptoms → Feed the infant (or give ordered dextrose if unable to feed) → Recheck glucose after intervention → Escalate to provider if glucose remains low → Document.

**Scene 6 — Evaluate (trend):** hour 1: 32 mg/dL (fed) → hour 2: 46 mg/dL → hour 4: 58 mg/dL, feeding well, no tremors. Glucose is rising into acceptable range with feeding — hypoglycemia is resolving and the plan is working.

**Debrief — three decision points:**
- **Connecting maternal glucose to the newborn** — intrapartum control directly predicts newborn hypoglycemia.
- **Waiting for symptoms** — screen the at-risk infant early; low value in "asymptomatic" still needs action.
- **Delaying feeding** — early feeding is first-line prevention and treatment; do not withhold.

## 12 NGN UNFOLDING CASE — Intrauterine Fetal Demise and Bereavement

**Sofía, 30, G2 P1, at 36 weeks**, presents reporting absent fetal movement for a day.

**Scene 1 — Recognize Cues:** No fetal movement for ~24 hours; no fetal heart tones on Doppler; ultrasound confirms absent cardiac activity; patient tearful. *Ultrasound-confirmed absent cardiac activity establishes IUFD; the psychosocial need is now central.*

**Scene 2 — Analyze Cues (priority):** Compassionate, clear communication → Priority now. Assessing maternal coagulation status → Priority now (medical safety). Offering choices for seeing/holding → Priority now. Detailed contraception counseling → Later/secondary.

**Scene 3 — Prioritize:** Nursing priority = **compassionate bereavement care** while ensuring **maternal physical safety, including monitoring for coagulopathy** during delivery planning. Bereavement care and maternal safety run in parallel.

**Scene 4 — Generate Solutions (3 correct):** ✅ Offer (without pressure) to see, hold, name, and create keepsakes. ✅ Ask about and honor cultural and spiritual practices. ✅ Provide clear information about the birth process and choices. ❌ Tell her "you can have another baby." ❌ Discourage seeing the infant to "protect" her. ❌ Rush decisions.

**Scene 5 — Take Actions (SATA):** ✅ Provide a private, unhurried environment. ✅ Offer bereavement resources and spiritual care. ✅ Monitor maternal vital signs and labs. ✅ Document family's wishes and care provided.

**Scene 6 — Evaluate (over the stay):** Admission: acute shock; after support — family creates keepsakes, involves clergy; before discharge — family verbalizes feeling respected, has follow-up appointments. Care honored the family's wishes, maintained maternal safety, and connected them to follow-up.

**Debrief — three decision points most often missed:**
- **Minimizing the loss** — "You can have another" invalidates grief; acknowledge this baby.
- **Deciding for the family** — seeing/holding are offered, never imposed or withheld.
- **Forgetting maternal safety** — prolonged fetal retention risks coagulopathy; monitoring continues amid emotional care.

- B. Respiratory distress syndrome (RDS)
- C. Transient tachypnea of the newborn (TTN)

- A. Begin phototherapy

- A. Avoid mentioning the baby to prevent sadness
- B. Encourage viewing and holding the newborn; provide a memory box
- C. Tell them they can try again soon
- D. Explain that the cause is usually the parent's fault

**Q7.** Using VEAL CHOP, late decelerations indicate which fetal condition?

- A. Cord compression
- B. Head compression
- C. Placental insufficiency

**Q8.** A Category II tracing shows recurrent late decelerations but maternal SpO₂ is 99%. Which action is appropriate per current guidance?

- A. Apply routine maternal oxygen by mask
- B. Perform intrauterine resuscitation without routine oxygen
- C. Immediate cesarean delivery
- D. No action needed

### Exit-Ticket Add-Ons (Day 4)

- First action in cord prolapse? → **Lift presenting part off the cord (hand stays)**
- VEAL CHOP: Late decelerations → cause? → **Placental insufficiency**
- Priority NRP step for a non-breathing newborn? → **Positive pressure ventilation (PPV)**
- Category II, recurrent late decels, maternal SpO₂ 99% — O₂ by mask? → **No — routine O₂ not recommended; do intrauterine resuscitation**
- TOLAC: sudden tearing pain, loss of station, bradycardia — concern + action? → **Uterine rupture → stop oxytocin, emergency cesarean**
