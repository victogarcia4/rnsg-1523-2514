## 02 1. DYSTOCIA — THE 5 Ps OF ABNORMAL LABOR

**Dystocia = abnormal progression of labor**; accounts for ~50% of unplanned cesarean births in nulliparous patients. Slow or stalled cervical dilation, descent, or contractions.

### The Five Contributing Factors (5 Ps)

| FACTOR | EXAMPLES |
| --- | --- |
| **POWER** (Expulsive forces) | Hypertonic dysfunction (latent-phase, uncoordinated, painful) • Hypotonic dysfunction (active-phase, weak/fading contractions) • Precipitous labor (<3 h) |
| **PASSENGER** (Fetus) | Malpresentation: breech, face/brow, persistent occiput posterior • Macrosomia (>4,000 g) • Shoulder dystocia • Multifetal pregnancy |
| **PASSAGE** (Pelvis & canal) | Contracted pelvic inlet/midpelvis/outlet • Soft-tissue obstruction (full bladder, cervical swelling) • Gynecoid pelvis = most favorable |
| **POSITION** (Maternal) | Upright/ambulation promotes progress; supine may impede descent |
| **PSYCHE** (Maternal stress) | Fear/anxiety → catecholamines → myometrial dysfunction • Increases pain perception, reduces uterine contractility |

### Hypertonic vs Hypotonic Uterine Dysfunction

| FEATURE | HYPERTONIC | HYPOTONIC |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Phase | **Latent** phase (cervix <4 cm) | **Active** phase |
| Contraction pattern | Uncoordinated; high resting tone; **painful** | Coordinated but **weak**; frequency & intensity decrease |
| Pain | Increased (uterine anoxia) | Less severe |
| Management | **Therapeutic rest + sedation**; rule out abruption | **Amniotomy + oxytocin augmentation**; rule out CPD |

> **Memory hook:** Hypertonic in latent phase = **R-E-S-T**. Hypotonic in active phase = **augment with oxytocin**.

### Shoulder Dystocia

**Definition:** fetal head delivers but the shoulders are wedged behind the pubic bone — obstetric emergency. The chest cannot expand; cord compression between fetal body and maternal pelvis.

**Risk:** macrosomia • maternal diabetes • obesity • postdates • prior shoulder dystocia.

**Emergency response:** **McRoberts maneuver** (hyperflex thighs to abdomen) + **suprapubic pressure** (NOT fundal). Effective in >50% of cases. Also: hands-and-knees, squatting, lateral recumbent.

**Avoid:** fundal pressure • strong lateral traction • forced head rotation (increase brachial plexus injury).

**After birth:** assess newborn for crepitus, deformity, Erb palsy, bruising; assess parent for hemorrhage, bladder trauma.

> **■ TAKE NOTE! FUNDAL pressure is contraindicated in shoulder dystocia** — it does not free the shoulder and increases the risk of injury. Use **suprapubic pressure**.

## 03 2. LABOR INDUCTION vs AUGMENTATION

| PROCEDURE | DEFINITION | KEY POINT |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Induction** | Stimulating contractions **before** spontaneous labor begins | 25% of U.S. births; requires medical indication |
| **Augmentation** | Enhancing inadequate contractions **after** labor has started | Typically oxytocin; labor already in progress |

### Indications & Contraindications

- **Indications:** postterm/prolonged gestation • PPROM • gestational HTN/preeclampsia • chorioamnionitis • oligohydramnios • placental abruption • FGR • fetal demise • diabetes.
- **Contraindications:** complete placenta previa • transverse fetal lie • prolapsed cord • prior classic uterine incision • active genital herpes • CPD.

### Bishop Score — Cervical Readiness

Score ranges 0–13. **>8 = favorable** (likely to succeed). **<5 = unfavorable** → ripen cervix before induction. Evaluates dilation, effacement, station, consistency, and position.

| PARAMETER | SCORE 0 | SCORE 1 | SCORE 2 | SCORE 3 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Dilation (cm) | Closed | 1–2 | 3–4 | 5–6 |
| Effacement (%) | 0–30 | 40–50 | 60–70 | 80 |
| Station | −3 | −2 | −1, 0 | +1, +2 |
| Consistency | Firm | Medium | Soft | Very soft |
| Position | Posterior | Mid | Anterior | Anterior |

### Methods of Cervical Ripening & Induction

| TYPE | METHOD | NOTES |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pharmacologic | Dinoprostone (Cervidil/Prepidil) • Misoprostol (Cytotec, off-label) • Oxytocin (Pitocin) | Only dinoprostone is FDA-approved for cervical ripening; **misoprostol contraindicated after prior cesarean** (uterine rupture risk) |
| Mechanical | Foley balloon catheter; hygroscopic dilators (Laminaria, Dilapan) | Balloon reduces hyperstimulation risk; dilators expand over 12–24 h |
| Surgical | Amniotomy (AROM); membrane stripping | Risk: cord prolapse, infection, FHR deceleration; amniotic fluid characteristics must be monitored |

## 04 OXYTOCIN (PITOCIN) NURSING

- **Administration:** 10 units in 1,000 mL isotonic solution via IV infusion pump (piggyback). Titrate to contractions every 2–3 min, lasting 40–60 sec.
- **Monitor:** contractions (frequency, duration, intensity, resting tone) • FHR continuously • maternal VS every 1–2 h • I&O (antidiuretic effect) • cervical progress.
- **DISCONTINUE if:** tetanic contractions >90 sec • **uterine hyperstimulation / tachysystole** (contractions <2 min apart) • elevated resting tone • **Category II/III FHR pattern**.
- **Adverse effects:** uterine hyperstimulation → fetal hypoxia • uterine rupture • water intoxication (if rate >20 mU/min in electrolyte-free solution) • hypotension.

> **■ TAKE NOTE! Resting uterine tone above 20 mm Hg compromises uteroplacental perfusion → fetal hypoxia.** Continuous FHR monitoring is mandatory.

### Tachysystole

**Definition:** >5 contractions in 10 minutes averaged over 30 minutes. With a Category II tracing, signals over-stimulation compromising the fetus.

**Action:** **Stop/decrease oxytocin**, reposition (left lateral), give IV fluid bolus → reassess. **Routine maternal oxygen is NOT recommended** for Category II/III when maternal SpO2 is adequate (ACOG 2025 reversal). Remove the correctable cause (excess stimulation) before escalating.

## 05 3. UMBILICAL CORD PROLAPSE

**Definition:** the umbilical cord slips ahead of the presenting part through the cervix — **obstetric emergency.** Complete occlusion = fetal death if not relieved.

**Risk:** fetal malpresentation (breech, transverse) • prematurity • hydramnios • PROM • amniotomy without engaged presenting part • multifetal pregnancy • long cord.

**First sign:** sudden fetal bradycardia or recurrent variable decelerations that worsen after membranes rupture.

### Emergency Response

- **Call for help immediately** — do not leave the patient.
- **Relieve cord compression:** insert sterile gloved hand into vagina and **push the presenting part off the cord and hold it there**. **The hand that finds the prolapse stays to protect perfusion until delivery.**
- **Position change:** modified Sims, Trendelenburg, or knee-chest position.
- **DO NOT** attempt to replace the cord back into the uterus (causes vasospasm and worsens hypoxia).
- Administer oxygen if ordered; continuous FHR monitoring.
- **Prepare for emergency cesarean** if cervix is not fully dilated.

> **■ TAKE NOTE!** When the presenting part does not fully occupy the pelvic inlet, prolapse is more likely. After amniotomy, verify the presenting part is applied to the cervix and engaged.

> **The three things most likely to be tested:** (1) cord prolapse → **relieve compression first, hand stays**; (2) shoulder dystocia → **McRoberts first**; (3) tachysystole → **reduce oxytocin**, do not increase.

## 06 4. UTERINE RUPTURE DURING TOLAC

**Definition:** tearing of the uterine wall — a dual emergency (fetal hypoxia + maternal bleeding).

**TOLAC = Trial of Labor After Cesarean.** VBAC = Vaginal Birth After Cesarean. Main risk = uterine rupture.

### Recognition Triad

| Finding | Significance |
| --- | --- |
| Sudden severe, **tearing** abdominal pain between contractions | Not normal labor — tearing pain with lost station is rupture |
| **Loss of fetal station** (presenting part rises) | Fetus extruded through rupture |
| **Prolonged fetal bradycardia** (FHR 60s–70s) | First and most reliable sign |

> **■ First sign of impending uterine rupture: sudden fetal bradycardia.** Requires immediate surgical delivery.

### Emergency Actions

1. **Stop oxytocin infusion immediately** (stimulation worsens rupture).
2. **Call for help** — notify surgical and neonatal teams.
3. **Prepare for emergency cesarean** — do NOT delay for imaging.
4. Establish large-bore IV access and prepare blood products.
5. Position for optimal perfusion; give oxygen if maternal hypoxia present.

**Debrief:** Tearing pain between contractions with lost station is not normal labor. Continuing/increasing oxytocin is contraindicated. Suspected rupture goes to the OR — do NOT wait for a formal ultrasound.

## 08 NGN UNFOLDING CASE — Dystocia and Failed Induction

**Bianca, 28, G1 P0, at 41 weeks**, admitted for induction of labor. Bishop score 3 on admission (unfavorable).

**Scene 1 — Recognize Cues:** After cervical ripening and oxytocin, the monitor shows 7 contractions in 10 minutes averaged over 30 minutes; FHR shows recurrent late decelerations with minimal variability. *Tachysystole (>5/10 min over 30 min) + recurrent late decels + minimal variability = excessive stimulation compromising the fetus.*

**Scene 2 — Analyze Cues:** 7 contractions/10 min → tachysystole concern; recurrent late decels → Category II concern; minimal variability → Category II concern.

**Scene 3 — Prioritize:** Immediate problem = **oxytocin-induced tachysystole with a Category II tracing**. Priority = **reduce uterine stimulation and perform intrauterine resuscitation**. Remove the correctable cause before escalating.

**Scene 4 — Generate Solutions (3 correct):** ✅ Stop or decrease the oxytocin. ✅ Reposition patient (left lateral) and give IV fluid bolus. ✅ Reassess tracing and notify provider. ❌ Apply routine maternal oxygen despite normal SpO₂ (ACOG 2025: no routine O₂ without maternal hypoxia). ❌ Increase oxytocin to "push through." ❌ Take no action.

**Scene 5 — Take Actions (in order):** Decrease/stop oxytocin → Reposition and IV bolus → Reassess contractions/FHR → Notify provider; prepare for cesarean if no improvement → Document.

**Scene 6 — Evaluate:** After resuscitation: 4 contractions/10 min, no decels, moderate variability. BUT cervix remains 4 cm with no further change over 4 hours despite adequate contractions. → **Labor now meets criteria for arrest** (no cervical change with adequate contractions) → anticipate cesarean. **Evaluate on two fronts: tracing improved (resuscitation worked), while arrest of dilation guides delivery planning.**

**Debrief — three decision points most often missed:**
- **Increasing oxytocin during tachysystole** — the fix is less stimulation, not more.
- **Reaching for routine oxygen** — with normal maternal SpO₂, routine oxygen is no longer recommended.
- **Saying "failure to progress"** — use **labor arrest** with the defined stage and criteria.

## 09 NGN UNFOLDING CASE — Umbilical Cord Prolapse

**Nia, 25, G1 P0, at 39 weeks** in active labor. Fetus at a high station; artificial rupture of membranes just performed.

**Scene 1 — Recognize Cues:** Immediately after rupture, FHR drops to 70 and stays low; nurse palpates a pulsating cord in the vagina; presenting part is high. *Abrupt bradycardia after ROM + palpable pulsating cord + high presenting part = cord prolapse — the fastest emergency in the unit.*

**Scene 2 — Analyze Cues:** High/unengaged presenting part at ROM → raises prolapse risk. Rupture of membranes → raises prolapse risk. Fetal bradycardia after ROM → consequence to act on.

**Scene 3 — Prioritize:** Emergency = **umbilical cord prolapse**. Priority = **relieve cord compression and expedite birth**. Every second of compression risks fetal hypoxia.

**Scene 4 — Generate Solutions (3 correct):** ✅ Manually elevate the presenting part off the cord with a gloved hand. ✅ Reposition to knee-chest or steep Trendelenburg. ✅ Call for help and prepare for immediate cesarean. ❌ Push cord back into uterus. ❌ Increase oxytocin. ❌ Place supine and flat.

**Scene 5 — Take Actions (in order):** Call for help → Lift presenting part off cord → Place in knee-chest/Trendelenburg → Prepare for cesarean → **Keep hand in place during transfer to OR**.

**Scene 6 — Evaluate:** After manual elevation and repositioning: FHR 70 → 120 → 140; delivered by cesarean with Apgar 8/9. Manual elevation restored fetal perfusion — emergency response was effective.

**Debrief — three decision points:**
- **Removing the hand** — it must stay to keep the presenting part off the cord until birth.
- **Handling the cord** — pushing it back causes vasospasm and worsens hypoxia.
- **Wrong position** — supine-flat worsens compression; knee-chest or Trendelenburg offloads the cord.

## 10 NGN UNFOLDING CASE — Uterine Rupture During TOLAC

**Camila, 32, G2 P1, at 40 weeks**, attempting TOLAC after one prior low-transverse cesarean. Oxytocin augmentation running.

**Scene 1 — Recognize Cues:** Sudden severe, tearing abdominal pain reported between contractions; fetal station, previously 0, now higher; FHR drops to 60s–70s (prolonged bradycardia); maternal HR rising, BP falling. *Triad: acute tearing pain + loss of station + prolonged bradycardia during TOLAC = uterine rupture until proven otherwise.*

**Scene 3 — Prioritize:** Emergency = uterine rupture. Priority = **immediate preparation for emergency cesarean** with readiness for neonatal resuscitation and maternal hemorrhage. Rupture is a dual emergency!

**Scene 4 — Generate Solutions (3 correct):** ✅ Stop the oxytocin infusion. ✅ Call for help and prepare for emergency cesarean. ✅ Begin maternal resuscitation (IV fluids, prepare for transfusion). ❌ Increase oxytocin. ❌ Continue expectant management. ❌ Delay surgery for a formal ultrasound.

**Scene 5 — Take Actions (SATA):** ✅ Discontinue oxytocin immediately. ✅ Notify surgical and neonatal teams. ✅ Establish large-bore IV access and prepare blood products. ✅ Position for optimal perfusion; give O2 if maternal hypoxia present.

**Scene 6 — Evaluate:** Minute 0 FHR 65, maternal HR 118, BP 92/54 → minute 10 in OR, delivery accomplished → post-op BP 110/68 after fluids/blood; neonate Apgar 4→8. Rapid recognition and emergency cesarean stabilized both — escalation was timely and effective.
