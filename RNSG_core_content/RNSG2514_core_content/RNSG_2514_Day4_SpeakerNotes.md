## SLIDE 04 — BIRTH INJURIES
"Birth injuries affect two to three percent of births. The midclavicular fracture is the most common and heals in two to four weeks. Erb palsy is an upper brachial plexus injury with the arm adducted and internally rotated, but the grasp reflex is present. Klumpke palsy is a lower plexus injury with an absent grasp. Facial nerve palsy resolves in weeks. Cephalohematoma does not cross suture lines. Caput succedaneum crosses suture lines."

---

## SLIDE 05 — TAKE NOTE: CEPHALOHEMATOMA vs CAPUT
"Take note. Cephalohematoma is subperiosteal blood that does not cross suture lines and resolves in two to three weeks. Caput succedaneum is soft-tissue edema that crosses suture lines and resolves in a few days. Both resolve on their own. This distinction is frequently tested."

---

## SLIDE 06 — HYPERBILIRUBINEMIA
"Hyperbilirubinemia is a total serum bilirubin above five milligrams per deciliter. About eighty percent of newborns appear jaundiced. Physiologic jaundice begins after 24 hours, peaks on day three to four, and is treated with early frequent feedings. Pathologic jaundice begins within the first 24 hours, rises rapidly — more than five milligrams per deciliter per day — and requires phototherapy."

---

## SLIDE 07 — PHOTOTHERAPY
"Phototherapy uses blue LED light to convert bilirubin into water-soluble isomers excreted without conjugation. Nursing care includes exposing maximum skin, covering the eyes and genitals, turning the infant every two hours, monitoring temperature, and watching for dehydration. Severe bilirubin of 25 or above causes kernicterus, irreversible brain damage."

---

## SLIDE 08 — TAKE NOTE: EARLY JAUNDICE
"Take note. Jaundice before 24 hours of age is pathologic until proven otherwise. Report it immediately. Use transcutaneous or serum bilirubin measurement — do not rely on visual estimation of the degree of jaundice."

---

## SLIDE 09 — INFANT OF DIABETIC MOTHER
"The infant of a diabetic mother develops fetal hyperinsulinism from maternal hyperglycemia, causing macrosomia and, after birth, hypoglycemia. The key problems are hypoglycemia, macrosomia, RDS from inhibited surfactant, hypocalcemia, polycythemia, and hyperbilirubinemia."

---

## SLIDE 10 — IDM PRIORITY ACTIONS
"Priority nursing actions for the IDM. Check blood glucose hourly for the first four hours, then every three to four hours. A glucose below 40 is hypoglycemia. Provide early feeding with breast milk or formula, and use IV dextrose if unstable. Maintain a neutral thermal environment because cold stress increases glucose demand. Monitor for RDS, calcium, magnesium, and bilirubin."

---

## SLIDE 11 — NEONATAL SEPSIS
"Neonatal sepsis is a bloodstream infection in the first 28 days, with a mortality rate of about three percent. Early-onset is vertical transmission; late-onset is from the nursery. The most common organisms are Group B Streptococcus and E. coli. Risk factors include maternal GBS colonization, prolonged rupture of membranes, and prematurity. Signs are nonspecific — temperature instability, poor feeding, lethargy, and the infant who just does not look right. Start antibiotics before cultures result."

---

## SLIDE 12 — RESPIRATORY CONDITIONS
"The compromised newborn respiratory conditions. TTN, transient tachypnea, presents with tachypnea above 60 from retained lung fluid. RDS presents with a ground-glass chest x-ray from surfactant deficiency. MAS, meconium aspiration, presents with meconium-stained fluid. Perinatal asphyxia presents with apnea and limpness from impaired gas exchange."

---

## SLIDE 13 — TAKE NOTE: RESPIRATORY MEMORY
"Take note of the respiratory memory hooks. RDS is surfactant deficiency, seen in preterm and IDM infants. TTN is retained fluid, seen in cesarean births without labor. MAS is meconium, seen in term or postterm infants with distress. These three hooks will carry you through the exam."

---

## SLIDE 14 — VEAL CHOP
"VEAL CHOP is the mnemonic for fetal heart rate patterns. Variable decelerations mean cord compression — reposition. Early decelerations mean head compression and are benign. Accelerations are OK. Late decelerations mean placental insufficiency and are concerning — act. Late decelerations are the pattern you must never ignore."

---

## SLIDE 15 — FHR CATEGORIES
"The three-tier system. Category I is normal and requires routine monitoring. Category II is indeterminate and requires intrauterine resuscitation and evaluation, not immediate delivery. Category III is abnormal and requires expedited delivery. Intrauterine resuscitation is repositioning, IV fluids, reducing uterotonics, and treating hypotension."

---

## SLIDE 16 — TAKE NOTE: NO ROUTINE O2
"Take note of the 2025 reversal. Routine maternal oxygen is no longer recommended for Category II or III tracings when maternal oxygen saturation is normal. Give oxygen only if maternal hypoxia is present. Perform intrauterine resuscitation first. This is a significant change from older guidance."

---

## SLIDE 17 — NRP ABC
"Newborn resuscitation follows the ABCs. Dry, stimulate, and warm. For airway, position and suction mouth then nose. For breathing, provide positive-pressure ventilation — the single most important step. For circulation, begin chest compressions if the heart rate is below 60 after adequate ventilation. The goal is a heart rate above 100, spontaneous breathing, and a pink tongue."

---

## SLIDE 18 — FETAL DEMISE
"Fetal demise and perinatal loss. Miscarriage is before 20 weeks, stillbirth or intrauterine fetal demise is after 20 weeks, and neonatal death is within the first 28 days. Demise is confirmed by absent cardiac activity on ultrasound. Nursing care combines compassionate bereavement support with monitoring the mother for coagulopathy, since prolonged retention risks DIC."

---

## SLIDE 19 — TAKE NOTE: GRIEF SUPPORT
"Take note of bereavement care. Allow parents to view, hold, and create keepsakes. Use the word died — do not use euphemisms. Honor cultural and spiritual practices. Include the partner. Never say you can have another baby — it invalidates grief. Monitor maternal safety throughout. Grief has no timeline, and meeting the family where they are is the most important nursing intervention."

---

## SLIDE 20 — KEY TERMS NEONATAL
"Key terminology. TTN is transient tachypnea of the newborn. RDS is respiratory distress syndrome. MAS is meconium aspiration syndrome. NAS or NOWS is neonatal abstinence syndrome. HIE is hypoxic-ischemic encephalopathy. PPV is positive-pressure ventilation. NRP is the Neonatal Resuscitation Program. BIND is bilirubin-induced neurologic dysfunction."

---

## SLIDE 21 — NGN CASE IDM
"An NGN case. Ana is 33, G2 P1, at 39 weeks with class A2 gestational diabetes. The fetus is large for gestational age, and intrapartum glucose is 150. In the first hour, the newborn is jittery with poor feeding and a glucose of 32. This is neonatal hypoglycemia from fetal hyperinsulinism. Feed the infant, or give IV dextrose if unable to feed, then recheck."

---

## SLIDE 22 — NGN CASE FETAL DEMISE
"Another case. Sofia is 30, G2 P1, at 36 weeks, reporting no fetal movement for 24 hours. There are no fetal heart tones, and ultrasound confirms absent cardiac activity. The priority is compassionate bereavement care alongside maternal coagulopathy monitoring. Offer viewing and memory-making, honor cultural practices, and provide clear information about the birth process."

## SLIDE 31 — EXIT TICKET
"Exit ticket questions. The first action in cord prolapse is to lift the presenting part off the cord. Late decelerations mean placental insufficiency. The priority NRP step for a non-breathing newborn is positive-pressure ventilation. And for a Category II tracing with normal oxygen saturation, do not give routine oxygen — perform intrauterine resuscitation."

## SLIDE 34 — QUICK RECALL PART 1
"Quick recall. Cephalohematoma does not cross sutures; caput does and resolves fast. Erb palsy is the upper plexus with grasp present. Physiologic jaundice is after 24 hours; pathologic is before 24 hours with a rapid rise. Kernicterus occurs at a TSB of 25 or more. IDM hypoglycemia is below 40, treated with feeding or dextrose."

---

## SLIDE 35 — QUICK RECALL PART 2
"TTN is retained fluid, seen in cesarean births. RDS is surfactant deficiency with a ground-glass x-ray. MAS is meconium with a ball-valve effect. Sepsis is GBS or E. coli, and antibiotics are started immediately. VEAL CHOP: late decelerations mean placental insufficiency. Category II means resuscitate; Category III means deliver. PPV is the most important NRP step. For perinatal loss, allow holding and never blame."
