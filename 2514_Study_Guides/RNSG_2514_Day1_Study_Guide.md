02 BLEEDING IN PREGNANCY: EARLY vs LATE
Bleeding at any point in pregnancy is potentially life-threatening. The biggest single cause of maternal death worldwide
is obstetric hemorrhage. Management = early recognition → assessment → resuscitation.
TIMING COMMON CAUSES HALLMARK
EARLY Spontaneous abortion • Ectopic pregnancy • Gestational Vaginal bleeding ± pain, before 20

03 1. SPONTANEOUS ABORTION (MISCARRIAGE)
Definition
Loss of a pregnancy before 20 weeks of gestation, from natural causes. Spontaneous (miscarriage) is the most common
complication of early pregnancy - up to 31% of first-trimester pregnancies. Induced = elective or therapeutic
termination. Loss after 20 weeks is a stillbirth.

Nursing priorities
- Assess: color (bright red = significant), amount (pad count - saturating 1 pad/hour = significant), clots/tissue; have
the client save any tissue.
- Monitor: vital signs, pain, cramping, bleeding; watch for signs of shock (tachycardia, hypotension).
- Prepare: suction curettage / D&C or misoprostol to empty the uterus.
- Administer RhoGAM within 72 hours if Rh-negative and not sensitized.
- Support: reassure the family their actions did not cause the loss; validate grief and offer follow-up.
TAKE NOTE! Reassure the client that most spontaneous abortions result from a fetal abnormality and are not caused by
anything the parent did. Emotional recovery may take years.
04 2. ECTOPIC PREGNANCY
Definition: fertilized ovum implants outside the uterus - 96% in the fallopian tube. No other site can accommodate a
growing embryo → risk of rupture and massive hemorrhage.
Risk factors: previous ectopic • PID / STI • IUD use • smoking • endometriosis • IVF • sterilization failure.
Diagnosis: low serum beta-hCG + ultrasound showing an extrauterine sac.
Treatment: stable / non-ruptured → single-dose IM methotrexate (folic-acid antagonist); ruptured / unstable → surgery
(salpingostomy preserves the tube, salpingectomy removes it).
Nursing: teach signs of rupture - severe sharp unilateral pain, vertigo/fainting, hypotension, tachycardia; use
contraception ≥3 cycles; RhoGAM if Rh-negative.
TAKE NOTE! The hallmark of ectopic pregnancy is abdominal pain with spotting 6-8 weeks after a missed period. Shoulder
pain = referred pain from intra-abdominal blood and suggests rupture - a surgical emergency.

Definition: abnormal overgrowth of trophoblastic (placental) tissue; pregnancy is not viable. Tissue produces hCG, which
is the clinical marker.
Hydatidiform mole: chorionic villi degenerate into grape-like vesicles. Complete = empty egg + sperm (46 all-paternal);
Partial = triploid (69). May transform into choriocarcinoma.
Findings: vaginal bleeding • uterus larger than dates • pelvic pain • hyperemesis • preeclampsia before 20 weeks (red
flag). Choriocarcinoma first sign is often shortness of breath (lung metastases).
Management: evacuation by D&C (or hysterectomy) → serial beta-hCG until non-detectable → reliable contraception
(6-12 months) → methotrexate chemo if indicated.
TAKE NOTE! Pregnancy is contraindicated for at least 6-12 months after a molar pregnancy because it would hide rising hCG
used to detect malignancy.
06 4. CERVICAL INSUFFICIENCY
Definition: painless cervical dilation in the second trimester → expulsion of an immature fetus.
Risk: prior cervical trauma/surgery • second-trimester loss • DES exposure • connective-tissue disorders.
Findings: pink-tinged discharge • pelvic pressure • painless dilation.
Management: cerclage (purse-string suture to reinforce the internal os) placed in the second trimester.
Nursing: monitor for preterm labor (backache, increased discharge, ROM, contractions); teach the family to report
changes immediately.
07 5. PLACENTA PREVIA vs PLACENTAL ABRUPTION
Both cause late-pregnancy bleeding, but they are managed very differently. Previa = placenta over the cervix (bleed is
maternal, painless). Abruption = placenta separates early (bleed can be concealed, painful, fetal distress).
FEATURE PLACENTA PREVIA PLACENTAL ABRUPTION
Onset Insidious Sudden
Bleeding Always visible; bright red Concealed or visible; dark (port-wine)
Pain Painless Painful - "knifelike"; uterine tenderness
Uterine tone Soft, relaxed Firm to rigid (board-like)
Fetal heart rate Usually normal Distress or absent
Fetal presentation May be breech/transverse No relationship
Placenta previa - key points
- Placenta covers or is near the internal cervical os ("low-lying" = edge <2 cm from os).
- Risk: prior previa • prior cesarean • multiparity • advanced age • multiple gestation.
- NEVER perform a vaginal exam - it can disrupt the placenta and cause hemorrhage.
- Care: IV access • type & crossmatch • monitor fetal status • bed rest • plan cesarean birth.

08 6. DIC (DISSEMINATED INTRAVASCULAR COAGULATION)
What it is: widespread clotting that uses up clotting factors, then bleeding occurs (thrombin/plasmin imbalance).
Obstetric triggers: abruption • amniotic fluid embolism • retained dead fetus • sepsis after abortion • molar pregnancy •
HELLP.
Lab picture: ↓ platelets • ↓ fibrinogen • ↑ PT / aPTT • positive D-dimer.
Care: treat the cause • replace volume • transfuse PRBCs, FFP, cryoprecipitate, platelets.

10 8. HYPEREMESIS GRAVIDARUM
Definition: severe, persistent, uncontrollable nausea and vomiting beginning ~5-6 weeks, causing weight loss >5% of
prepregnancy weight, dehydration, ketosis, electrolyte imbalance, and ketonuria.
Why: elevated hCG (and estrogen) • relaxed lower esophageal sphincter • genetics.
Risk: prior N/V with estrogen/migraine/motion sickness • molar pregnancy • multiple gestation • family history.
Management - step by step
STEP INTERVENTION
1. Priority Stop ALL oral food/fluid (NPO) to rest the gut until vomiting stops
2. Rehydrate IV normal saline + vitamins (pyridoxine/B6) + electrolytes

Client teaching to minimize nausea (Teaching Guidelines 19.1)
- Eat small, frequent meals; separate fluids from solids (fluids between meals).
- Avoid strong odors, perfumes, and fatty foods; avoid tight waistbands.
- Eat dry crackers/toast; try ginger or peppermint tea; high-protein drinks.
- Do not lie down for 2 hours after eating; rest; manage stress.
TAKE NOTE! The priority in hyperemesis is to stop all oral intake for a period until vomiting stops, then rehydrate - not to
force fluids by mouth.

12 ANSWER KEY & RATIONALE
Q ANSWER WHY
1 C Painless bright-red bleeding + soft uterus + normal FHR = placenta previa. A vaginal exam is contraindicated
(may disrupt placenta and cause hemorrhage).
2 B Shoulder pain is referred pain from intra-abdominal blood; with hypotension/tachycardia it signals rupture
(surgical emergency).
3 B Molar tissue produces hCG; serial beta-hCG detects residual/malignant tissue. Pregnancy would mask rising
hCG, so contraception is essential.
4 B Priority = NPO to rest the gut, then IV fluids and antiemetics. Forcing oral intake prolongs vomiting.
5 A Rh-negative, non-sensitized clients receive RhoGAM within 72 hours to prevent isoimmunization.
6 B DIC = ↓ platelets, ↓ fibrinogen, ↑ PT/aPTT, positive D-dimer (clotting factors consumed).
