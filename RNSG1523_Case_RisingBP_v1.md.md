Maria, 28, G2 T1 P0 A0 L1, singleton pregnancy. Prior birth at 39 weeks, no complications. Prepregnancy BP normal.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Scene 1 — Recognize Cues (highlight)

#### Chart (24 weeks):

BP 118/72, weight +6 lb, urine dip negative protein, no headache, reports normal fetal movement.

#### Chart (28 weeks):

BP 128/82, weight +9 lb, trace protein, denies symptoms.

#### Chart (32 weeks, today):

BP 142/92 repeated 142/94 four hours later; weight +12 lb; reports mild ankle swelling; denies headache or visual change.

Highlight the findings across the three visits that should prompt further evaluation.

the upward BP trend (118/72 → 128/82 → 142/92) and today's two readings ≥ 140/90.

A rising trend crossing 140/90 after 20 weeks is the earliest cue of a hypertensive disorder \[ACOG PB 222\]. Mild ankle swelling alone is normal dependent edema.

Select the 3 appropriate plan elements.

✅ Teach preeclampsia warning signs (severe headache, visual change, RUQ pain, sudden facial/hand swelling)

✅ Arrange increased BP surveillance and lab follow-up

Scene 6 — Evaluate Outcomes (trend)

#### Follow-up trend:

week 33 BP 138/88, denies symptoms; week 34 BP 144/94 with new P:C 0.4 and a persistent headache; reflexes 3+.

Which interpretation should drive the next action?

The picture is progressing toward preeclampsia (new significant proteinuria plus a CNS symptom); escalate evaluation now.

A change in the trend — new proteinuria and a headache — signals progression and outweighs any single reassuring reading \[ACOG PB 222\].
