Unfolding Case (WO-G) — Umbilical Cord Prolapse

RNSG 2514 Nursing III ·

#### Traces to objectives:

2514-D4-4, 2514-D4-5 ·

6 scenes, one per NCJMM operation.

Nia, 25, G1 P0, at 39 weeks in active labor. Fetus at a high station; artificial rupture of membranes just performed.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Scene 1 — Recognize Cues (highlight)

Immediately after rupture, the FHR drops to 70 and stays low; the nurse palpates a pulsating cord in the vagina; the presenting part is high.

Highlight the cues of cord prolapse.

abrupt fetal bradycardia after ROM + a palpable pulsating cord + high presenting part.

Bradycardia right after rupture with a palpable cord is prolapse — the fastest emergency in the unit \[Lowdermilk\].

An unengaged part at ROM is the classic setup; prolapse compresses the cord and causes bradycardia \[Lowdermilk\].

Scene 3 — Prioritize Hypotheses (cloze/dropdown)

The emergency is

\[umbilical cord prolapse\]

, and the priority is to

\[relieve cord compression and expedite birth\]

Every second of compression risks fetal hypoxia; relieving it comes first \[Lowdermilk\].

Scene 4 — Generate Solutions (extended multiple response)

Select the 3 correct immediate solutions.

✅ Manually elevate the presenting part off the cord with a gloved hand

✅ Reposition to knee-chest or steep Trendelenburg

✅ Call for help and prepare for immediate cesarean birth

❌ Attempt to push the cord back into the uterus

❌ Increase oxytocin to speed delivery

❌ Place the patient supine and flat

Offload the cord and reposition while mobilizing surgery; never handle or replace the cord \[Lowdermilk\].

Scene 5 — Take Actions (drag-and-drop ordering)

Order the emergency actions.

Call for help and press the emergency call light

Use a gloved hand to lift the presenting part off the cord

Place the patient in knee-chest or steep Trendelenburg

Prepare for immediate cesarean birth

Keep the examining hand in place during transfer to the OR

The hand that finds the prolapse stays to protect perfusion until delivery \[Lowdermilk\].

Scene 6 — Evaluate Outcomes (trend)

minute 0 FHR 70 with cord compression; minute 2 after manual elevation and repositioning, FHR 120; minute 8 in the OR, FHR 140; infant delivered by cesarean with Apgar 8/9.

Which interpretation best evaluates the response?

Manual elevation restored fetal perfusion (FHR recovered), and expedited cesarean delivered a vigorous infant — the emergency response was effective.

Evaluate the intervention by FHR recovery once compression is relieved and by neonatal condition at birth \[Lowdermilk\].

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Debrief — three decision points students most often miss

Removing the hand.

The examining hand must stay to keep the presenting part off the cord until birth.

Handling the cord.

Pushing it back or manipulating it causes vasospasm and worsens hypoxia.

Wrong position.

Supine-flat worsens compression; knee-chest or Trendelenburg offloads the cord.
