Scene 3 — Prioritize Hypotheses (cloze/dropdown)

The evolving diagnosis is

\[preeclampsia with severe features / HELLP\]

HELLP is a severe-features variant requiring magnesium, antihypertensives, and timing of birth \[ACOG PB 222\].

Scene 4 — Generate Solutions (extended multiple response)

Select the 3 correct interventions.

✅ Begin magnesium sulfate for seizure prophylaxis

✅ Give an antihypertensive (labetalol, hydralazine, or nifedipine) for severe-range BP

✅ Prepare for delivery planning and give corticosteroids if indicated

❌ Use magnesium sulfate to lower the blood pressure

❌ Discharge home with oral iron

❌ Withhold treatment until proteinuria is quantified by 24-hour urine

Magnesium prevents seizures; separate antihypertensives lower BP; do not delay for a 24-hour urine when severe features are present \[ACOG PB 222\].

Scene 5 — Take Actions (SATA)

While starting magnesium, which nursing actions apply? Select all that apply.

✅ Keep calcium gluconate at the bedside

✅ Monitor deep tendon reflexes, respirations, and urine output

✅ Assess for pulmonary edema and continuous fetal monitoring

✅ Reassess BP and treat severe-range values promptly

❌ Expect magnesium to normalize the blood pressure

Magnesium safety monitoring plus separate BP control are both required \[ACOG PB 222\].

Debrief — three decision points students most often miss

Expecting magnesium to lower BP.

Magnesium is seizure prophylaxis; severe-range BP needs a separate antihypertensive.

Waiting for proteinuria quantification.

Severe features (thrombocytopenia, RUQ pain, transaminases) justify acting now.

Missing the HELLP trend.

A single platelet value can mislead; the falling platelet/rising enzyme trajectory is the alarm.
