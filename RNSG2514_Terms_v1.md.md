Safety/Framework

Situation, Background, Assessment, Recommendation; structured handoff and escalation tool.

Alliance for Innovation on Maternal Health; source of national maternal safety bundles.

MEWS / MEOWS

Maternal Early Warning System; vital-sign triggers prompting rapid clinical review.

Rapid Response Team; summoned when early-warning criteria are met.

Quantitative Blood Loss; weighed measurement, QI-preferred over visual estimation.

Bleeding/Hemorrhage

Antepartum Hemorrhage; any bleeding before birth, always evaluate before vaginal exam.

Postpartum Hemorrhage; ≥1,000 mL cumulative loss or loss with hypovolemia within 24 h.

Early Pregnancy Loss; nonviable intrauterine pregnancy before 13 0/7 weeks.

Ectopic pregnancy

Implantation outside the uterus; unilateral pain and bleeding, risk of rupture and shock.

GTD / molar

Gestational Trophoblastic Disease; grape-like villi, very high hCG, needs hCG follow-up.

Placenta previa

Placenta covering the internal os; painless bright-red bleeding, no digital exam.

Placenta Accreta Spectrum; abnormal invasion (accreta/increta/percreta), major hemorrhage risk.

Vasa previa

Fetal vessels crossing the os; rupture causes fetal exsanguination, not maternal.

Abruptio placentae

Premature separation of a normal placenta; painful dark bleeding, rigid tender uterus.

Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation; consumptive clotting then bleeding, oozing from sites.

Tranexamic acid; antifibrinolytic given within 3 hours for postpartum hemorrhage.

Packed red cells / fresh frozen plasma; components of massive transfusion protocol.

Intrauterine Fetal Demise; fetal death before birth, confirmed by absent cardiac activity.

Spontaneous Abortion (miscarriage); pregnancy loss before 20 weeks.

Dilation and Curettage / Evacuation; uterine evacuation for loss or retained tissue.

Manual Vacuum Aspiration; office-based uterine evacuation for early pregnancy loss.

RhIG after bleeding

Rh(D) immune globulin for Rh-negative patients after loss, bleeding, or procedures.

Uterine atony

Failure of the uterus to contract after birth; the leading cause of PPH.

Hypertension

Hypertensive Disorders of Pregnancy; spectrum from gHTN to preeclampsia and HELLP.

Gestational Hypertension; new hypertension after 20 weeks without proteinuria or severe features.

Chronic Hypertension; hypertension before pregnancy or before 20 weeks.

Preeclampsia

New hypertension after 20 weeks with proteinuria or end-organ signs.

Preeclampsia w/ severe features

Severe BP, thrombocytopenia, renal/liver involvement, pulmonary edema, or CNS symptoms.

Hemolysis, Elevated Liver enzymes, Low Platelets; a severe preeclampsia variant.

Lactate dehydrogenase; elevated with the hemolysis of HELLP.

Liver transaminases; elevation signals hepatic involvement in HELLP or severe preeclampsia.

Urine protein-to-creatinine ratio; quantifies proteinuria without a 24-hour collection.

Deep Tendon Reflexes; monitored on magnesium — loss signals rising toxicity.

Magnesium sulfate; seizure prophylaxis in preeclampsia, not an antihypertensive.

Mean Arterial Pressure; average perfusion pressure across the cardiac cycle.

Low-dose aspirin

81 mg started 12–28 weeks for high-risk patients to reduce preeclampsia.

Magnesium antidote

Calcium gluconate reverses magnesium sulfate toxicity; keep it at the bedside.

Right upper quadrant pain; liver capsule signal in HELLP or severe preeclampsia.

Creatinine (preeclampsia)

Rising serum creatinine marks renal involvement, a severe feature.

Diabetes/Hyperemesis

GDM A1 vs A2

A1 gestational diabetes is diet-controlled; A2 requires medication.

Pregestational Diabetes Mellitus; diabetes predating pregnancy, higher anomaly risk.

Glycosylated hemoglobin; reflects average glucose over roughly 3 months.

Diabetic Ketoacidosis; can occur at lower glucose in pregnancy, threatens the fetus.

Large / Small for Gestational Age; LGA follows poor glycemic control.

Hyperemesis Gravidarum; severe vomiting with weight loss, ketosis, and electrolyte loss.

Pregnancy-Unique Quantification of Emesis; scores nausea and vomiting severity.

Neonatal hypoglycemia

Low newborn glucose after maternal hyperglycemia caused fetal hyperinsulinism.

Nothing by mouth; ordered before possible operative birth or for severe hyperemesis.

Total / Peripheral Parenteral Nutrition; IV nutrition when the gut cannot be used.

Preterm/Membranes

Preterm Labor; regular contractions with cervical change before 37 weeks.

Prelabor Rupture of Membranes at or after 37 weeks; formerly 'premature'.

Preterm Prelabor Rupture of Membranes; rupture before 37 weeks, high infection risk.

Fetal Fibronectin; negative result strongly predicts NOT delivering within two weeks.

Cervical length

Transvaginal ultrasound measure; short cervix raises spontaneous preterm-birth risk.

Antenatal Corticosteroids (betamethasone/dexamethasone); accelerate fetal lung maturity.

Respiratory Distress Syndrome; surfactant-deficient preterm lung disease ACS helps prevent.

17α-hydroxyprogesterone caproate; withdrawn from the U.S. market in 2023, no longer standard.

Nifedipine (tocolytic)

Calcium channel blocker used to delay preterm birth long enough for steroids.

Indomethacin (tocolytic)

NSAID tocolytic limited to before 32 weeks and short duration.

Chorioamnionitis

Intraamniotic infection; maternal fever, tachycardia, uterine tenderness, needs delivery.

Mg neuroprotection

Magnesium sulfate before ~32 weeks reduces cerebral palsy in the preterm neonate.

Oligo / Poly

Oligohydramnios (too little) / Polyhydramnios (too much) amniotic fluid.

SROM / AROM

Spontaneous / Artificial Rupture of Membranes; note time, color, odor, and FHR.

Intraventricular Hemorrhage; a preterm brain complication ACS and Mg help reduce.

Necrotizing Enterocolitis; serious preterm bowel injury, feeding intolerance and distension.

Labor/Operative

Difficult or abnormally slow labor; analyze via the 5 Ps.

Cephalopelvic Disproportion; fetal head too large for the maternal pelvis.

Tachysystole

More than 5 contractions in 10 minutes averaged over 30 minutes.

Montevideo Units; adequate labor is roughly ≥200 MVU per 10 minutes.

Bishop score

Cervical favorability (dilation, effacement, station, consistency, position) predicting induction success.

Misoprostol (PGE₁)

Cervical ripening agent; contraindicated with prior cesarean due to rupture risk.

Dinoprostone (PGE₂)

Prostaglandin cervical ripening agent; stop if tachysystole or Category II/III occurs.

TOLAC / VBAC

Trial of Labor / Vaginal Birth After Cesarean; main risk is uterine rupture.

NTSV cesarean rate

Nulliparous Term Singleton Vertex cesarean rate; a key obstetric QI metric.

Shoulder dystocia

Fetal shoulder impacted after head delivery; an unpredictable obstetric emergency.

McRoberts maneuver

Sharp maternal hip hyperflexion; first-line maneuver for shoulder dystocia.

Labor arrest

No cervical change despite adequate contractions and specified dilation; replaces 'failure to progress'.

Operative vaginal birth

Forceps- or vacuum-assisted birth; requires specified station and consent.

Intrauterine Pressure Catheter; measures contraction strength in Montevideo units.

External Cephalic Version; manual turning of a breech fetus to vertex.

Surveillance/Neonatal

Electronic Fetal Monitoring; continuous fetal heart rate and contraction tracing.

Category I FHR

Normal tracing; routine monitoring, no intervention needed.

Category II FHR

Indeterminate tracing; requires evaluation and intrauterine resuscitation, not immediate delivery.

Category III FHR

Abnormal tracing (absent variability with recurrent decels or bradycardia); expedite delivery.

Intrauterine resuscitation

Reposition, IV fluids, reduce uterotonics, treat hypotension — restore fetal oxygenation.

Routine O₂ reversal

ACOG 2025 advises against routine maternal oxygen for Category II/III without maternal hypoxia.

Fetal Growth Restriction; estimated weight or abdominal circumference below the 10th percentile.

Umbilical artery Doppler; absent or reversed end-diastolic flow signals severe FGR.

Uterine rupture

Tearing of the uterine wall; loss of station, acute pain, Category III tracing.

Cord prolapse

Umbilical cord ahead of the presenting part; relieve compression and expedite birth.

Neonatal Resuscitation Program; the AAP/AHA algorithm for newborn resuscitation.

Positive Pressure Ventilation; the single most important NRP step for a non-breathing newborn.

Hypoxic-Ischemic Encephalopathy; brain injury from perinatal oxygen deprivation.

Therapeutic hypothermia

Neonatal cooling within 6 hours to limit brain injury after significant hypoxia.

Meconium Aspiration Syndrome; meconium below the cords causing respiratory distress.

Transient Tachypnea of the Newborn; self-limited from retained fetal lung fluid.

Neonatal Abstinence / Opioid Withdrawal Syndrome; irritability, poor feeding, high-pitched cry.

Intermittent auscultation

Periodic fetal heart listening; an option for low-risk labor instead of continuous EFM.

Umbilical cord blood gas; documents fetal acid-base status at birth.

Discrimination

Distinguish previa from abruption

Previa: painless bright-red bleeding, soft uterus. Abruption: painful dark bleeding, rigid tender uterus.

Distinguish PROM from PPROM

PROM is rupture at ≥37 weeks; PPROM is rupture before 37 weeks with higher infection risk.

Distinguish symmetric from asymmetric FGR

Symmetric FGR is early and global; asymmetric spares the head, suggesting placental insufficiency.

Distinguish Category I/II/III

I normal, no action; II indeterminate, resuscitate and evaluate; III abnormal, expedite delivery.

Distinguish gHTN from preeclampsia

gHTN is hypertension alone; preeclampsia adds proteinuria or end-organ dysfunction.

Distinguish gHTN from chronic HTN

gHTN starts after 20 weeks; chronic hypertension predates pregnancy or precedes 20 weeks.

Distinguish HELLP from preeclampsia

HELLP adds hemolysis, elevated liver enzymes, and low platelets; may occur with mild BP.

Distinguish vasa previa from previa

Vasa previa rupture bleeds the fetus; placenta previa bleeding is maternal.

Distinguish early from late decelerations

Early mirror contractions (head compression, benign); late lag behind (placental insufficiency, concerning).

Distinguish variable from late decelerations

Variable are abrupt and vary (cord compression); late are gradual and delayed (placental insufficiency).

Distinguish MgSO₄ role from antihypertensive

Magnesium prevents seizures; labetalol/hydralazine/nifedipine lower blood pressure.

Distinguish tocolytic from uterotonic

Tocolytics (nifedipine) stop contractions; uterotonics (oxytocin) cause them to treat hemorrhage.

4 Ts of PPH

Tone (atony), Trauma (laceration), Tissue (retained placenta), Thrombin (coagulopathy).

5 Ps of labor

Passenger, Passageway, Powers, Position, Psyche — the determinants of labor progress.

Variable→Cord compression; Early→Head compression; Accelerations→OK; Late→Placental insufficiency.

Shoulder dystocia: Help, Episiotomy eval, Legs (McRoberts), Pressure suprapubic, Enter, Remove arm, Roll.

Magnesium toxicity sequence

Loss of reflexes → respiratory depression → cardiac arrest; monitor DTRs, RR, and output.

Say-this-not-that

Say: prelabor ROM

Not 'premature rupture of membranes' — say prelabor rupture of membranes (PROM).

Say: with/without severe features

Not 'mild vs severe preeclampsia' — say preeclampsia without or with severe features.

Say: gestational hypertension

Not 'PIH' — say gestational hypertension (gHTN).

Say: Category II or III

Not 'nonreassuring fetal heart rate' — say Category II (indeterminate) or III (abnormal).

Say: fetal growth restriction

Not 'IUGR' in obstetric documentation — say fetal growth restriction (FGR).

Say: preeclampsia

Not 'toxemia' — say preeclampsia.

Say: labor arrest

Not 'failure to progress' — say labor arrest with a defined stage and criteria.

Say: recurrent pregnancy loss

Not 'habitual aborter' — say recurrent pregnancy loss.

Say: Category II/III with features

Not 'fetal distress' — describe the Category II/III tracing with its specified features.
