02 1. DYSTOCIA - THE 4 Ps OF ABNORMAL LABOR
Dystocia = abnormal progression of labor; accounts for ~50% of unplanned cesarean births in nulliparous patients. Slow
or stalled cervical dilation, descent, or contractions.
The four contributing factors (4 Ps)
FACTOR EXAMPLES
POWER Hypertonic dysfunction (latent-phase, uncoordinated, painful) • Hypotonic dysfunction (active-phase,
(Expulsive forces) weak/fading contractions) • Precipitous labor (<3 h)
PASSENGER Malpresentation: breech, face/brow, persistent occiput posterior • Macrosomia (>4,000 g) • Shoulder
(Fetus) dystocia • Multifetal pregnancy
PASSAGE Contracted pelvic inlet/midpelvis/outlet • Soft-tissue obstruction (full bladder, cervical swelling) • Gynecoid
(Pelvis & canal) pelvis = most favorable
PSYCHE Fear/anxiety → catecholamines → myometrial dysfunction • Increases pain perception, reduces uterine
(Maternal stress) contractility
Hypertonic vs Hypotonic uterine dysfunction
FEATURE HYPERTONIC HYPOTONIC
Phase Latent phase (cervix <4 cm) Active phase

Shoulder dystocia
Definition: fetal head delivers but the shoulders are wedged behind the pubic bone - obstetric emergency. The chest
cannot expand; cord compression between fetal body and maternal pelvis.
Risk: macrosomia • maternal diabetes • obesity • postdates • prior shoulder dystocia.
Emergency response: McRoberts maneuver (hyperflex thighs to abdomen) + suprapubic pressure (NOT fundal).
Effective in >50% of cases. Also: hands-and-knees, squatting, lateral recumbent.
Avoid: fundal pressure • strong lateral traction • forced head rotation (increase brachial plexus injury).
After birth: assess newborn for crepitus, deformity, Erb palsy, bruising; assess parent for hemorrhage, bladder trauma.
TAKE NOTE! FUNDAL pressure is contraindicated in shoulder dystocia - it does not free the shoulder and increases the risk
of injury. Use suprapubic pressure.
03 2. LABOR INDUCTION vs AUGMENTATION
PROCEDURE DEFINITION KEY POINT
Induction Stimulating contractions before spontaneous labor 25% of U.S. births; requires medical indication
begins
Augmentation Enhancing inadequate contractions after labor has Typically oxytocin; labor already in progress
started
Indications & contraindications
- Indications: postterm/prolonged gestation • PPROM • gestational HTN/preeclampsia • chorioamnionitis •
oligohydramnios • placental abruption • FGR • fetal demise • diabetes.
- Contraindications: complete placenta previa • transverse fetal lie • prolapsed cord • prior classic uterine incision •
active genital herpes • CPD.
Bishop score - cervical readiness
Score ranges 0-13. >8 = favorable (likely to succeed). <5 = unfavorable → ripen cervix before induction. Evaluates
dilation, effacement, station, consistency, and position.

Methods of cervical ripening & induction
TYPE METHOD NOTES
Pharmacologic Dinoprostone (Cervidil/Prepidil) • Misoprostol (Cytotec, Only dinoprostone is FDA-approved for cervical
off-label) • Oxytocin (Pitocin) ripening; misoprostol contraindicated after prior
cesarean (uterine rupture risk)
Mechanical Foley balloon catheter; hygroscopic dilators (Laminaria, Balloon reduces hyperstimulation risk; dilators
Dilapan) expand over 12-24 h
Surgical Amniotomy (AROM); membrane stripping Risk: cord prolapse, infection, FHR deceleration;
amniotic fluid characteristics must be monitored
Oxytocin (Pitocin) nursing
- Administration: 10 units in 1,000 mL isotonic solution via IV infusion pump (piggyback). Titrate to contractions every
2-3 min, lasting 40-60 sec.
- Monitor: contractions (frequency, duration, intensity, resting tone) • FHR continuously • maternal VS every 1-2 h •
I&O; (antidiuretic effect) • cervical progress.
- DISCONTINUE if: tetanic contractions >90 sec • uterine hyperstimulation (contractions <2 min apart) • elevated
resting tone • category II/III FHR pattern.
- Adverse effects: uterine hyperstimulation → fetal hypoxia • uterine rupture • water intoxication (if rate >20 mU/min in
electrolyte-free solution) • hypotension.
TAKE NOTE! Resting uterine tone above 20 mm Hg compromises uteroplacental perfusion → fetal hypoxia. Continuous FHR
monitoring is mandatory.
04 3. UMBILICAL CORD PROLAPSE
Definition: the umbilical cord slips ahead of the presenting part through the cervix - obstetric emergency. Complete
occlusion = fetal death if not relieved.
Risk: fetal malpresentation (breech, transverse) • prematurity • hydramnios • PROM • amniotomy without engaged
presenting part • multifetal pregnancy • long cord.
First sign: sudden fetal bradycardia or recurrent variable decelerations that worsen after membranes rupture.

- Call for help immediately - do not leave the patient.
- Relieve cord compression: insert sterile gloved hand into vagina and push the presenting part off the cord and hold it
there.
- Position change: modified Sims, Trendelenburg, or knee-chest position.
- DO NOT attempt to replace the cord back into the uterus.
- Administer oxygen if ordered; continuous FHR monitoring.
- Prepare for emergency cesarean if cervix is not fully dilated.
TAKE NOTE! When the presenting part does not fully occupy the pelvic inlet, prolapse is more likely. After amniotomy, verify
the presenting part is applied to the cervix and engaged.

06 ANSWER KEY & RATIONALE
Q ANSWER WHY
1 B Decreasing frequency/intensity in active labor after normal progress = hypotonic dysfunction; treated with
amniotomy + oxytocin.
2 B Shoulder dystocia = McRoberts maneuver + suprapubic pressure. NOT fundal pressure (increases brachial
plexus injury).
3 B Bishop score <5 = unfavorable cervix; cervical ripening (prostaglandins, balloon) is needed before oxytocin
induction.
4 C Uterine hyperstimulation (contractions <2 min apart) + non-reassuring FHR = discontinue oxytocin immediately;
turn left side, notify provider.
5 B Sudden bradycardia + variable decels after ROM with non-engaged part = cord prolapse; priority = push
presenting part off the cord, call for help.
6 A Sudden fetal bradycardia is the first and most reliable sign of uterine rupture. Requires immediate surgical
delivery.
