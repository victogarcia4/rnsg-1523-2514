02 1. HYPERTENSIVE DISORDERS OF PREGNANCY
Hypertensive disorders complicate up to 16% of pregnancies and are the leading cause of maternal death in the United
States. They form a spectrum: chronic hypertension → gestational hypertension → preeclampsia → eclampsia.

Risk factors & prevention
- Risk: primigravida • age <20 or >35 • obesity • prior preeclampsia • diabetes/chronic HTN/kidney disease • multiple

- Prevention: low-dose aspirin 81 mg daily from 12-16 weeks for high-risk patients.
Management
SEVERITY CARE
Without severe features Home: rest, BP checks, fetal kick counts, labs (CBC, liver enzymes, platelets); admit if BP rises or
symptoms appear; expectant management to 37 weeks
With severe features Hospitalize: antihypertensives (hydralazine, labetalol, nifedipine), magnesium sulfate, quiet/dim room,
seizure precautions, continuous fetal monitoring, deliver
Eclampsia Seizure management; turn patient on side; pad rails; suction + O2; continue magnesium sulfate 24 h
after last seizure; then deliver
Magnesium sulfate (seizure prophylaxis)
- Dose: loading 4-6 g IV over 15-30 min, then maintenance 1-2 g/h.
- Monitor: deep tendon reflexes (DTRs), respiratory rate, hourly urine output, serum magnesium.
- Toxicity signs: RR <12/min, absent DTRs, urine output <30 mL/h, lethargy, hypotension.
- Antidote: calcium gluconate 10% 10 mL IV over 3 min.
- Newborn effect: may show respiratory/neurologic depression, hypotonia, hypocalcemia.
TAKE NOTE! Preeclampsia increases the risk of placental abruption, preterm birth, and intrauterine growth restriction - be
prepared when you see symptoms.
03 2. HELLP SYNDROME & DIC
HELLP = Hemolysis, Elevated Liver enzymes, Low Platelets. A severe variant of preeclampsia (4-12% of cases), onset
27 weeks to delivery (may be postpartum).
Findings: epigastric / right-upper-quadrant pain • nausea, malaise • headache • visual changes.
Labs: ↓ hemoglobin, ↑ LDH (hemolysis) • ↑ AST/ALT (liver) • ↓ platelets <100,000.
Complications: DIC • liver rupture • placental abruption • eclampsia • acute renal failure • retinal detachment.
Care: stabilize BP • magnesium sulfate • steroids for fetal lungs • transfer to tertiary center • deliver.
DIC (Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation)

04 3. GESTATIONAL DIABETES MELLITUS (GDM)
Definition: glucose intolerance with onset during pregnancy (usually 2nd/3rd trimester). Distinct from pregestational
diabetes (type 1 / type 2 present before conception).
Why it happens: placental hormones (human placental lactogen, cortisol, prolactin, progesterone) cause insulin
resistance; beta-cell dysfunction → hyperglycemia.
Risk factors: obesity • prior GDM • first-degree relative with DM • PCOS • prior infant >9 lb • hypertension • certain
ancestries.
Screening & glucose targets (75 g OGTT at 24-28 weeks)
MEASURE DIAGNOSTIC THRESHOLD (GDM) TREATMENT TARGET
Fasting ≥92 mg/dL <95 mg/dL
1-hour postprandial ≥180 mg/dL <140 mg/dL
2-hour postprandial ≥153 mg/dL <120 mg/dL
Effects on parent vs fetus/newborn
EFFECTS ON PARENT EFFECTS ON FETUS / NEWBORN
Hypertension, preeclampsia • polyhydramnios • UTI/monilial Macrosomia • shoulder dystocia • neonatal hypoglycemia •
vaginitis • ketoacidosis • difficult labor, cesarean • future type 2 respiratory distress syndrome • hyperbilirubinemia •
DM polycythemia • congenital anomalies (pregestational)
Management
- Nutrition (Diabetes Plate Method) + exercise; monitor blood glucose 4x/day (before meals + bedtime).
- Insulin is first-line when medication is needed (does not cross the placenta).
- Metformin crosses the placenta - avoid in hypertension/preeclampsia.
- Frequent prenatal visits; serial ultrasounds, NST, biophysical profile; daily kick counts.
- Breastfeeding encouraged (lowers glucose, reduces future type 2 DM risk).
TAKE NOTE! Nutrient needs and weight-gain goals are the same for pregnant people with and without diabetes; the difference
is tight glucose control.

Definition: regular uterine contractions + cervical change (effacement & dilation) before 37 weeks.
Warning signs: change/increase in vaginal discharge • pelvic pressure • low dull backache • menstrual-like cramps • >6
contractions/hour.
Testing: cervical length <30 mm (increased risk); fetal fibronectin >50 ng/mL predicts preterm birth within 14 days.
Medications (Drug Guide 21.1)
MEDICATION ACTION NURSING IMPLICATIONS
Magnesium sulfate Relaxes uterine muscle (tocolytic, off-label); Loading 4-6 g, then 1-4 g/h; assess DTRs hourly;
neuroprotective for fetus watch toxicity (RR <12, absent DTRs, UO <30 mL/h);
calcium gluconate at bedside
Indomethacin (Indocin) Prostaglandin inhibitor (tocolytic) Oral with food; monitor bleeding; contraindicated >32
weeks, peptic ulcer; use ≤48 h
Nifedipine (Procardia) Calcium channel blocker (tocolytic) Caution with magnesium sulfate (hypotension);
monitor BP/pulse; watch flushing, headache, edema
Betamethasone Matures fetal lungs (surfactant); reduces RDS Two IM doses 24 hours apart; monitor for maternal
(Celestone) & intraventricular hemorrhage <34 weeks infection/pulmonary edema
- Tocolytics delay birth 2-7 days (time for steroids + transport) - they do not prevent preterm birth.
- Contraindications: abruption • fetal distress/death • severe preeclampsia/eclampsia • chorioamnionitis • active
bleeding • dilation >6 cm.
- Nursing: left side-lying • continuous fetal monitoring • limit vaginal exams • I&O; • hydrate.
TAKE NOTE! Cervical-length measurement is contraindicated with placenta previa, cervical cerclage, and PPROM.
06 5. PROM & PPROM
PROM = prelabor rupture of membranes at ≥37 weeks. PPROM = rupture before 37 weeks.
Presentation: leakage/gush of fluid, vaginal discharge, pelvic pressure - without contractions.
Complications: infection (chorioamnionitis) • cord compression/prolapse • placental abruption • preterm labor.
Diagnosis
TEST FINDING
Pooling Clear fluid pooled in the vaginal vault
Fern test Dried fluid shows ferning (salt crystallization) under microscope
Nitrazine paper Turns blue (amniotic fluid alkaline, pH 7.0 vs vaginal 4.5)

08 ANSWER KEY & RATIONALE
Q ANSWER WHY
1 A Severe features (BP ≥160/110, headache, visual changes, epigastric pain, hyperreflexia) signal impending
eclampsia; magnesium sulfate prevents seizures.
2 B HELLP = ↓ platelets, ↑ AST/ALT, ↑ LDH (hemolysis, liver injury, thrombocytopenia).
3 B RR <12 and absent DTRs = magnesium toxicity; give calcium gluconate (the antidote).
4 B GDM is diagnosed when any OGTT value meets threshold: fasting ≥92 mg/dL (96 meets it); the other sets are
all below thresholds.
5 A Betamethasone matures fetal lungs (surfactant) and reduces RDS and intraventricular hemorrhage in a preterm
newborn.
6 C With PROM/PPROM, a digital vaginal exam is contraindicated (infection risk); use a sterile speculum
examination.
