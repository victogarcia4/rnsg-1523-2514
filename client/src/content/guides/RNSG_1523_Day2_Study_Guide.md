## 02 FETAL DEVELOPMENT

Pregnancy lasts approximately **280 days (40 weeks)** from the first day of the LMP, or **266 days** from fertilization. Development proceeds cephalocaudal (head-to-toe), proximal-to-distal, and general-to-specific.

| Stage | Timeframe | Key Events |
|---|---|---|
| **Preembryonic** | Fertilization → end of 2nd week | Fertilization in outer third of fallopian tube → **zygote** (46 chromosomes). Cleavage → **morula** (16 cells, ~72 h) → **blastocyst**. Trophoblast (outer layer) → placenta & chorion. Inner cell mass → embryo & amnion.<br><br>**Implantation:** 7–10 days after conception in upper uterus (fundus).<br><br>**Three germ layers:** ectoderm (CNS, skin, glands), mesoderm (skeletal, circulatory, reproductive), endoderm (respiratory, liver, pancreas, digestive). |
| **Embryonic** | Day 15 → end of week 8 | **MOST CRITICAL period.** All major organs and external structures form (organogenesis). Embryo is **most susceptible to teratogens** (days 17–56 after conception). Heart begins beating ~22 days. By end of week 8, all organ systems present in basic form. |
| **Fetal** | End of week 8 → birth | Longest stage. Dramatic growth and refinement of all organ systems. Fetal heart rate: **110–160 bpm**. Quickening felt at **16–22 weeks**. Viability ~22–24 weeks with intensive care.<br><br>**Fetal circulation:** three shunts bypass lungs and liver — ductus venosus, ductus arteriosus, foramen ovale. Shunts close after birth with first breath.<br><br>**Placenta:** transfer organ + hormone factory (hCG, hCS, progesterone, estrogen); pass-through, NOT a true barrier.<br><br>**Amniotic fluid:** cushions, allows movement, maintains temperature, essential for lung development; peaks ~800–1,000 mL at term. |

> **■ TAKE NOTE! The embryonic stage (days 17–56) is the period of greatest teratogenic risk.**
>
> **Organogenesis** occurs during the embryonic stage. Teratogens include ionizing radiation, alcohol, certain medications (isotretinoin, valproic acid, warfarin, ACE inhibitors, tetracycline), infections (rubella, CMV, toxoplasmosis, syphilis), maternal conditions (uncontrolled diabetes, PKU), and environmental toxins (lead, mercury). The placenta is a **pass-through, NOT a barrier** — almost everything the pregnant person ingests crosses to the fetus.

## 03 SIGNS & SYMPTOMS OF PREGNANCY

Pregnancy signs are classified into three categories. Only **positive signs** confirm pregnancy with 100% certainty.

| Category | Definition & Key Examples | Clinical Significance |
|---|---|---|
| **PRESUMPTIVE** (Subjective) | **Definition:** Signs the pregnant person perceives themselves.<br>**Examples:** Amenorrhea (most obvious); nausea/vomiting (~6–12 wk, ≥90%); breast enlargement & tenderness; urinary frequency without dysuria; excessive fatigue; quickening (fetal movement felt ~18–20 wk). | **NOT diagnostic.** Can be caused by non-pregnancy conditions: stress, endocrine disorders, GI illness, infection, anemia, oral contraceptives, eating disorders, or cancer. |
| **PROBABLE** (Objective) | **Definition:** Signs detected on physical exam by a provider.<br>**Physical signs:** Hegar sign (softening of lower uterine segment, ~6 wk); Goodell sign (softening of cervix, ~5 wk); Chadwick sign (bluish-purple cervix/vagina, ~6–8 wk); ballottement (~16–28 wk); Braxton Hicks contractions (16–28 wk); uterine enlargement; abdominal enlargement.<br>**Pregnancy test:** urine or serum hCG. <5 mIU/mL = negative; >25 mIU/mL = positive. Doubles every 29–53 h; peaks 8–10 weeks (corresponds to morning sickness). | **Strongly suggestive but NOT 100%.** Uterine tumors, pelvic congestion, molar pregnancy can mimic. hCG elevated in choriocarcinoma or hydatidiform mole. Home tests may be false-negative if done too early. |
| **POSITIVE** (Diagnostic) | **Definition:** Signs directly attributable to the fetus — only caused by a developing fetus.<br>**Examples:** Ultrasound visualization of embryo/fetus (~4–5 wk transvaginal); fetal heart tones by Doppler (~10–12 wk) or fetoscope (~17–20 wk); fetal movement palpated by experienced clinician (~20 wk); FHR visualized on ultrasound (~5–6 wk). | **100% confirms pregnancy.** No other condition produces these findings. Once identified, pregnancy is definitively diagnosed. |

## 04 GTPAL — OBSTETRIC HISTORY DOCUMENTATION

The GTPAL system provides a precise, standardized way to communicate a patient's complete reproductive history.

| Letter | Definition | How to Count |
|---|---|---|
| **G** | Gravida — total number of pregnancies, including the current pregnancy | Count every pregnancy regardless of outcome or duration. Nulligravida = never pregnant. Primigravida = 1st pregnancy. Multigravida = ≥3rd. |
| **T** | Term births — births at 38–42 weeks' gestation | Count birth events, not infants. Twins at term = T+1 (one birth event). |
| **P** | Preterm births — births between >20 weeks and <37 weeks | Viability threshold = 20 weeks. Births before 20 weeks count as abortion (A), not preterm (P). |
| **A** | Abortions — pregnancies ending before 20 weeks (spontaneous or therapeutic) | Includes spontaneous abortions (miscarriages) and elective/induced abortions. Does not distinguish type in the number. |
| **L** | Living children — number of children currently alive | Count each child individually. Twins = L+2. A child who died after birth is NOT counted in L. |

### GTPAL Worked Examples

| Scenario | GTPAL | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| Mary is pregnant for the 4th time. She had one abortion at 8 weeks, a daughter born at 40 weeks, and a son born at 34 weeks. | **G4 (1102)** | G=4 pregnancies total. T=1 (daughter at 40 wk). P=1 (son at 34 wk). A=1 (abortion at 8 wk). L=2 living children. |
| Anna is pregnant for the 1st time. She has never been pregnant before. | **G1 (0000)** | Primigravida. No prior births, abortions, or living children. |
| Jane is pregnant for the 3rd time. She has twin boys born at 38 weeks and had one miscarriage at 10 weeks. | **G3 (1012)** | G=3 pregnancies. T=1 (twins = 1 birth event). A=1 (miscarriage). L=2 (twins living). |
| Keisha is pregnant for the 5th time. She has 3 children living: born at 39 weeks, 36 weeks, and 41 weeks. One child died at age 2 (SIDS). She had one elective abortion at 12 weeks. | **G5 (2133)** | G=5. T=2 (39 wk + 41 wk). P=1 (36 wk). A=1 (abortion). L=3 living (the child who died is NOT counted in L). |

### Key OB Terminology

- **Gravida/para notation:** "G4P2" = 4 pregnancies, 2 births after 20 weeks. **GTPAL notation:** "G4 (1112)" provides detail.
- **Nullipara (para 0):** never given birth after 20 weeks. **Primipara:** one birth after 20 weeks. **Multipara:** ≥2 births after 20 weeks.
- **Multiple births count as ONE birth event** for T, P, A. But count **each living child individually** for L.
- T + P + A should equal (G − 1). L ≤ (T + P). A deceased child subtracts from L.

> **■ TAKE NOTE! Always include the current pregnancy in the gravida count.**
>
> GTPAL is the preferred documentation method because it provides more clinical detail than gravida/para alone. When a child died after birth (e.g., SIDS, accident, neonatal death), they are subtracted from L. Do NOT subtract from T, P, or A — those reflect the birth outcome, not survival.

## 05 EDD CALCULATION — NAGELE'S RULE

The **estimated date of delivery (EDD)**, also called EDB or EDC, is calculated from the first day of the LMP. Margin of error: **±2 weeks**. Birth 2 weeks before or after EDD is considered normal.

### Naegele's Rule — Step by Step

- **Step 1:** Identify the first day of the last normal menstrual period (LMP).
- **Step 2:** Subtract 3 months from the LMP date.
- **Step 3:** Add 7 days.
- **Step 4:** Add 1 year (adjust year forward).
- **Alternative:** Add 7 days to LMP, then count forward 9 months (adjust year as needed).

### EDD Calculation Practice

| LMP | −3 Months | +7 Days | +1 Year | EDD (±2 wk) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| October 14, 2023 | July 14, 2023 | July 21, 2023 | July 21, 2024 | **July 21, 2024** |
| March 5, 2024 | December 5, 2023 | December 12, 2023 | December 12, 2024 | **December 12, 2024** |
| January 20, 2024 | October 20, 2023 | October 27, 2023 | October 27, 2024 | **October 27, 2024** |
| June 30, 2024 | March 30, 2024 | April 6, 2024 | April 6, 2025 | **April 6, 2025** |

### Important EDD Considerations

- Naegele's rule assumes a **28-day cycle** with ovulation on day 14. Less accurate with irregular cycles, breastfeeding, or conception before first postpartum menses.
- A **gestational/birth calculator wheel** can also be used.
- **Ultrasound** (especially first-trimester transvaginal) is the **most accurate** method of dating a pregnancy.
- If prenatal care begins late, second-trimester fetal head circumference via ultrasound can predict EDD; transvaginal ultrasound identifies intrauterine pregnancy ~4 weeks after LMP.

> **■ TAKE NOTE! Ovulation always occurs 14 days before the next menses, regardless of cycle length.**
>
> For a 35-day cycle: ovulation on day 21 (35 − 14 = 21). For a 21-day cycle: ovulation on day 7 (21 − 14 = 7). This is why Naegele's rule is less accurate with irregular cycles — it assumes ovulation on day 14. Always verify EDD with ultrasound when available.

## 06 THE FIRST PRENATAL VISIT

The initial prenatal visit establishes baseline data, screens for risk factors, estimates gestational age, and begins patient education. Goal: reduce adverse health effects for the pregnant person, fetus, and newborn. Can be delivered individually or through **CenteringPregnancy** (group model with improved outcomes).

| Component | Key Elements |
|---|---|
| **Health History** | **Reason for seeking care:** LMP date; presumptive/probable signs; hCG confirmation.<br>**Past medical/surgical history:** chronic conditions (diabetes, HTN, thyroid, cardiac, seizure disorders); allergies; mental health.<br>**Personal history:** occupation, exercise, sleep, nutrition, substance use (alcohol, tobacco, drugs), CAM therapies.<br>**Reproductive history:** menstrual history (menarche age, cycle length, flow); contraceptive history; obstetric history (GTPAL); gynecologic history (surgeries, STIs, safer sex practices).<br>**Family & partner history:** genetic disorders, chronic diseases, pregnancy complications; partner's blood type and Rh factor. |
| **Physical Exam** | **Vital signs, height & weight:** baseline BMI. Report weight <100 lb or >200 lb or sudden gain.<br>**Head-to-toe:** Head/neck (nasal edema, gum hypertrophy, thyroid); chest (soft systolic murmur normal; breast changes — size, tenderness, pigmentation, colostrum by 3rd trimester); abdomen (striae, linea nigra, fundal height); extremities (dependent edema, varicosities, DVT signs).<br>**Pelvic exam:** external (lesions, discharge); internal (Goodell, Hegar, Chadwick signs); Pap smear; STI cultures; bimanual exam.<br>**Pelvic measurements** (primigravida): diagonal conjugate (≥12.5 cm), true/obstetric conjugate (~11 cm), ischial tuberosity diameter (≥10.5 cm). |
| **Laboratory Tests** | **CBC:** Hgb 12–14 g/dL, Hct 42% ±5%, platelets 150,000–450,000.<br>**Blood type & Rh factor:** identify Rh-negatives (RhoGAM at 28 wk + postpartum if infant Rh+).<br>**Rubella titer:** ≤1:8 = not immune; immunize postpartum, avoid exposure during pregnancy.<br>**Hepatitis B (HbsAg), HIV** (opt-out recommended), **VDRL/RPR** (syphilis).<br>**Cervical cultures:** gonorrhea, chlamydia; GBS at 35–37 weeks.<br>**Urinalysis:** albumin, glucose, ketones, nitrites, bacteria.<br>**Pap smear** if due.<br>**Ultrasound:** confirm intrauterine pregnancy, viability, gestational age, number of fetuses. |

## 07 MATERNAL PHYSIOLOGIC ADAPTATIONS BY SYSTEM

Virtually every body system changes during pregnancy. Adaptations are driven by hormones (estrogen, progesterone, hCS) and mechanical effects of the enlarging uterus. Most are reversible after birth.

| Body System | Key Adaptations During Pregnancy | Clinical Implications |
|---|---|---|
| **Reproductive** | **Uterus:** size ×20; weight 70 g → 1,100 g; capacity 10 mL → 5,000 mL. Fundus: 12 wk = symphysis; 16 wk = midway; 20 wk = umbilicus; 36 wk = xiphoid. Lightening ~38–40 wk.<br>**Cervix:** Goodell sign (~5 wk); mucus plug; Chadwick sign (~6–8 wk).<br>**Vagina:** ↑ vascularity, leukorrhea, ↑ pH → ↑ candidiasis.<br>**Breasts:** ↑ size/tenderness; Montgomery tubercles; colostrum by 3rd trimester; striae. | Fundal height (cm) ≈ weeks gestation (18–32 wk most accurate). Supine hypotension after 20 wk → position L lateral. Lightening = easier breathing, ↑ urinary frequency. |
| **Cardiovascular** | **↑ Blood volume** 30–50% (1,100–1,600 mL); peaks 30–34 wk. Plasma ↑ > RBC → **physiologic anemia**. ↑ Cardiac output 30–40% (peaks 20–24 wk). ↑ HR 10–15 bpm (14–20 wk). ↓ BP (diastolic) mid-pregnancy (progesterone vasodilation): ↓ 5–10 mm Hg. ↑ Clotting factors + venous stasis → **hypercoagulable state** (↑ DVT risk). | Physiologic anemia = NORMAL adaptation. Iron supplementation still needed. ↑ BP is ABNORMAL → investigate gestational HTN. Monitor for DVT: unilateral calf pain, edema, warmth. |
| **Respiratory** | ↑ Tidal volume 30–40%. ↑ O2 consumption 20–30%. Diaphragm rises ~4 cm. Thoracic breathing replaces abdominal. Chest circumference ↑ 2–3 in. Mild hyperventilation → mild hypocapnia. Nasal stuffiness, epistaxis (estrogen). | Dyspnea common (60–75%) from diaphragm elevation. Asthma patients at higher decompensation risk. Nasal congestion is normal — not URI. |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Progesterone → ↓ LES tone → **pyrosis (heartburn)**. ↓ Peristalsis → constipation, bloating, hemorrhoids. Delayed gastric emptying. ↑ Gallstone risk. Gums: hyperemic, friable (estrogen). Ptyalism.<br>**Nausea/vomiting** (≥90%) begins ~6 wk, linked to hCG peak, resolves by 20 wk. | Morning sickness = normal; hyperemesis gravidarum = >5% weight loss + dehydration. Small frequent meals; ginger; B6 + doxylamine. Dental care important — gum bleeding is hormonal. |
| **Renal/Urinary** | ↑ GFR → ↑ urine output. Renal pelvis and ureters dilate (progesterone); right ureter more dilated. Urinary stasis → ↑ UTI/pyelonephritis risk. Kidneys enlarge ~1–1.5 cm. Frequency in 1st tri (uterine pressure) and 3rd tri (engagement). | Trace glucosuria/proteinuria may be normal (↑ GFR). Screen for asymptomatic bacteriuria. Teach: empty bladder frequently, increase fluids, wipe front to back. |
| **Musculoskeletal** | Lordosis compensates for enlarging uterus. Relaxin + progesterone → joint laxity, symphysis pubis widening. Waddling gait. Diastasis recti possible. Round ligament pain: sharp groin pain with position change. | Back pain common; teach body mechanics, pelvic rock exercises. Avoid heavy lifting. Round ligament pain benign — reassure, slow position changes. |
| **Integumentary** | Hyperpigmentation: linea nigra, melasma ("mask of pregnancy"), areolae darkening. Striae gravidarum (stretch marks) on abdomen, breasts, thighs. Palmar erythema, spider angiomas. ↑ Hair growth; postpartum telogen effluvium. | Most skin changes fade postpartum but may not completely disappear. Sun worsens melasma → teach sun protection. Striae influenced by genetics and weight gain rate. |
| **Endocrine/Metabolic** | Placenta = major endocrine organ: hCG, hCS, progesterone, estrogen. ↑ Prolactin (lactation); ↓ FSH/LH (anovulation). ↑ Insulin resistance (hCS) → may unmask GDM. ↑ BMR ~15–20%.<br>Weight gain: 25–35 lb (normal BMI); 28–40 lb (underweight); 11–20 lb (overweight). ~300 additional kcal/day in 2nd/3rd trimesters. | Screen GDM at 24–28 wk. Slight thyroid enlargement normal (estrogen → ↑ TBG). Monitor weight gain pattern: 2–4 lb 1st tri, then ~1 lb/week. |

> **■ TAKE NOTE! Supine hypotensive syndrome is a preventable emergency after 20 weeks.**
>
> The gravid uterus compresses the inferior vena cava when the patient lies supine, reducing venous return, cardiac output, and BP. **Symptoms:** dizziness, lightheadedness, nausea, tachycardia, hypotension, syncope. **Nursing action:** position patient in **LEFT LATERAL** or semi-Fowler's. Place a wedge under the right hip if supine is required. This is a mechanical complication, not a disease.

## 08 FOLLOW-UP PRENATAL VISITS

Continuous prenatal care is essential for a successful pregnancy outcome. Standard schedule for a healthy pregnancy:

### Visit Schedule

- **Every 4 weeks** from conception to 28 weeks.
- **Every 2 weeks** from 29 to 36 weeks.
- **Every week** from 37 weeks to birth.

### Assessments at Every Visit

- **Weight & BP:** compare with baseline. Sudden weight gain or ↑ BP needs investigation.
- **Urine dipstick:** protein, glucose, ketones, nitrites — screen for preeclampsia, GDM, UTI.
- **Fundal height** (McDonald method): symphysis to fundus in cm. ≈ weeks gestation 18–32 wk. Drops with lightening after 36 wk.
- **FHR:** 110–160 bpm by Doppler. Locate fetal back for clearest sound. Differentiate from maternal pulse.
- **Fetal movement (quickening):** first felt 16–22 wk (earlier multiparas). "Count to 10" — if >2 h, contact provider.
- **Leopold maneuvers:** assess fetal lie, presentation, position — especially after 36 weeks.

### Fundal Height Quick Reference

| GA | Fundal Location | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 12 wk | Symphysis pubis | Fundus just palpable above pubic bone |
| 16 wk | Midway symphysis → umbilicus | ~16 cm; quickening may begin |
| 20 wk | At umbilicus | ~20 cm; cm ≈ weeks (most accurate 18–32 wk) |
| 28 wk | ~28 cm | GDM screen (24–28 wk); RhoGAM if Rh− at 28 wk |
| 32 wk | ~32 cm | Still correlates with weeks |
| 36 wk | Xiphoid process (~36 cm) | Highest level; may cause dyspnea |
| 38–40 wk | Drops (lightening) | Fetal head engages pelvis; easier breathing, ↑ urinary frequency |

### Danger Signs During Pregnancy — Patient Must Report Immediately

- 🔴 **First trimester:** Spotting/bleeding (miscarriage); severe persistent vomiting (hyperemesis); fever >100°F; painful urination; lower abdominal pain + dizziness + shoulder pain (ruptured ectopic).
- 🔴 **Second trimester:** Regular uterine contractions (preterm labor); back pain; sudden gush/leakage of fluid (PROM); absent fetal movement >12 hours; calf pain with dorsiflexion = DVT.
- 🔴 **Third trimester:** Sudden weight gain; periorbital/facial/hand edema; severe epigastric pain; headache with visual changes (preeclampsia); decreased fetal movement >24 hours; late-pregnancy bleeding (previa/abruption).

> **■ TAKE NOTE! Fetal movement is a key indicator of fetal well-being.**
>
> **Quickening:** first perception of fetal movement (16–22 wk). **"Count to 10" method:** Lie on left side, count fetal movements at the same time daily. Record time to feel 10 movements. **If >2 hours to reach 10 movements → contact provider immediately.** Decreased fetal movement = possible fetal hypoxia — the fetus conserves oxygen by reducing activity.
