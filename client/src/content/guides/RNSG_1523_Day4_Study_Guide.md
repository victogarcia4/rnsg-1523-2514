## 02  SCREENING vs DIAGNOSTIC TESTS — THE CRITICAL DISTINCTION

Screening tests identify **RISK**; diagnostic tests **CONFIRM** a diagnosis. Confusing the two is a common source of error and patient anxiety.

| Feature | Screening Test | Diagnostic Test |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | Identifies patients at increased RISK for a condition | CONFIRMS or rules out a specific diagnosis |
| Method | Noninvasive: blood test, urine test, ultrasound | May be invasive: amniocentesis, CVS; or definitive ultrasound |
| Accuracy | May have false positives/negatives; NOT definitive | Definitive diagnosis (e.g., karyotype of fetal cells) |
| Risk | Minimal to none | Procedure-related risks (miscarriage, infection, fetal injury) |
| Examples | MSAFP, triple/quad/penta marker, cffDNA, nuchal translucency, GDM screen, GBS culture, CBC, rubella titer | Amniocentesis, CVS, detailed anatomy ultrasound, fetal blood sampling |
| Result meaning | Abnormal = needs further diagnostic follow-up | Confirms condition; guides definitive management decisions |
| Key teaching | Screening is OPTIONAL, not mandatory; abnormal does NOT mean the baby is affected | Requires informed consent; weigh risk vs benefit; nondirective counseling |

## 03  PRENATAL SCREENING TESTS

Offered to ALL pregnant patients. Abnormal results require diagnostic follow-up — they do NOT mean the fetus is affected.

| Screening Test | Timing | What It Detects | Nursing Implications |
|---|---|---|---|
| **CBC** | 1st visit (baseline); repeat ~28 wk | Anemia (Hgb/Hct), infection (WBC), platelet/clotting status | Baseline for comparison. Hgb <11 g/dL (1st/3rd tri) or <10.5 (2nd tri) suggests anemia. Treat iron-deficiency. Physiologic anemia (hemodilution) is normal — Hgb/Hct decrease, not a true deficiency. |
| **Blood type & Rh factor** | 1st visit | ABO group and Rh status; Rh incompatibility risk | Identify Rh-negative patients → RhoGAM at 28 wk + postpartum if infant Rh+. Screen for atypical antibodies if prior transfusion/pregnancy. |
| **Rubella titer** | 1st visit | Immunity to rubella (German measles) | Titer ≤1:8 = not immune. Immunize POSTPARTUM (live vaccine contraindicated in pregnancy). Advise avoiding people with undiagnosed rash during pregnancy. |
| **Hepatitis B (HbsAg)** | 1st visit | Active/chronic hepatitis B infection | If positive → newborn receives HBIG + HBV vaccine within 12 h of birth to prevent vertical transmission. |
| **HIV** | 1st visit (opt-out) | HIV infection | Opt-out screening recommended by ACOG. If positive → antiretroviral therapy reduces vertical transmission to <1–2%. Counsel confidentially; refer to HIV specialist. |
| **Syphilis (VDRL/RPR)** | 1st visit; repeat in 3rd tri if high risk | Syphilis infection | Treat with penicillin G (prevents congenital syphilis). Early treatment prevents fetal demise, neonatal complications. |
| **Urinalysis** | Every visit | Protein, glucose, ketones, nitrites, bacteria | Protein → preeclampsia screening. Glucose → GDM screening. Nitrites/bacteria → UTI. Trace protein/glucose may be normal (increased GFR). |
| **Cervical cultures** | 1st visit; GBS at 35–37 wk | Chlamydia, gonorrhea; Group B Streptococcus | Chlamydia/gonorrhea → treat to prevent neonatal conjunctivitis, pneumonia. GBS positive → intrapartum antibiotic prophylaxis (penicillin) to prevent neonatal GBS sepsis. |
| **MSAFP** | 15–20 wk (optimal 16–18 wk) | ↑ = neural tube defects, omphalocele, gastroschisis, multiple gestation; ↓ = trisomy 21/18 | Confirm accurate dating, weight, race, # fetuses before interpreting. False positives common if dating incorrect. Abnormal → detailed ultrasound, genetic counseling. |
| **Triple/Quad/Penta marker** | 15–22 wk | Risk of Down syndrome (trisomy 21), trisomy 18, open neural tube defects | Triple = AFP + hCG + uE3. Quad = + inhibin A (improves Down syndrome detection). Penta = + h-hCG. Abnormal → offer diagnostic testing (amniocentesis) + genetic counseling. |
| **cffDNA (cell-free fetal DNA)** | ≥9–10 wk | Trisomy 21, 18, 13; fetal sex; some microdeletions | Noninvasive (maternal blood). High sensitivity/specificity for trisomy 21. Screening only — positive result still requires diagnostic confirmation (amniocentesis/CVS). |
| **Nuchal translucency (NT) ultrasound** | 11–14 wk | ↑ NT = trisomy 21, 18, 13; cardiac anomalies | Fluid-filled space at fetal neck measured via ultrasound. ↑ thickness associated with chromosomal and structural anomalies. Combined with serum markers for 1st trimester screen. |
| **GDM screening** | 24–28 wk (earlier if risk factors) | Gestational diabetes | Two-step: 1-hr 50-g glucose challenge (nonfasting). If ≥140 mg/dL → 3-hr 100-g OGTT. One-step: 75-g 2-hr OGTT. Diagnose if ≥2 values elevated (3-hr). Risk: obesity, age >35, family hx, prior GDM. |

## 04  PRENATAL DIAGNOSTIC TESTS

Diagnostic tests are definitive but carry procedure-related risks. They require informed consent and careful nursing management.

| Diagnostic Test | Timing | Procedure / What It Detects | Risks & Nursing Management |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ultrasound (diagnostic)** | 1st tri: transvaginal; 18–20 wk: anatomy; 32–36 wk: growth/position | High-frequency sound waves via abdominal transducer or vaginal probe. Visualizes gestational age, fetal number, viability, anatomy, placental location, amniotic fluid, presentation. | Noninvasive, no known risks. Early pregnancy: full bladder. Explain procedure; cold gel sensation. Reassure no radiation. First-trimester ultrasound is most accurate for dating. |
| **Doppler flow studies** | 2nd/3rd tri (high-risk) | Measures blood flow velocity in umbilical/fetal vessels. Detects absent/reversed diastolic flow (fetal compromise). | Indications: IUGR, preeclampsia, suspected cardiac defects. Noninvasive, no contraindications. Absent/reversed diastolic flow → fetal compromise requiring close surveillance/early delivery. |
| **Amniocentesis** | 15–20 wk (optimal; adequate fluid ~150 mL) | Transabdominal needle (22-gauge) aspirates amniotic fluid under ultrasound. Fetal cells analyzed for chromosomal abnormalities (>40 types), inborn errors of metabolism, NTDs (AFP + AChE), fetal lung maturity (L/S ratio). | Risks: miscarriage (~1/300–500), infection, fluid leakage, Rh sensitization, fetal injury. Nursing: informed consent; empty bladder; 20-min EFM baseline before; RhoGAM if Rh−; VS + FHR q15 min ×1 h after; teach report fever, fluid leak, bleeding, contractions, ↓fetal movement. Results up to 3 weeks. |
| **CVS (Chorionic Villus Sampling)** | 10–13 wk (earlier than amnio) | Transcervical catheter OR transabdominal needle aspirates chorionic villi (placental tissue, embryonic origin). Detects chromosomal disorders, cystic fibrosis, enzyme deficiencies, sex-linked disorders (hemophilia, Tay–Sachs). | Risks: miscarriage (~1/200–400), limb reduction defects (if <10 wk), bleeding, infection. Does NOT detect neural tube defects (no amniotic fluid AFP). Nursing: informed consent; full bladder (transcervical); RhoGAM if Rh−; no strenuous activity ×48 h; teach report fever, cramping, bleeding. |
| **NST (Nonstress Test)** | ≥32 wk (high-risk surveillance) | External fetal monitor records FHR + uterine activity 20–40 min. Fetal movement → FHR acceleration (normal). Assesses uteroplacental function indirectly. | Noninvasive, no side effects. Patient eats before test. Left lateral/semi-Fowler (avoid supine hypotension). Patient pushes event marker with fetal movement. Reactive = ≥2 accelerations (≥15 bpm ×15 sec) in 20 min. Nonreactive → further evaluation (BPP, CST, vibroacoustic stimulation). |
| **BPP (Biophysical Profile)** | 3rd tri (high-risk) | Ultrasound + NST. 5 components (2 pts each): fetal breathing, fetal movement, fetal tone, amniotic fluid volume, NST reactivity. | Score 8–10 = normal/reassuring; 6 = equivocal → repeat or deliver; ≤4 = abnormal → consider delivery. More expensive; requires trained personnel. Lower false-negative rate than NST alone. Explain purpose; provide support; NST completed before BPP. |

---

## 05  NST INTERPRETATION

| Result | Criteria | Interpretation / Next Step |
|---|---|---|
| **REACTIVE (reassuring)** | ≥2 FHR accelerations: ≥15 bpm above baseline, lasting ≥15 seconds, within 20 minutes | Indicates adequate fetal oxygenation and intact neurologic regulation. No further testing needed. Normal finding — reassure patient. |
| **NONREACTIVE** | Fails to meet reactive criteria within 40 minutes of monitoring | May indicate fetal hypoxia, but >50% are FALSE POSITIVES (fetal sleep cycle, prematurity <32 wk, maternal smoking, maternal drug ingestion, fetal CNS anomalies). → Repeat test, vibroacoustic stimulation, or proceed to BPP or contraction stress test (CST). |

## 06  BIOPHYSICAL PROFILE (BPP) SCORING

| Component (2 pts each) | Normal Criteria (score 2) | Abnormal (score 0) |
|---|---|---|
| Fetal breathing movements | ≥1 episode of rhythmic breathing ≥30 seconds in 30 min | Absent or <30 sec |
| Fetal body movements | ≥3 discrete body/limb movements in 30 min | <3 movements |
| Fetal tone | ≥1 episode of full extension + flexion of limb/trunk | No extension-flexion; flaccid |
| Amniotic fluid volume | ≥1 pocket of fluid >2 cm in 2 perpendicular planes | No pocket >2 cm (oligohydramnios) |
| NST | Reactive NST | Nonreactive NST |

**Score interpretation:** 8–10 = normal/reassuring (if AFV adequate). 6 = equivocal — repeat in 24 h or deliver if near term. ≤4 = abnormal — strongly associated with fetal compromise; consider expedited delivery. **Modified BPP** = NST + amniotic fluid volume only (acute + chronic markers) — excellent false-negative rate comparable to full BPP. **Fetal hypoxia progression:** the fetus loses functions in reverse developmental order — tone first (acquired 8 wk), then movement (9 wk), then breathing (20 wk), then FHR reactivity (24 wk).

## 07  ETHICAL & LEGAL CONSIDERATIONS IN PRENATAL TESTING

| Principle | Application to Prenatal Testing |
|---|---|
| **Autonomy** | Patient has the RIGHT to make decisions about their own body and pregnancy. Accepting or declining any test is the patient’s choice. Nurse provides information, not coercion. |
| **Informed consent** | Invasive procedures (amniocentesis, CVS) require explicit informed consent. Patient must understand: purpose, procedure, risks (miscarriage rate), benefits, alternatives (including no testing), and timing of results. |
| **Beneficence / Nonmaleficence** | Weigh benefit of early diagnosis vs risk of procedure-related loss. Screening provides information without procedure risk — balance against anxiety of false positives. |
| **Confidentiality & privacy** | Genetic information is sensitive. Protect test results. GINA (2008) prohibits genetic discrimination by health insurers and employers (does NOT cover life/disability/LTC insurance). |
| **Nondirective counseling** | Present ALL options without bias. Support the patient’s decision whether to test, and their decision if an abnormality is found (continue pregnancy vs termination). Refer to genetic counselor for complex cases. |
| **Justice** | Ensure equitable access to testing and counseling regardless of socioeconomic status. Insurance coverage varies — help patients understand their options and financial resources. |

> **■ TAKE NOTE! The decision to test, and what to do with results, rests solely with the patient.**
> 
> Prenatal testing raises profound ethical questions. A positive screening result does NOT diagnose — it increases risk. Patients may choose NOT to have testing (some would not consider termination regardless of results). Respect the decision. For a confirmed abnormality: provide accurate information, offer genetic counseling, and support the family’s values. The decision to continue or terminate a pregnancy rests solely with the patient/family — the nurse’s role is nondirective support, not to impose personal beliefs.

## 08  INTEGRATED ANTEPARTUM SURVEILLANCE CASE (NGN)

**Unfolding case — prioritize hypotheses, take action, evaluate outcomes.** A 36-year-old G2P1 (previous term vaginal delivery) at 32 weeks’ gestation presents for a routine follow-up. She reports decreased fetal movement over the past 24 hours and a mild frontal headache. Vital signs: BP 148/92, HR 92, RR 18, Temp 98.8°F. Urine dipstick: protein 2+, glucose negative. Fundal height 30 cm. FHR 150 bpm.

**1. Recognize cues** — Decreased fetal movement (danger sign), BP 148/92 (elevated), proteinuria 2+, headache. Fundal height 30 cm at 32 wk (slightly small).

**2. Analyze cues** — Elevated BP + proteinuria + headache = possible preeclampsia. Decreased fetal movement = possible fetal hypoxia. SGA fundal height = possible IUGR.

**3. Prioritize hypotheses** — PRIMARY: preeclampsia (maternal risk). CONCURRENT: fetal well-being concern (hypoxia/IUGR). Preeclampsia can compromise placental perfusion → fetal hypoxia.

**4. Generate solutions** — Notify provider STAT. Obtain NST immediately to assess fetal well-being. Monitor BP. Assess for other preeclampsia signs (visual changes, epigastric pain, hyperreflexia, edema). Prepare for possible BPP and preeclampsia labs (CBC, platelets, LFTs, creatinine, uric acid, 24-hr urine protein).

**5. Take action** — Position patient left lateral (optimize placental perfusion). Initiate continuous fetal monitoring. Draw labs. Notify provider. Document. Anticipate magnesium sulfate (seizure prophylaxis) if severe features or eclampsia.

**6. Evaluate outcomes** — Reassess BP. Evaluate NST reactivity and fetal movement. Review lab results (platelets, LFTs for HELLP). Determine if delivery is indicated. Provide ongoing patient education and emotional support.

## 09  SELF-CHECK (NGN-STYLE)

Choose the **single best** answer.

**Q1.** The nurse is explaining the difference between screening and diagnostic testing to a pregnant patient. Which statement by the patient indicates correct understanding?

- A. "A screening test tells me for sure if my baby has a problem."
- B. "An abnormal screening test means my baby definitely has a genetic disorder."
- C. "Screening tests identify risk, but I need a diagnostic test to confirm a diagnosis."
- D. "Diagnostic tests are always noninvasive and carry no risks."

**Q2.** A patient at 26 weeks’ gestation is scheduled for a 1-hour glucose challenge test. Which instruction is most accurate?

- A. "You must fast for 12 hours before this test."
- B. "You will drink 50 g of glucose, and your blood will be drawn 1 hour later."
- C. "This test confirms gestational diabetes, and no further testing will be needed."
- D. "You should exercise vigorously before the test to lower your blood sugar."

**Q3.** The nurse is interpreting a nonstress test (NST) on a patient at 34 weeks. The monitor shows two fetal heart rate accelerations of 20 bpm lasting 20 seconds each during a 20-minute period. How should the nurse document this result?

- A. Nonreactive — requires immediate cesarean delivery
- B. Reactive — a reassuring result indicating adequate fetal oxygenation
- C. Nonreactive — the fetus is sleeping and needs stimulation
- D. Equivocal — repeat in 24 hours

**Q4.** A patient is Rh-negative and undergoes amniocentesis at 17 weeks’ gestation. Which intervention is essential after the procedure?

- A. Administer RhoGAM (anti-D immune globulin) to prevent Rh sensitization
- B. Administer betamethasone to mature fetal lungs
- C. Give magnesium sulfate for seizure prophylaxis
- D. Restrict all oral fluids for 24 hours

**Q5.** A patient’s biophysical profile (BPP) score is 4 out of 10. What is the correct interpretation?

- A. Normal result — no further action needed
- B. Equivocal result — repeat in 24 hours and continue surveillance
- C. Abnormal result — associated with fetal compromise; consider expedited delivery
- D. Inconclusive — the test must be repeated immediately to confirm

**Q6.** A patient declines amniocentesis after an abnormal quad screen, stating she would not terminate the pregnancy regardless of results. What is the most appropriate nursing response?

- A. "You are making the wrong decision — you need this information to prepare."
- B. "That is your choice. I will document your decision and continue to support your prenatal care."
- C. "Your provider will be disappointed — this is the standard of care."
- D. "You should reconsider; knowing the diagnosis is always better."

## 10  ANSWER KEY & RATIONALE

| Q | Ans | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 | **C** | Screening tests identify RISK (noninvasive, may have false positives). Diagnostic tests CONFIRM diagnosis (may be invasive, definitive). A and B incorrectly equate screening with diagnosis. D is false — amniocentesis and CVS are invasive with procedure-related risks. |
| Q2 | **B** | The 1-hour glucose challenge test uses a 50-g glucose load (nonfasting), with blood drawn 1 hour later. A is false — no fasting required for the 50-g challenge (only the 3-hr OGTT requires fasting). C is false — the 1-hr test is a SCREENING test; abnormal results require the 3-hr OGTT to diagnose. D is inappropriate — exercise would falsely lower results. |
| Q3 | **B** | Reactive NST criteria: ≥2 accelerations of ≥15 bpm lasting ≥15 seconds in 20 minutes. Two accelerations of 20 bpm × 20 sec = REACTIVE (reassuring). A is wrong — reactive NST requires no intervention. C is wrong — this is reactive, not nonreactive. D is wrong — reactive requires no repeat. |
| Q4 | **A** | Rh-negative patients undergoing invasive procedures (amniocentesis, CVS) risk fetomaternal hemorrhage and Rh sensitization. RhoGAM (anti-D immune globulin) must be administered after the procedure. B (betamethasone) is for fetal lung maturity in threatened preterm birth. C (magnesium sulfate) is for preeclampsia seizure prophylaxis. D has no basis. |
| Q5 | **C** | BPP ≤4/10 = abnormal — strongly associated with fetal compromise; consider expedited delivery. 8–10 = normal; 6 = equivocal (repeat or deliver). A and D are incorrect for a score of 4. B (equivocal) applies to score of 6, not 4. |
| Q6 | **B** | Autonomy — the patient has the right to decline testing. The nurse provides nondirective support and respects the decision. A, C, and D are coercive and violate the ethical principle of autonomy. Many patients choose not to test if they would not terminate regardless of results. |
