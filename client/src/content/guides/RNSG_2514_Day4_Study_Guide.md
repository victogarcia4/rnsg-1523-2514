03 2. HYPERBILIRUBINEMIA (JAUNDICE)
Definition: total serum bilirubin (TSB) >5 mg/dL. ~80% of newborns appear jaundiced. Caused by breakdown of RBCs
(short-lived fetal hemoglobin).
Unconjugated (indirect) = fat-soluble, toxic. Conjugated (direct) = water-soluble, nontoxic. Newborn liver must conjugate
bilirubin on its own after birth.
FEATURE PHYSIOLOGIC (BENIGN) PATHOLOGIC (SIGNIFICANT)
Onset After 24 hours of age Within first 24 hours of age
Peak Day 3-4 Variable
TSB level 8-14 mg/dL peak >95th percentile; rising >5 mg/dL/day or >0.2 mg/dL/h
Treatment Early frequent feedings, reassurance Phototherapy; exchange transfusion if phototherapy
fails
Danger Rare Severe TSB ≥25 → BIND/kernicterus (bilirubin
crosses blood-brain barrier → irreversible brain
damage)
Phototherapy
- Mechanism: blue LED light converts bilirubin into water-soluble isomers excreted via bile/urine (without conjugation).
- Nursing: expose maximum skin • cover eyes and genitals • turn every 2 h • remove from lights only for feedings •
monitor temp every 4 h • watch for dehydration • stools become loose and green.
- At home: lights 12-30 in above newborn; eye patches; keep skin clean/dry.
TAKE NOTE! Jaundice before 24 hours of age = pathologic until proven otherwise - report immediately. Do not rely on visual
estimate; use TCB or TSB.
04 3. INFANT OF A DIABETIC MOTHER (IDM)
Definition: newborn of a person with pregestational (type 1/2) or gestational diabetes. Up to 90% of diabetic pregnancies
are GDM.
Cause: maternal hyperglycemia → fetal hyperinsulinism → excessive growth (or growth restriction if vascular
compromise).
Key problems & critical lab values
PROBLEM MECHANISM LAB VALUE
Hypoglycemia Abrupt cessation of maternal glucose + ongoing fetal <50 mg/dL (AAP: <40)
insulin
Macrosomia Fetal hyperinsulinism acts as growth factor >4,000 g (>90th percentile)

Priority nursing actions
- Blood glucose hourly for first 4 hours, then every 3-4 h. <40 mg/dL = hypoglycemia.
- Early feeding (breast milk or formula) every 2-3 h; if unstable, IV dextrose.
- Neutral thermal environment - cold stress increases glucose demand.
- Monitor for respiratory distress (RDS/TTN), calcium/magnesium, bilirubin, hematocrit.
- Look for birth trauma (shoulder dystocia, fractures, brachial plexus).
05 4. NEONATAL SEPSIS
Definition: bloodstream infection within first 28 days. Mortality rate ~3%. Early-onset = vertical transmission
(intrapartum). Late-onset = horizontal (nursery).
Most common organisms: Group B Streptococcus (GBS) and E. coli.
Risk factors: maternal GBS colonization • ROM >18 h • intrapartum fever • chorioamnionitis • prematurity • low birth
weight.
Signs (nonspecific): temperature instability • poor feeding • lethargy • irritability • respiratory distress • “doesn’t look
right.”
Diagnosis: CBC, CRP, blood/CSF/urine cultures. Start antibiotics before cultures result.
TAKE NOTE! Neonatal sepsis often presents with nonspecific signs. If the newborn “doesn’t look right,” investigate.

Newborn resuscitation priorities (ABC)
- Dry, stimulate, warm. If no response → active resuscitation.
- Airway: position, suction (mouth then nose).
- Breathing: positive-pressure ventilation (bag-mask); intubation if needed.
- Circulation: chest compressions if HR <60 after adequate ventilation.
- Goal: HR >100 bpm, spontaneous breathing, pink tongue (good brain oxygenation).
TAKE NOTE! RDS = surfactant deficiency (preterm, IDM). TTN = retained fluid (cesarean, fast labor). MAS = meconium
(term/postterm, distress).
07 6. FETAL DEMISE & PERINATAL LOSS
Perinatal loss: any pregnancy loss and/or neonatal death up to 1 month of age. Prevalence ~5.5 per 1,000 births.
Miscarriage: fetal death before 20 weeks. Stillbirth: fetal death after 20 weeks. Neonatal death: death within first 28
days.
Causes: placental abnormalities • diabetes • hypertension • infection • trauma • congenital anomalies • often
unexplained.
Grief support & nursing care
- Confirm demise: inability to obtain fetal heart sounds; ultrasound confirms absent cardiac activity.
- Induction or expectant management (most go into spontaneous labor in 1-2 weeks).
- Allow parents to view, touch, hold; provide privacy; use the word “died.”
- Memory-making: photographs, hand/footprints, lock of hair, memory box.
- Include the partner; validate grief - each person grieves differently.
- Refer: SHARE Pregnancy & Infant Loss Support; bereavement counselor; community resources.
- Follow-up: send card from nursing staff; attend funeral if appropriate; remember at anniversaries.
TAKE NOTE! Grief has no timeline. “Meeting the family where they are” and listening without judgment are the most important
nursing interventions.

09 ANSWER KEY & RATIONALE
Q ANSWER WHY
1 B Caput crosses suture lines and resolves quickly (edema). Cephalohematoma is subperiosteal (does not cross)
and takes weeks to resolve.
2 B Pathologic = TSB rising >5 mg/dL/day or >0.2 mg/dL/h, or jaundice before 24 hours of age.
3 B IDM hypoglycemia (<40 mg/dL) → priority is early feeding (glucose); if oral feeding fails, IV dextrose.
4 C Cesarean without labor = reduced thoracic compression → delayed fetal lung fluid clearance = TTN. RDS
(surfactant deficiency) is more common in preterm/IDM.
5 B Non-vigorous meconium-stained newborn → tracheal suctioning + PPV under radiant warmer. Do NOT suction
before birth (routine intrapartum suctioning no longer recommended).
6 B Therapeutic response = encourage viewing/holding + memory-making; validate grief; never assign blame. Do
NOT avoid the topic or minimize loss.
